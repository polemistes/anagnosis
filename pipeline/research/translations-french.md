# French versions of Anagnosis texts — source assessment (2026-11-14)

Rules: EEA PD = translator/editor died 1955 or earlier (France: wartime extensions for "morts pour la France" add ~30 years). US PD = published 1930 or earlier (1931+ may be protected in US).
Status: COMPLETE (2026-11-14)

## 1. Homer, Odyssey

Translations
- **Leconte de Lisle** (prose, 1867; ed. 1893). Translator d. 1894. PD EEA + US. fr.wikisource: **validated** clean text (Lemerre 1893), by chant, paragraphs without line numbers. Very literal prose, so line-range alignment per paragraph is feasible but must be done by hand/semiautomatically (no line numbers in the text). https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/L%E2%80%99Odyss%C3%A9e
- **Victor Bérard** (Budé 1924, 3 vols; rhythmic prose "versets"). Bérard d. 1931 -> PD EEA; published 1924 -> PD US. Budé edition has Greek line numbers, but Bérard athetised many lines (relegated/bracketed), so alignment needs care. Wikisource: Budé 1924/1933 = facsimile only (not transcribed); the ACF/Schmied 1930-33 edition is transcribed, but REARRANGED into three "Drames" + "En marge de l'Odyssée" (not by chant) -> poor for alignment. Ca' Foscari has Chant I as PDF. Scan exists; OCR needed for Budé layout.
- **Ulysse de Séguier** (verse, 1896): validated on wikisource, but verse paraphrase, not alignable.
- **Édouard Sommer** juxtalinear (Hachette 1886; Sommer d. 1866): Greek + word-for-word + literal translation; wikisource only 25 %; scans on Gallica (unverified per chant).

Commentary
- **Alexis Pierron** (1814-1878), *L'Odyssée d'Homère, texte grec ... accompagné d'un commentaire critique et explicatif*, Hachette 1875 (2nd ed. 1888), 2 vols. PD. Scans: archive.org 8XSUP113_4_1 and 8XSUP113_4_2 (Ste-Geneviève), lodyssedhomrete00homegoog; HathiTrust 001844927; Google Books (1888 vol 2). Full-line commentary in French, keyed to lines. Scan only -> OCR (Greek + French).
- Pierron also has school editions of single chants (Hachette, 1890s).

Assessment: GOOD (clean validated Leconte de Lisle + full Pierron commentary).

## 2. Plato, Symposium

Translations
- **Léon Robin**, Budé 1929 (CUF Platon t. IV.2). Robin 1866-1947 -> PD EEA; 1929 -> PD US. fr.wikisource: **validated** clean text from the Budé scan, with Robin's Greek text and his long Notice (cxxi pp.) also transcribed. Budé prints Stephanus pages/letters -> sentence-level alignment easy. https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Le_Banquet_(trad._Robin)
- **Émile Chambry** (Garnier 1919, with notices and notes). Chambry 1864-1938 -> PD EEA + US. On wikisource (listed on Le Banquet (Platon) page) and Gallica bpt6k57918430.
- **Victor Cousin** (1831; d. 1867): remacle.org and wikisource; older, freer.

Commentary
- No full French school commentary on the Symposium found (not a French school text). Robin's 121-page Notice (validated on wikisource) is the substantial French introduction; Chambry's footnotes are light. Dalmeyda's *Platon, Extraits* (Hachette 1898, Gallica bpt6k5516413b) annotates only the Alcibiades portrait. Robin's *La théorie platonicienne de l'amour* (1908) is a monograph (wikisource, incomplete).

Assessment: GOOD for translation (clean, Stephanus-numbered, PD both sides); commentary weak (intro only).

## 3. Plato, Gorgias

Translations
- **Alfred Croiset with Louis Bodin**, Budé 1923 (CUF Platon t. III.2). Croiset d. 1923, Bodin 1869-1949 -> PD EEA; 1923 -> PD US. fr.wikisource: **validated**, Stephanus numbers + letters inline; Notice also transcribed. https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Gorgias_(trad._Croiset) ; scan on Commons (tome III, 2.djvu).
- Chambry (Garnier, 1919-ish; PD) — ebooksgratuits PDF; Cousin on remacle.org.

Commentary
- **Édouard Sommer** (1822-1866), *Gorgias, dialogue de Platon : édition classique, publiée avec des arguments et des notes en français*, Hachette (1872 printing). PD. Scans: archive.org gorgiasdialogue00sommgoog; HathiTrust 000398869. Scan only -> OCR.
- Hachette juxtalinear Gorgias with notes (Gallica bpt6k6227344m, has texteBrut OCR).

Assessment: GOOD (validated Croiset + Sommer school commentary).

## 4. New Testament (27 books)

Translations (all verse-numbered -> verse-level alignment is trivial)
- **Louis Segond** (1810-1885), NT 1880, revised ed. 1910 (anonymous revision committee). PD EEA + US. fr.wikisource: complete and formatted ("à relire", not yet validated), one page per book: https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Bible_Segond_1910/%C3%89vangile_selon_Jean . Also eBible.org fraLSG (public domain), chretien.fr; a GitHub project (jesuspirate/plain-bible) has compared LSG and Crampon word by word against the 1910/1923 printings. Caveat: Segond follows a text close to the Textus Receptus in places (verses such as Mt 17:21, 18:11 that SBLGNT omits are present) -> needs a mapping/flag for verses absent in SBLGNT.
- **Augustin Crampon** (1826-1894), revised ed. 1923 (Société de S. Jean l'Évangéliste, Desclée; anonymous Jesuit revisers -> EEA: anonymous work 70 years from publication = PD; US 1923 PD). wikisource complete "à relire": https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Bible_Crampon_1923/Nouveau_Testament . Catholic, from the Greek, with a small NT dictionary appendix. Crampon's 1864 Gospels with notes also on wikisource.
- **J. N. Darby** French NT (1859/1885, from a critical text, closer to SBLGNT); Stapfer 1889, Oltramare 1872 (unverified individually, all PD by date). Darby text widely digitised (chretien.fr etc.; licence of those sites unverified — the wikisource/eBible route is cleaner).

Commentaries (strong area)
- **Frédéric Godet** (1812-1900): Luc (1871; 3rd ed. 1888), Jean (3rd ed. 1881-85), Romains (1879-80), 1 Corinthiens (1886-87). PD. Scans: archive.org commentairesurl01godeuoft, commentairesurl05godegoog, commentairesurl02godegoog (Luc); others on archive.org/Google Books. Clean e-texts exist from ThéoTeX (theotex.org): underlying PD text free, but ThéoTeX claims rights in its added notes/prefaces and sells DRM editions; no open licence -> use the scans or ask ThéoTeX; do not copy their files blindly. Georges Godet, 2 Corinthiens (1914; G. Godet d. ? unverified).
- **M.-J. Lagrange OP** (1855-1938) Études bibliques: Marc 1911 (archive.org evangileselonsai1911lagr; 2nd ed. 1920 full view Google Books; 4th ed. 1929), Romains 1916, Galates 1918, Luc 1921, Matthieu 1923, Jean 1925. PD EEA (d. 1938); all published before 1931 -> PD US (later revised editions may not be). Each volume contains Lagrange's own French translation of the Greek plus verse-by-verse philological commentary — the best scholarly French NT commentary that is free. Scan only -> OCR.
- **Bible annotée** (Neuchâtel), NT by Louis Bonnet (d. 1892) revised Alfred Schroeder (death year unverified), 1892-1905. Digital texts at bibliatodo.com and epelorient.free.fr; licence of those transcriptions unknown; spibook sells PDFs. Scans probably on archive.org (unverified).
- E.-B. Allo (1873-1945), Apocalypse 1921 (PD both), 1 Corinthiens 1934 (PD EEA; US status after URAA uncertain).

Lexicon
- No PD Greek-French NT lexicon found (Carrez-Morel 1985 is protected). Bailly covers NT vocabulary; Crampon 1923's small "Dictionnaire du Nouveau Testament" (wikisource) is a French Bible-names dictionary, not a Greek lexicon.

Assessment: GOOD (verse alignment free; Segond/Crampon clean on wikisource; Godet + Lagrange commentaries are excellent but need OCR; no NT-specific lexicon).

## 5. Sophocles, seven plays (Antigone, Ajax, Electra, OT, OC, Philoctetes, Trachiniae)

General note for drama: speaker turns give natural alignment anchors, so even a prose translation without line numbers can be aligned speech by speech (long choral odes need stanza-level splitting).

Translations
- **Paul Masqueray**, Budé 1922 (t. 1: Ajax, Antigone, OT, Electra — contents of t.1 inferred) and 1924 (t. 2: Trachiniae, Philoctetes, OC, Ichneutae). Masqueray 1862-1931 -> PD EEA; 1922/1924 -> PD US. Modern, accurate prose; Budé prints Greek line numbers. fr.wikisource: t. 2 transcribed (Philoctète, Œdipe à Colone, Trachiniennes with Notices), status not shown as validated; line numbers dropped in the transcription (only in footnotes). t. 1 not on wikisource; scans: HathiTrust 006790504 (vols 1-2 full view, possibly US-only), archive.org sopholem02soph (vol 2). t. 1 would need OCR.
- **Leconte de Lisle** (prose, 1877; d. 1894): all seven plays **validated** on fr.wikisource. Literal but archaising (Greek name spellings "Aias", "Odysseus"…), no line numbers -> align by speaker turns.
- **Nicolas Artaud** (1859, Charpentier; Artaud d. 1861?, unverified; PD by date): all plays, "relu et corrigé" on wikisource, with notices per play. Bellaguet (Hachette 1879, notice by Tournier) also exists (scan).

Commentary (strong)
- **Édouard Tournier** (d. 1899), *Les Tragédies de Sophocle, texte grec ... avec un commentaire critique et explicatif*, Hachette 1867/1877; revised 1886 with **A.-M. Desrousseaux** (1861-1955, death year from memory — unverified; if 1955, PD EEA since 2026). Full French commentary on all seven plays, keyed to lines. Scans: archive.org bub_gb_HiETAAAAQAAJ (OCR text available), HathiTrust (1877, 1886) via onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu.
- Tournier-Desrousseaux single-play school editions with French notes (Hachette, e.g. Antigone 1883 on HathiTrust; Ajax 3rd ed. 1898).
- Masqueray's Notices (introductions) per play (wikisource for t. 2).

Assessment: GOOD (validated Leconte de Lisle for all seven plays, better Masqueray Budé for three plays already transcribed, full Tournier commentary).

## 6. Aeschylus, Oresteia

Translations
- **Paul Mazon**, Budé *Eschyle* t. 2 (Agamemnon, Choéphores, Euménides), 1925. Mazon died **13 Feb 1955** (IdRef, BnF, Budé obituary) -> **PD in the EEA since 1 Jan 2026**; published 1925 -> PD US (later revised editions, e.g. 6th ed. 1955, may differ — use the 1925 printing). Not "mort pour la France". Scans: archive.org eschyletexteta02aesc (1920s Toronto copy, vol 2) and eschyle0002paul (1925); OCR needed. Greek line numbers in the Budé margin -> good alignment.
- **Mazon 1903** (earlier Orestie translation, Fontemoing): on fr.wikisource as "texte complet non-formaté" (raw, not proofread).
- **Leconte de Lisle** (prose 1872): **validated** on fr.wikisource (https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/L%E2%80%99Orestie); no line numbers; remacle.org has bilingual Greek/LdL pages.
- Pierron (1841, revised after Hermann/Dindorf/Weil; d. 1878): remacle.org.

Commentary
- No full PD French commentary on the Oresteia located. Henri Weil's Aeschylus commentary is in LATIN (Giessen 1858-67). Paul Regnaud, *Agamemnon, texte, traduction et commentaires* (c. 1900; Regnaud d. 1910 from memory, unverified) — reviewed REG 1901; scan not located. Mazon's Notices and short notes only.

Assessment: POSSIBLE-to-GOOD for translation (Mazon now PD, needs OCR; LdL clean), POOR for commentary.

(Verified: A.-M. Desrousseaux d. 25 Dec 1955 (IdRef, Maitron) -> Tournier-Desrousseaux editions PD in EEA since 1 Jan 2026.)

## 7. Aristophanes, Clouds

Translations
- **Hilaire Van Daele**, Budé *Aristophane* t. 1 (Acharniens, Cavaliers, Nuées), 1923, Greek text Coulon. Van Daele 1863-1943 (BnF) -> PD EEA; 1923 printing PD US; 2nd ed. 1934 (revised) may be protected in the US. Coulon (Greek text) not needed. Digital: archive.org only has 1934/1964 Budé copies in controlled lending (aristophane0001vict, 1934) — not openly downloadable; free scan of the 1923 printing not located (HathiTrust unverified). Accurate but "dated" prose; Budé margin line numbers.
- **Eugène Talbot** (d. 1894; Lemerre 1897, préface Sully Prudhomme): **validated** on fr.wikisource (https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Les_Nu%C3%A9es); prose, no line numbers -> align by speaker turns. Also PDF at ac-bordeaux.
- Poyard (1860) and Brumoy (1763, raw) also exist; remacle.org has a bilingual page (old translation).

Commentary
- None found: no 19th-c. French school edition of the Clouds located (Aristophanes was hardly a French school author). Modern ones (Riedinger 1995, Milanezi 2009) are protected.

Assessment: POSSIBLE (Talbot clean and PD, Van Daele PD but no clean digital text; no French commentary).

## 8. Hesiod, Theogony

Translations
- **Paul Mazon**, Budé *Hésiode* (Théogonie, Travaux, Bouclier), 1928, with notes. Mazon d. 13 Feb 1955 -> PD EEA since 2026; 1928 -> PD US. Rigorously literal ("d'une exactitude rigoureuse", REA 1930); Budé margin line numbers -> line-level alignment. No free scan located (archive.org: none; an Academia.edu upload of the 1947 reprint exists — not a usable source; HathiTrust unverified). Would need a physical/library scan + OCR. Mazon also published a French-only Théogonie with introduction and notes in 1912 (scan not located).
- **Leconte de Lisle** (prose, c. 1869): fr.wikisource "relu et corrigé" (https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Th%C3%A9ogonie). No line numbers.
- **Henri Patin** (1892 printing; Patin d. 1876 — from memory, unverified): wikisource "relu et corrigé". Anne Bignan 1838 (verse) also there.

Commentary
- None found for the Theogony in French. Mazon's 1914 commentary is on the Works and Days only (*Les Travaux et les Jours, édition nouvelle avec commentaire*). Mazon's Budé footnotes and Notice are the only French apparatus/notes.

Assessment: POSSIBLE (clean LdL/Patin; Mazon PD but needs a scan; no commentary).

## 9. Lucian, A True Story

Translations
- **Eugène Talbot** (1814-1894), *Œuvres complètes de Lucien*, Hachette 1857/1866, t. 1 pp. 380-417. PD EEA + US. fr.wikisource: **"relu et corrigé"**, two pages (livre I, livre II): https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Histoire_v%C3%A9ritable_(Lucien). Remacle.org already shows it side by side with the Greek (Bibliotheca Augustana text), which suggests paragraph-level alignment to the Greek chapters is straightforward (Talbot's paragraphs follow the chapters closely — inferred from remacle's layout, verify). Caveat (unverified): Talbot may soften a few risqué passages (the vine-women, Selenite marriages) — check against Nilén.
- Perrot d'Ablancourt (1654): too free.

Commentary
- None for the Verae Historiae found. Hachette's school series with French notes covered other Lucian works (*Comment il faut écrire l'histoire*, Talbot 1866 and Lehugeur, Gallica bpt6k65301151, bpt6k6227982c; *Dialogues des morts* etc.), but no annotated *Histoire véritable* confirmed (a Calepinus listing "Lucien : Histoire véritable, livres I et II, Hachette" is unclear, unverified). Talbot's own footnotes are sparse.

Assessment: GOOD for translation (short, clean, proofread, PD both sides), no commentary.

## 10. Shepherd of Hermas

Translations
- **Auguste Lelong**, *Les Pères apostoliques IV: Le Pasteur d'Hermas, texte grec, traduction française, introduction et index* (Textes et documents, Hemmer & Lejay, no. 16), Paris, Picard 1912, cxii + 347 pp. US: 1912 -> PD. EEA: Lelong born 1855 (Yvetot); death year NOT found (he was still publishing c. 1933 if he was "André Siouville" — that identification itself is a single-source claim); a death after 1955 would make him over 100, so PD in the EEA is near-certain but formally unverified. Greek and French facing, divided by Vision/Mandate/Similitude with chapter-verse numbers (standard in the series) -> verse-level alignment with Lake should be easy. Scan: Google Books full view https://books.google.com/books?id=0vX8E8iXj3EC (Michigan copy; likely also HathiTrust). Clean e-text: ThéoTeX "Hermas, le Pasteur, grec-français" (2019, BoD/ebook) — same ThéoTeX caveat (no open licence; they claim rights in their additions). Otherwise OCR.
- **Eugène de Genoude** (1792-1849), *Les Pères de l'Église* t. 1, 1837: on fr.wikisource, but translated from the LATIN version (Greek not yet recovered) -> poor fit with Lake's Greek.
- Joly (SC 53, 1958) and Henne (2011) are protected.

Commentary
- Lelong's 112-page introduction and short notes; no separate PD French commentary. Studies exist (Réville 1900 archive.org lavaleurdutmoig00rvgoog; Ribagnac 1887; DTC article "Hermas" on wikisource), but not line commentaries.

Assessment: POSSIBLE (Lelong is the right edition, alignable, PD in US, almost certainly PD in EEA; needs OCR; Lelong's death year should be pinned down before release).

## 11. Greek-French dictionaries (word sheet)

- **Bailly 2020 "Hugo Chávez"** (Gérard Gréco, Charbonnet, De Wilde, Maréchal et al.; based on Bailly 1935): **CC BY-NC-ND 4.0** (bailly.app, BBAW, chaerephon site: "pas d'utilisation commerciale") -> UNUSABLE for paid apps (and ND forbids adaptation).
- **Anatole Bailly, Dictionnaire grec-français** (1895; Bailly d. 1911, Egger d. 1885): PD. Avoid the 1950 revision (Séchan, Chantraine — protected). Scans: archive.org BaillyDictionnaireGrecFrancais (1935 printing, PD Mark; pre-1950 text), Commons djvu. No clean open digital text: the only good one is Gréco's (NC). Would need own OCR of 2,200 dense pages — big job.
- **Bailly, Abrégé du dictionnaire grec-français** (1901; 6th ed. 1919): PD. archive.org bailly-abr (1919, PD Mark, ABBYY OCR) and AbrgDuDictionnaireGrecFranais; fr.wikisource transcription by letter (Livre:Bailly - Dictionnaire Grec-Français.djvu; completeness/proofreading level unverified); remacle.org text (no licence stated); Chæréphon/Gréco StarDict "Bailly Abrégé v8.7 (2025)" with additions from the Grand Bailly and Chantraine — no licence stated (and Chantraine material is protected) -> do not use; build from wikisource/own OCR instead. The Abrégé (c. 56,000 headwords) is the realistic French word-sheet dictionary.
- **Charles Alexandre** (1797-1870): Dictionnaire grec-français (full ed. with Pillon's proper-names vocabulary, 1873-74, Gallica bpt6k6509479z — Gallica commercial reuse needs a paid BnF licence); Abrégé 1861 on archive.org. Scan only.
- **Alexis Chassang** (1827-1888), Nouveau dictionnaire grec-français (1872): full scan not confirmed on archive.org (dictionnairegrec00chas is a 2-page item). Pessonneaux, Dictionnaire grec-français (1899?, archive.org dictionnaire-grec-francais, PD Mark; Pessonneaux's death year unverified).
- **NT**: no PD Greek-French NT lexicon found (Carrez-Morel 1985 protected). Bailly Abrégé covers NT vocabulary adequately; Koine-specific senses would be missing.

## 12. Sites and their terms

- **fr.wikisource**: PD texts; fine. Main source for clean translations.
- **Gallica (BnF)**: non-commercial reuse free; **commercial reuse is paid and licensed** (conditions d'utilisation; the BnF concedes scans do not create new copyright, but its terms still apply to its files). Prefer archive.org/Google/HathiTrust/wikisource copies of the same books.
- **remacle.org**: no stated terms; mostly 19th-c. PD translations, "sometimes revised" and a few modern ones -> check each page's source; use only to locate, take text from wikisource/scans.
- **Hodoi Elektronikai (UCL)**: no formal terms found; translations largely taken from remacle; Greek texts unsourced (TLG dispute 2011) -> avoid.
- **ThéoTeX**: clean e-texts of Godet, Lelong's Hermas etc.; underlying texts PD, but ThéoTeX claims rights in its additions and sells DRM ebooks; no open licence -> ask permission or work from scans.
- **archive.org/Google Books/HathiTrust**: scans of PD books; HathiTrust full view of 1920s French books may be US-only.
