# German, French and Italian versions of the texts: overview

Assessed 2026-11-14. Question: is it plausible to make German, French and Italian versions of the
19 texts built so far, given a translation and ideally a commentary in each language?

Answer: yes, in all three. Translations are easy to find. Commentaries decide how full a version
can be: German is rich in them, French has some, Italian has almost none. Every commentary exists
only as a scan, so each needs text recognition (OCR) and proofreading. Nothing is decided.

Details, with scan identifiers, death years and licence checks:

- [translations-german.md](translations-german.md)
- [translations-french.md](translations-french.md)
- [translations-italian.md](translations-italian.md) (section 11b corrects section 11 on dictionaries)

## Rule

No AI translation of dictionaries or texts (the author, 2026-11-14). In a version in another
language, every translation, dictionary entry and short definition comes from a printed source in
that language. Where no such source exists, the English stays.

## Per text

"Clean" = a proofread digital text already exists under a usable licence. "OCR" = scan only.

| Symposium | good: Apelt or Schleiermacher (OCR); Hug commentary | good: Robin's Budé, clean, Stephanus numbers; no commentary | possible: Martini or Bonghi (OCR) |
| Gorgias | good: Apelt or Schleiermacher (OCR); Deuschle-Cron commentary | good: Croiset-Bodin's Budé, clean, Stephanus numbers; Sommer commentary (OCR) | possible: Martini or Bonghi (OCR); no commentary |

Suggested first editions:

- German: Clouds, True Story, Oresteia, New Testament.
- French: Gorgias, Symposium, one Sophocles play, New Testament (Gospels first).
- Italian: New Testament, Theogony, Odyssey, then Antigone or True Story.
- As a single pilot: French Gorgias (clean translation plus Sommer's commentary) or German Clouds
  (clean translation plus Kock's commentary).

## Dictionaries for the word sheet

None exists as clean text under a usable licence; each language needs one dictionary put through
OCR and proofreading once, to serve all its texts. Short definitions are then taken from the first
sense of that printed dictionary.

- German: Gemoll 1908 (one volume); Preuschen 1910 for the New Testament and Hermas; Autenrieth's
  German original for Homer. Pape exists digitally only at Zeno.org (non-commercial terms).
- French: Bailly abrégé (1901; 6th ed. 1919). The full Bailly of 1895 is scan only; the 1950
  revision is protected; "Bailly 2020" is CC BY-NC-ND and cannot be used.
- Italian: Schenkl-Ambrosoli (972 pp., clean layout, covers 15 of the 19 texts: not Clouds,
  Theogony, True Story, Hermas) or Müller 1871 (about 1,250 pp., all of Greek, nested entries);
  Nazari 1922 for Homer. Bonazzi and the Italian Gemoll (1922, 1926) are public domain but have no
  scan online. Rocci is protected in the US until 2035/2039.

## Sources that cannot be used in a sold app

- Zeno.org and Projekt Gutenberg-DE (private, non-commercial use only).
- Liber Liber e-texts (CC BY-NC-SA).
- Bailly 2020 by Gérard Gréco (CC BY-NC-ND).
- Gallica scans (commercial reuse is licensed and charged): take the same books from archive.org
  or Google Books.
- ThéoTeX, remacle.org, Hodoi Elektronikai, opera-platonis.de, Olivetti: rights claimed or no
  terms stated. Use them only to find texts.

Usable: Wikisource (de, fr, it), TextGrid (CC BY 3.0), Deutsches Textarchiv (CC BY-SA 4.0,
share-alike), BKV at bkv.unifr.ch, eBible.org and CrossWire Bible texts, archive.org scans of
public-domain books.

## Open questions

- Death years to confirm before use: Zeller (German Hermas), Lelong (French Hermas).
- Whether scans of Bonazzi or the Italian Gemoll exist on Google Books or HathiTrust (both
  blocked automated lookups on 2026-11-14; a manual search would settle it).
- Google's cover sheet on its scans asks that the scan files not be used commercially. The texts
  are public domain and the app would carry our own transcription; the author's call.
- The app side: the reader's own buttons and menus, the introductions and the glossaries are in
  English and would need a version in each language, from printed sources or written by hand.
- Italian commentaries: full-view scans of Zuretti (Odyssey I), Cesareo (Antigone, Oedipus
  Tyrannus) and Bassi (Antigone) not confirmed.
