# Italian versions of Anagnosis texts: source assessment (2026-11-14)

Rules: EEA PD = translator/editor died 1955 or earlier; US PD = published 1930 or earlier.

## 1. Homer, Odyssey

### Translations
1. **Ettore Romagnoli, Odissea (Bologna: Zanichelli, 1926), 2 vols.** Romagnoli d. 1938 -> EEA PD; published 1926 -> US PD. Verse, but in long "barbaric hexameter" lines that run almost line for line with the Greek (Book IX ends at his line 560 vs 566 in the Greek; line numbers printed every 5 lines). Line-by-line alignment is realistic with small local drift. **Clean text on it.wikisource, SAL 100% (proofread against the scan)**: https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Odissea_(Romagnoli)/Canto_I ... Canto_XXIV; index https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Odissea . Scans on archive.org: omero-odissea-vol-uno, omero-odissea-vol-due.
2. **Ippolito Pindemonte, Odissea (Verona 1822)**, d. 1828; PD everywhere. Hendecasyllables (endecasillabi sciolti), expands the Greek: NOT line-aligned; could only be aligned by passages/speeches. Clean text on it.wikisource (100%): https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Odissea_(Pindemonte)/Libro_I etc. The classic Italian Odyssey that every Italian reader knows, so worth offering as a second translation even if alignment is coarse.
3. Nicola Festa, Odissea tradotta e annotata (Sandron 1937; archive.org omero-odissea-nicola-festa). Festa d. 1940 -> EEA PD; but 1937 -> US protected until 2033 (URAA restoration). Not usable worldwide.

### Commentaries (Greek text with Italian notes)
- **Carlo Oreste Zuretti (1865-1931)**, L'Odissea commentata, Loescher "Collezione di classici greci e latini con note italiane", Libro I 1897 (and further books; reviewed CR 11, 1897). PD EEA + US. Scan: not found on archive.org (only his Iliad, archive.org/details/liliade00home). Google Books / HathiTrust likely but unverified (HathiTrust and Google Books API were blocked for me).
- **Augusto Corradi**, Omero, La Odissea commentata (Loescher; vol. II = books V-VIII, by 1905). Corradi still published in 1945 (Xenophon, Chiantore) -> death year unverified, probably after 1955 -> treat as NOT PD in EEA until checked.
- Salvatore Rossi, Odissea IX (Giusti 1920, school booklet) - death year unverified.
- Vittorio Turri: commentary on Pindemonte's Italian translation (Sansoni 1924) - notes on the Italian, not the Greek; death year unverified.
Verdict: a usable Italian commentary exists in principle (Zuretti) but a full 24-book scan is not confirmed; this is the weak point.

## 2. Plato, Symposium

### Translations (all prose; all PD in the EEA and the US)
1. **Emidio Martini (1852-1940), Il convito (Simposio), Torino: Paravia 1922.** EEA PD (d. 1940), US PD (1922). Prose, made from **Burnet's Oxford text (1910)** - the same edition Anagnosis uses - with Stephanus pages in the margin ("p. 172") and chapter numbers; short introductions per section and footnotes on the text. Scan + rough OCR: https://archive.org/details/PlatoneConvitoMartini . No clean text found -> OCR/proofreading needed (177 KB OCR, modest size). Martini says he leaned on Bonghi's version.
2. **Ruggero Bonghi (1826-1895), Convito, Torino-Roma-Firenze: Bocca 1888** (Dialoghi di Platone series). PD everywhere. Prose; Stephanus references used throughout (notes cite "172 C", "173 D"). Long proemio + extensive numbered notes (the volume's OCR is 640 KB, much of it Bonghi's essay and notes). Scan: https://archive.org/details/PlatoneConvito . OCR poor (needs re-OCR). Bonghi's notes are the nearest thing to an Italian commentary on the Symposium.
3. Francesco Acri (1834-1913), Il Convito (1885 etc.), PD. Clean e-text on Liber Liber (https://liberliber.it/autori/autori-p/plato-platone/il-convito/) but **licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 -> unusable** (non-commercial); would need own transcription from a scan. Acri's style is literary/archaising (famous but free).
Not on it.wikisource (Autore:Platone lists only Eutifrone, Apologia, Critone, Timeo by Acri, Sofista/Politico by Fraccaroli, Fedro by Dalbono).

### Commentaries
- No PD school edition of the Greek Symposium with Italian notes found (searches gave nothing; unverified that none exists). Bonghi 1888 and Martini 1922 both carry substantial explanatory notes on the translation, which could serve as Italian notes keyed to Stephanus sections.

## 3. Plato, Gorgias

### Translations (prose)
1. **Emidio Martini, Gorgia, Torino: Paravia 1929.** EEA PD (d. 1940); US PD (published 1929, PD since 2025). Same Paravia series as his Convito (so probably Burnet-based with Stephanus margins - inferred from the series, not checked page by page). Scan: https://archive.org/details/PlatoneGorgiaMartini . OCR needed.
2. **Ruggero Bonghi, Gorgia o della rettorica, in Dialoghi di Platone vol. XI (Bocca 1903, posthumous volume).** PD everywhere (d. 1895; publ. 1903). Prose, chapter numbers + Stephanus pages in the margin (e.g. "I. Callicle ... 447"), so section alignment is direct. Scan: https://archive.org/details/LacheteGorgiaMenonePlatoneBonghi . OCR fair; re-OCR advised.
3. Giuseppe Fraccaroli (1849-1918), Il Gorgia, ed. Paolo Ubaldi (1872-1934), La Nuova Italia 1947 (archive.org/details/PlatoneGorgiaFraccaroli). EEA PD; US: 1947 printing protected unless an earlier (pre-1931) edition exists (unverified). Fraccaroli is the best-regarded of the three, but US status blocks it.
Older: Il Gorgia ovvero della rettorica (1832; Google scan bub_gb_GQksUr1Qik8C), translator not checked.

### Commentaries
- None found for the Greek Gorgias with Italian notes (unverified).

## 4. New Testament (27 books)

### Translations (prose, verse-numbered -> exact verse alignment)
1. **Versione Riveduta (Giovanni Luzzi and committee), NT 1924, whole Bible 1924/1925, common print 1927.** Luzzi d. 25 Jan 1948 -> EEA PD since 2019 (Spain 80-year term runs to 2029 - irrelevant for Norway); US PD (1924). Translated from the Greek using post-Diodati critical editions (which edition exactly: unverified), so it fits SBLGNT better than Diodati's Textus Receptus. **Clean digital text**: CrossWire SWORD module ItaRive (licence "Public Domain"), eBible.org Riveduta (declared PD, credits the Bible Society in Italy, which in 2011 stated the historical text is PD while its typeset layouts are protected), STEP Bible. Caveat: seven1m/open-bibles issue #77 (Nov 2026) reports transcription damage ("damaged words") in one OSIS file -> spot-check against a scan.
2. **Giovanni Diodati (1641/1649; d. 1649)**, PD everywhere; clean text widely available (CrossWire "ItaDio", eBible). Textus Receptus base, 17th-c. language; verse alignment works but it diverges from SBLGNT in the TR places.
3. Antonio Martini (d. 1809) - translated from the Latin Vulgate, not the Greek: not suitable as a parallel to SBLGNT (PD, clean texts exist).

### Commentary - a real find
- **Giovanni Luzzi, La Bibbia tradotta dai testi originali e annotata, Nuovo Testamento: vol. XI I Vangeli e gli Atti (Firenze: Fides et Amor, printed 9-1929) and vol. XII Le Epistole e l'Apocalisse (printed 9-1930).** Luzzi's own fresh translation from the Greek + introductions to every book (5 + 22) + running notes. EEA PD (d. 1948); US PD (1929 and 1930 publication; "proprietà riservata" is irrelevant once the term has run). Scans + OCR (University of Connecticut copy, flagged NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT): https://archive.org/details/labibbialanticoe11luzz and https://archive.org/details/labibbialanticoe12luzz (about 1.1 MB OCR each; OCR quality fair, proofreading needed; plates and maps). This gives an Italian commentary for all 27 books, keyed by chapter and verse, and also an alternative, more modern translation.

## 5. Sophocles, all seven plays

### Translations
1. **Ettore Romagnoli, Tragedie di Sofocle (Bologna: Zanichelli, 1926), 3 vols** ("I poeti greci tradotti da Ettore Romagnoli"). EEA PD (d. 1938); US PD (1926). Verse: hendecasyllables for the dialogue, varied lyric metres for choruses; his OWN line numbering (every 5 lines), running ~10% longer than the Greek (OT: Greek 132 = his 145). Speaker turns follow the Greek one for one, so **speech-by-speech alignment** is straightforward; strict line alignment is not. **Clean text on it.wikisource, all seven plays (plus the Ichneutae), SAL 100%**, e.g. https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Edipo_re_(Sofocle_-_Romagnoli), Antigone_(Sofocle_-_Romagnoli), Edipo_a_Colono_(Sofocle_-_Romagnoli), Aiace, Elettra, Le Trachinie, Filottete (Autore:Sofocle). Scans: Indice:Tragedie di Sofocle (Romagnoli) I-III.djvu; archive.org sophocles03itws (vol. III).
2. **Felice Bellotti (1786-1858), Tragedie di Sofocle (1813; rev. 1821, 1826, 1829-30)**. PD everywhere. Endecasillabi sciolti, freer and longer than Romagnoli; speech-level alignment only. it.wikisource has only Edipo re (Bellotti, 100%); the rest as Google scans on archive.org (bub_gb_ApyKhKRuQkEC 1813, bub_gb_x3ImofXmOCMC 1826, bub_gb_8H37UEz9CoUC / bub_gb_Pz6WBvjexDkC 1829-30) -> OCR needed (19th-c. typography, long s not an issue by then).
3. Others: G.B. Giusti, Edipo Coloneo (1819, it.wikisource 100%); Ettore Bignone (d. 1953 -> EEA PD) Sansoni 1936-39 - US protected (post-1930), unusable worldwide.
No PD Italian PROSE translation of Sophocles found.

### Commentaries (Greek text with Italian notes)
- **Placido Cesareo (1862-1908, killed in the Messina earthquake)**: Antigone, con note (Torino: Loescher, Collezione di classici greci e latini con note italiane, 1901; reviewed CR); Edipo re, con note (Roma: Albrighi, Segati & C., 1907; HathiTrust record 006100859 - full-view status unverified, HathiTrust blocked my fetch). PD EEA + US.
- **Domenico Bassi (1859-1942/43/44, sources differ; PD either way)**: Antigone, con note per le scuole (Milano: Briola 1893; 2nd ed. Napoli). Scan unverified.
- **Angelo Nucciotti**: Elettra, con introduzione e note (Livorno: R. Giusti 1920, "Biblioteca di classici greci commentati"); Google scan https://archive.org/details/elettraconintro00sophgoog . US PD (1920); Nucciotti's death year NOT found -> EEA status unverified.
- Ajax, Philoctetes, Trachiniae, OC: no PD Italian commentary found (unverified that none exists; the OPAC SBN catalogue would settle it).

## 6. Aeschylus, Oresteia (Agamemnon, Choephoroi, Eumenides)

### Translations
1. **Ettore Romagnoli, Tragedie di Eschilo (Bologna: Zanichelli, 1921-22)**. EEA PD; US PD. Verse, own line numbering (Ag.: Greek 974 = his 991, drift ~2%), speech-for-speech with the Greek -> speech-level alignment easy, near line-level in dialogue. **Clean text on it.wikisource, SAL 100%**: https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Agamennone_(Eschilo), Le Coefore, Le Eumenidi (Autore:Eschilo). Scans: Indice:Tragedie di Eschilo (Romagnoli) II.djvu; also the 1921 De Carolis edition, archive.org letragedieconin00aescgoog.
2. **Felice Bellotti, Tragedie di Eschilo (1821; Milano 1828-30)**, PD. Verse. Google scans on archive.org (bub_gb_UIU4b03ngfkC 1828; bub_gb_kHGMilKvekgC, bub_gb__ntVdBz6Pf4C, bub_gb__8mVGoCEth8C 1830); OCR needed.
3. Fausto Salvatori, Le Eumenidi (1905, archive.org leeumeniditraged00salv) - stage adaptation, Salvatori d. 1929; not a translation to align.

### Commentaries
- None found for the Oresteia (searches on Loescher/Sansoni + Agamennone/Coefore/Eumenidi gave nothing). Unverified that none exists.

## 7. Aristophanes, Clouds

### Translations
1. **Ettore Romagnoli, Le Nuvole, in Commedie di Aristofane vol. II (Bologna: Zanichelli, 1924; Romagnoli's first Aristophanes edition was c. 1909-11, unverified)**. EEA PD; US PD. Verse, lively and free (Strepsiades renamed "Lesina", i.e. Italianised speaking names), NO line numbers printed; speech-by-speech alignment possible but needs manual work. it.wikisource: https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Le_Nuvole_(Aristofane-Romagnoli) - transcribed but only **SAL 25% (not proofread)**; scan Indice:Commedie di Aristofane (Romagnoli) II.djvu. Proofreading needed. Has an introduction.
2. Older verse versions (e.g. Domenico Capellina, Le commedie di Aristofane, 1850s; unverified) - not checked.

### Commentaries
- None found (unverified). Zuretti (1865-1931) wrote Analecta Aristophanea (1892) but no Italian school commentary on the Clouds was found.

## 8. Hesiod, Theogony

### Translations
1. **Ettore Romagnoli, I poemi di Esiodo (Bologna: Zanichelli, 1929)**. EEA PD; US PD (published 1929). Verse in long lines that keep the **Greek line numbering** (the Iapetids section runs 510-615, i.e. the Greek line numbers, marked every 5 lines) -> **line-by-line alignment**, the best case of all. it.wikisource SAL 100%: https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Teogonia_(Romagnoli) (scan Indice:Poemi (Esiodo).djvu).
2. Lorenzo Pozzuolo, Teogonia (in I poemi di Esiodo, 1873), it.wikisource 100% (https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Teogonia_(Pozzuolo)); hendecasyllables, no line numbers; translator's death year not found (published 1873, almost certainly PD but unverified).

### Commentaries
- None found for the Theogony in Italian (unverified).

## 9. Lucian, A True Story

### Translations
1. **Luigi Settembrini (1813-1876), Di una storia vera, in Opere di Luciano voltate in italiano, Tomo 2 (Firenze: Le Monnier, 1862)**. PD everywhere. Prose, close and much admired (a classic of Italian prose); paragraphs not numbered, so the Greek sections (1.1-1.42, 2.1-2.47) have to be matched by hand - easy for prose of this length. **Clean text on it.wikisource, SAL 100%**: https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Di_una_storia_vera (Libro primo, Libro secondo). One note (from a Greek scholium).
2. No other PD Italian version checked (Romagnoli did not translate Lucian as far as I could find).

### Commentaries
- No PD Italian school commentary on the Verae Historiae found (unverified). Settembrini's 'Discorso intorno la vita e le opere di Luciano' (it.wikisource) could serve as an introduction.

## 10. Shepherd of Hermas

### Translations
- Key fact: the Greek Hermas was only recovered from 1855-59 (Athos manuscript, Codex Sinaiticus). Any Italian version before c. 1860 is made from the Latin versions, not the Greek Lake prints -> unsuitable as a parallel.
- G.B. Gallicciolli (1733-1806), Il Pastore di S. Erma volgarizzato (Venezia: Palese 1796): PD, but from the Latin. Scan not located (Gallicciolli's Barnabas 1797, Justin 1799, Tatian 1800 are on archive.org as Google scans).
- Pietro Baldoncini, Il pastore di Erma (Tipografia Poliglotta Vaticana 1920): US PD (1920); translator's death year not found -> EEA unverified. Scan not found.
- **Maria Monachesi, Il "Pastore di Erma", traduzione, introduzione e note (Roma: Libreria di cultura 1923, Scrittori cristiani antichi 5)**: scan + OCR on archive.org https://archive.org/details/monachesi-pastore-di-erma (Google-digitised copy; numbered sections, notes). BUT it.wikipedia calls it a **partial** translation; US PD (1923); Monachesi (a pupil of Buonaiuti, so probably born c. 1900) - death year not found, likely after 1955 -> EEA probably NOT PD.
- Rovigo Marzini, Il Pastore (Siena: Cantagalli 1928): death year not found; scan not found.
- Guido Bosio (SEI 1955) and later: protected.
Verdict: no clearly usable Italian Hermas.

### Commentaries
- None PD found (Monachesi's notes share her status).

## 11. Greek-Italian dictionaries for the word sheet

- **Lorenzo Rocci SJ (1864-1950), Vocabolario greco-italiano (Roma: Dante Alighieri, 1939; 2nd 1941; 3rd 1943 = Rocci's last revision per the publisher; 4th 1949).** EEA: Rocci's own text is PD since 1 Jan 2021 (life + 70) - use the 1939-1943/49 editions only; later printings (esp. the 2011 revision with Mazzotti, Argan et al.) add protected material. **US: NOT PD** - Italian work still protected in Italy on 1 Jan 1996, so URAA-restored: 1939 ed. protected through 2034, 1943 ed. through 2038. No legitimate scan or clean digital text found (the only digital version is the publisher's paid "Il Rocci" app, 2024; the PDFs floating around are pirated modern printings). Would need own scan + OCR of a 2,074-page dictionary. Not usable worldwide for years.
- **Schenkl-Ambrosoli, Vocabolario greco-italiano per uso dei ginnasj** (from Karl Schenkl's Greek-German school dictionary, Schenkl 1827-1900; translated by Francesco Ambrosoli, d. 1868). PD everywhere. Scans: https://archive.org/details/schenkl-vocabolario-greco-italiano-per-uso-dei-ginnasj-ed.-1892-parti-1-2-3 (1892, PD mark), Google Books 1879 6th ed. (id QaMi0gEACAAJ), Austrian National Library (Kulturpool). Scan only; 972 pp. school-level; OCR of a two-script dictionary is heavy work.
- **Giuseppe (Joseph) Müller (1825-1895)**: Dizionario manuale della lingua greca (Loescher 1871; Vocabolario greco-italiano 4th ed. 1884); later Müller & F. Brunetti, Dizionario manuale della lingua greca, 2 vols (1889-97). Müller PD; Brunetti's death year not found. No scan located. (I found no "Müller-Bonghi" dictionary; Bonghi does not seem to be involved.)
- Giuseppe Rigutini (1829-1903), Vocabolario greco-italiano e italiano-greco (Barbèra 1892, 13th ed. 1925): PD; scans https://archive.org/details/rigutini-vocabolario-greco-italiano-e-italiano-greco-1892 and ...-1925. Small school dictionary.
- Michele Sartorio, Nuovo vocabolario greco-italiano (1846/1852), archive.org nuovovocabolari00sartgoog: PD, old.
- Olivetti, Dizionario Greco Antico (grecoantico.com): "Tutti i diritti riservati" -> unusable (an archive.org StarDict "ripoff" of it is likewise unusable).
- Liber Liber in general: its e-texts carry CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (checked on the Acri Convito page) -> unusable for the paid apps; it.wikisource transcriptions are fine.
Verdict: there is NO clean, licence-clear Greek-Italian dictionary. An Italian edition would keep the English dictionaries on the word sheet (with Italian translation/notes in the reader), unless the project invests in OCR of Schenkl-Ambrosoli (EEA+US PD) or waits for Rocci's US term (2035/2039).

## Overall verdict

General picture: **Ettore Romagnoli (d. 1938) is the backbone.** He translated Homer, Hesiod, Aeschylus, Sophocles and Aristophanes in 1921-1929, which is PD in both the EEA and the US, and it.wikisource has most of it fully proofread (SAL 100%). The weak points are (a) Italian commentaries: there are school editions with Italian notes for single plays/books, but scans were hard to confirm (HathiTrust and Google Books could not be queried from here), and (b) dictionaries: no clean, licence-clear Greek-Italian dictionary exists (Rocci is EEA PD but US-protected until 2035/2039; Schenkl-Ambrosoli is scan only). Plan on Italian translation + Italian notes, with the English dictionaries kept on the word sheet.

| Text | Rating | Why |
|---|---|---|
| New Testament | **good** | Riveduta 1924 (Luzzi d. 1948): PD, clean digital text, verse-aligned; Luzzi's own annotated NT (1929-30) gives introductions + notes for all 27 books (scan + OCR on archive.org). Only text with a full Italian commentary. |
| Hesiod, Theogony | **good** | Romagnoli 1929 keeps the Greek line numbers -> true line-by-line; clean on it.wikisource (100%). No commentary. |
| Homer, Odyssey | **good** | Romagnoli 1926, near line-for-line, clean (100%); Pindemonte 1822 as the classic second version. Commentary only Zuretti book I (scan unconfirmed). |
| Lucian, True Story | **good** | Settembrini 1862, classic prose, clean (100%); sections to be matched by hand (easy). No commentary. |
| Sophocles, Antigone | **good** | Romagnoli 1926 clean (100%), speech-level alignment; Cesareo 1901 (d. 1908) and Bassi 1893 commentaries exist (scans not confirmed). |
| Sophocles, Oedipus Tyrannus | **good** | Romagnoli clean + Bellotti clean on it.wikisource; Cesareo 1907 commentary (HathiTrust record; full view unconfirmed). |
| Sophocles, Ajax, Electra, OC, Philoctetes, Trachiniae | **possible** (good for translation alone) | Romagnoli clean (100%), speech-level alignment; no confirmed PD commentary except Nucciotti's Electra (1920, scan on archive.org; his death year unknown). |
| Aeschylus, Agamemnon / Choephoroi / Eumenides | **possible** (good for translation alone) | Romagnoli 1921-22 clean (100%), close to the Greek line count; no Italian commentary found. |
| Plato, Symposium | **possible** | Martini 1922 (from Burnet, Stephanus margins) or Bonghi 1888 (with long notes); both scan + OCR only; Acri's clean text is NC-licensed. |
| Plato, Gorgias | **possible** | Martini 1929 or Bonghi 1903, Stephanus margins, scan + OCR only; no commentary; Fraccaroli's better version is US-blocked (1947). |
| Aristophanes, Clouds | **possible** (weak) | Romagnoli 1924 only 25% proofread, free version with Italianised names, no line numbers; no commentary. |
| Shepherd of Hermas | **poor** | Pre-1860 versions come from the Latin; 1920s versions have translators of unknown death year, Monachesi 1923 is partial; nothing clearly PD in the EEA. |

**Best first Italian editions**
1. **New Testament** - Riveduta drops in verse by verse at almost no cost; Luzzi's notes can follow book by book (OCR + proofreading) and make it the one fully Italian edition.
2. **Theogony** - Romagnoli's line-numbered, proofread text: line-by-line alignment with almost no manual work; small text.
3. **Odyssey** - Romagnoli, proofread and almost line-for-line; the text Italian readers most want, with Pindemonte as an optional second translation.
4. **Antigone** (or **A True Story**) - Antigone: Romagnoli clean + the chance of Cesareo's Loescher notes if a scan turns up. True Story: Settembrini clean, short, a classic of Italian prose, zero licence risk.

Unverified points worth a follow-up in OPAC SBN / HathiTrust / Google Books: full-view scans of Zuretti's Odissea, Cesareo's Antigone and Edipo re, Bassi's Antigone; death years of Nucciotti, Corradi, Baldoncini, Monachesi, Marzini, Pozzuolo; which Greek edition the Riveduta NT followed; the transcription damage reported in one Riveduta OSIS file.
