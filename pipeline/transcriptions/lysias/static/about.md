The thirty-four speeches of Lysias in the Greek text of Karl Hude's Oxford edition (1912), with his critical apparatus, English commentaries on nineteen of the speeches (Adams, Morgan, Shuckburgh) with their introductions, English translations of twenty-six, and the Liddell–Scott dictionaries. Tap any word for its meaning and form.

Each speech is a book of the text; a passage is cited by speech and section (12.7). The text and apparatus were read from the scan of Hude's edition, matched word by word to the Perseus Digital Library's text of the speeches to find reading errors, and checked against the page wherever the two differed; where Hude evidently misprints a word it is corrected, and a note in the apparatus says so. Four pages of the second speech (2.23–2.30 and 2.59–2.66) are missing from the Internet Archive's online view of the scan; they were read from the scan's own image files.

Speeches 12, 13 and 19 have no syntax trees: the only trees made for them are under a licence that does not allow use here. Their word analyses (dictionary form and grammatical form) are automatic and were checked word by word in their sentences. The English is the anonymous literal translation of 1898 for twenty speeches (2, 5, 7, 9, 10, 12, 13, 14, 16, 17, 19, 22, 23, 24, 25, 28, 30, 31, 32, 33) and John Gillies's free version of 1778, laid beside the Greek by his sentences and paragraphs, for six (1, 3, 4, 21, 26, 34). Speeches 6, 8, 11, 15, 18, 20, 27 and 29 have no English translation in the public domain.
