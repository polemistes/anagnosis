## Mandate 2.4
**thou shouldest give or not give**: Note the parallel on almsgiving in chap. i. of the *Didache*, “*To every one that asketh of thee give and ask not back*, for the Father would have gifts given to all out of His own favours. Blessed is he that gives according to the commandment, for he is guiltless. Woe to him that receives. For if any one receives having need he shall be guiltless; but he that has no need shall give account wherefore he received and for what, and being in durance he shall be examined touching the things that he did, and *he shall not come forth thence till he has paid the uttermost farthing*. But further concerning this it has been said, *Let thine alms sweat into thine hands till thou know to whom to give.*” The mutual relations of the different forms of this remarkable precept have been much discussed. Its purport is to justify giving “to every one that asketh” by throwing the responsibility upon the receiver; but Hermas inaptly says in effect, Give to all *that need*, because we are commanded to give to all *whether they need or not*. The idea of receiving *in hypocrisy* reappears in *Sim.* ix. 19. 3 and in a fragment of Clem. Alex. Note that Hermas uses the saying of the *Didache* (iv. 7) and the *Epistle of Barnabas* (xix. 11), “Thou shalt not hesitate to give,” as if it forbade merely distinction of persons in giving. First he writes “not doubting” or *hesitating*, and then “making no distinction.”
