## 304
**ὕβριν** is plainly that which he inflicts, not that which he punishes; though the latter would be suggested by the usual sense of ἐκτίνεσθαι, as in Eur. H. F. 547 μή wore Κρέοντος θάνατον ἐκτεισαίατο (‘avenge’). Here the verb means, “inflict in vengeance.’—ékrelaavro (for the spelling, see on 181), not ἐκτείσοιτο : the latter would be tenable only if αὐτοῦ could replace αὐτῶν. When he spoke with Athena, he fancied that he had already slain the Atreidae (100); and Odysseus, though reserved for further chastisement, was already δεσμώτης (το5).

**ἰών** (=dre ἤε) adds animation; see on Ant. 768 δράτω, φρονείτω μεῖζον ἢ κατ᾽ ἄνδρ᾽ ἰών.

## 308
