(2) After τῶν ἁπάντων.. θεαμάτων ἄλγιστον, there is a rhetorical advantage in having ὁδός θ᾽ ὁδῶν πασῶν rather than ὁδῶν θ᾽ ἁπασῶν ὁδός. Τί 15 ἃ ‘chiasmus.’ (3) The absence of caesura in ὁδός θ᾽ ὁδῶν πασῶν may have been intentional : see on 855.

**ἀνιάσασα δή**. Here δή emphasises the whole phrase πασῶν ἀνιάσασα μάλιστα (rather than πασῶν only). Cp. Creon’s words in Ant. 1212 apa δυστυχεστάτην | κέλευθον ἕρπω τῶν παρελθουσῶν ὁδών ;

**ἣν δὴ**: δὴ emphasises the pron., as in 1020, 1043, 1045, and often.

## 996
**ὡς ἐπῃσθόμην**. On learning that Ajax had left the tent, the Messenger and others had hastened to the camp, to inform Teucer (804). They knew nothing as to the hero’s fate. Teucer, who had stayed near the council of chiefs in the hope of mediating (780), then set out to seek Ajax,—going, not, of course, to the tent itself, but to the coast near it. While engaged in the search, he heard the rumour that Ajax was dead.

**διώκων** expresses eager, rapid search ; as the midd. διώκομαι denotes speed (ZZ. 871).

**κἀξιχνοσκοπούμενος**. The act. ἐξιχνοσκοπεῖν occurs in 77. 271: ἰχνοσκοπεῖν in Aesch. Ck. 228.

## 1000
**δύστηνος**, L’s reading, is better attested than δείλαιος : otherwise there is little to choose between them.

**ἐκποδὼν μὲν** dv, merely, ‘while yet absent’ from this spot. It is grammatically possible to take it αϑ-εὅτι ἐκποδὼν ἣν, ‘I lamented that I had been absent’ (cp. fr. 667 ἀνδρὸς κακῶς πράσσοντος ἐκποδὼν φίλοι). But this would be strained ; and νῦν δ᾽ ὁρῶν is decisive in favour of the simpler νον.

**ὑπεστέναζον** : restraining the vehemence of his grief: cp. 322.

## 1003

## 1004
