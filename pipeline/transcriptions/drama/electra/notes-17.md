## 956
**κατοκνήσεις**. The clause ὅπως κατοκνήσεις denotes the object of the appeal implied in els σὲ δὴ βλέπω. Cp. Ar. Eg. 1255 καί σ᾽ αἰτῶ βραχύ, | ὅπως ἔσομαί σοι Φανός. This is the normal construction. The 2. J. κατοκνήσῃς is also correct, but is less probable. Any verb can be followed by a ‘final’ clause in the subjunctive, expressing the ‘end’ or purpose of the action, as ἔρχεται ἵνα ἴδῃ. But a verb of endeavouring, praying, contriving, usually takes an ¢object’ clause, expressing the object of the effort, with ὅπως (or ὡς) and faz. παῖε. τ. : as πειρᾶται ὅπως ὄψεται. Exceptions occur, as Xen. An. 5. 6. § 21 προστατεῦσαι.. ὅπως ἐκπλεύσῃ ἡ στρατιά: Dem. or. 1 § 2 παρασκευάσασθαι... ὅπως ἐνθένδε βοηθήσητε καὶ μὴ πάθητε ταὐτόν: but these are comparatively rare. After a verb of effort, the presumption is always in favour of the fut. indic. See Goodwin, Moods and Tenses (new ed.), § 364.

Brunck’s comment, ¢ soloece vulgo κατοκνήσῃς, alludes to the rule laid down by R. Dawes (ob. 1766) in Miscellanea Critica (p. 227), and once known as the canon Dawvesianus, that ὅπως could not be used with the first aor. subjunct. act. or midd. Among many examples that refute it are ἐκπλεύσῃ in the passage just quoted from Xen. (the fut. being ἐκπλεύσεται), and κλαύσω in 1122 (the fut. being κλαύσομαι).
