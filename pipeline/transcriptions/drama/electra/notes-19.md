**μητρὸς... φίλος**, her ‘dear one,” ‘darling.’ The objections made to φίλος, on the ground that it must mean friend,’ illustrate the danger of identifying a word with its conventional equivalent in another language. The conjectures are all bad (γάνος, θάλος, τέκος, φάος, and, worst of all, ᾽φελος, z.e. ὄφελος).

The schol. on 1146 has, τὸ δὲ φίλος ἀντὶ ὄφελος. But P. N. Papageorgius corrects ὄφελος to ὦ φίλος (Sciolia in Soph. Trag. Vetera, Leipsic, 1888). And this is confirmed by the first part of the same scholium : οὐκ ἦσθα τῆς μητρὸς ἀλλὰ μᾶλλον καὶ ἐμοῦ : Ζ.6., the scholiast understood, ‘for thou didst not belong to thy mother more than to me,’ and took φίλος as=a vocative.

## 1148
**ἀδελφὴ σοὶ**, rather than ἀδελφή σοι, since a slight emphasis on the pron. better marks the reciprocity of affection; ‘I was thy nurse; and by thee I was ever called “‘sister.”’’ He had other sisters, but it was she who stood in the child’s mind for all that ‘sister’ means.—wpooyudὦμην : cp. 274.

## 1152
**τέθνηκ᾽ ἐγὼ σοί**: ‘I am dead in relation to thee.” For Electra, this is another way of saying, “1 am dead, so far as any aim or joy in life is concerned’; since the only hopes which made life tolerable to her were centred in her brother. See her words in 808 ff., ὡς μ᾽ ἀπώλεσας θανών" | ἀποσπάσας γὰρ κ.τ.λ. For the dat. σοί, cp. Pk. 1030 τέθνηχ᾽ ὑμὶν πάλαι. These words are usu. written τέθνηκ᾽ ἐγώ σοι: but the enclitic σοι destroys the point.

Others explain: (1) ‘I am dead because of thee’; ‘thou hast been the death of me.” The dat. is then like that in “47. 1128 τῷδε δ᾽ οἴχομαι (‘in his purpose, I am dead’). But this is less suitable to the context. (2) “1 am dead to thee, as thou to me,’—z.e.,, we are now parted from each other by the barrier between earth and Hades.—The objection to τέθνηκ᾽ ἐγώ᾽ σὺ k.7.\. is that the unqualified τέθνηκα would be unfitting when a reference to actual death immediately follows.

## 1154
