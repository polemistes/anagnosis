**λύει κελαινὰ βλέφαρα**, allows her eyes to close in darkness. Adet=relaxes: the eyelids are deprived of power to remain open. The phrase has been suggested by the epic λῦσε δὲ γυῖα, λέλυντο δὲ γυῖα, etc., and seems quite intelligible ; though, doubtless, it would have been more natural to say κλύει, as Soph. has done in fr. 640, βλέφαρα κέκλῃται. In [(Eur.] R%es. 8 we have λῦσον βλεφάρων γοργωπὸν ἕδραν, of opening the eyes; but that has no bearing on the different use here. Wolff brings what at first sight is a perfect parallel: Anthol. P. 3. 11 ἀνθ’ ὧν ὄμματ᾽ ἔλυσε τὰ Iopybros ἐνθάδε Περσεύς. But unfortunately neither he nor Bellermann has observed the meaning. It is not, ‘caused the Gorgon’s eyes to close,’ but, ‘uncovered the Gorgon’s head.” The epigram refers to Perseus bringing Medusa’s head to Seriphos, and therewith petrifying Polydectes, who had married Danaé, and sent her son on his perilous mission.—The objection to μύει is that elsewhere the verb has these usages:—(1) intrans.,—éuuara μύει, the eyes close, or μύομεν, we shut our eyes.
