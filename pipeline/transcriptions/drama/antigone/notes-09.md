## 370
**ὑψίπολις** seems best taken as Ξε ὑψηλὴν πόλιν ἔχων : cp. Pind. 2. 8. 22 a δικαιόπολις...νᾶσος (Aegina). In O. 2. 8 Theron, tyrant of Acragas, is called ὀρθόπολις in an active sense, 85

**ὀρθῶν τὴν πόλιν**. In O. 7: 510 ἁδύπολις Ξε ἁνδάνων τῇ πόλει: but it is harder to suppose that ὑψίπολις could have been intended to mean, ‘standing high in his city.” Nor would that be the fittest sense. The loyal citizen makes the prosperous city ; and her prosperity is his. See on 189. In this clause the Chorus thinks especially of Creon (191 τοιοῖσδ᾽ ἐγὼ νόμοισι τήν δ᾽ αὔξω πόλιν).

**ἄπολις**. Where the typical citizen is a law-breaker, the city is ruined, and the evil-doer is left citiless. So Creon had described law-breaking as ἄτην.. ἀστοῖς (185). The contrast with ὑψίπολις shows that the sense is not merely, ‘when a man breaks the law he becomes an exile’ (Lys. or. 21 § 35 ἀντὶ τω πολιτῶν ἀπόλιδας). The central thought is the power of human wit to make or mar the πόλις, according as the man is moral or immoral.

**τὸν μὴ καλόν**, the generic μή, such a mood as is not good.

**τόλμας χάριν**, by reason of (‘thanks to’) his audacity, with ξύνεστι, not with ἄπο- Ais (ἐστ). In *O. T.* 888 δυσπότμου χάριν χλιδᾶς is not precisely similar, since it goes with κακά vw ἕλοιτο μοῖρα. Others point αἱ ξύνεστι, taking τόλμας χάριν with μήτ᾽ ἐμοί κ.τ.λ. : but μήτ᾽ should clearly be the first word in that sentence.
