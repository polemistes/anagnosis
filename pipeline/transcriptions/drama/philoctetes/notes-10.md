Schneidewin remarks that Philoctetes, the former comrade of Heracles, might naturally name Chalcodon, who had been the companion of Heracles in an expedition against the Eleans (Paus. 8. 15. 6). At any rate the Attic poet might think of the Attic legend, according to which Theseus had sent his sons for protection to Chalcodon’s son Elephenor, before retiring from Athens to Scyros (Plut. *Thes.* 35).
