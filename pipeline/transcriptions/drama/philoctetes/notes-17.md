We certainly cannot render (with Cavallin), ‘a warm sleep (i.e. a sound one, in which a gentle warmth pervades the body) is favourable to our plan.” ἀλεής occurs only here, though Hesych. has d\ebs= ἀλέεινός. It is, however, a correct formation from ἀλέα: and ἀλέας (gen.) is not a probable correction. The easy emendation ἀδεὴς (A for A) would give the sense, ‘’tis a secure (2.6. tranquil), sound sleep.” This may be right; but I have preferred to keep the Ms. reading. The addition of δ᾽ after ἀλεὴς might seem desirable in such a parenthesis: cp. Dem. or. 18 § 308 ἢ ἄλλο τι δύσκολον γέγονε, (πολλὰ δὲ τὰ ἀνθρώπινα,) εἶτ᾽ ἐπὶ τούτῳ τῷ καιρῷ κ.τ.λ.

Some reject ἀλεὴς ὕπνος ἐσθλός as a mere gloss. But a marginal commentator might have been expected to use more prosaic language,—e.g., ὁ μεσημ- Bpwos ὕπνος βαθύς. Dobree, reading ἀδεὴς, supposed the following process. (1) In v. 864 Soph. wrote ἀδεὴς πόνος ἐσθλός. This was supplanted by a gloss, πόνος ὁ μὴ φοβῶν κράτιστος, which now stands there. (2) Then the displaced ἀδεὴς πόνος ἐσθλός was corrupted into ἀλεὴς ὕπνος ἐσθλός, and inserted in the text after νύχιος. This hypothesis is very ingenious, but it seems much too complex to be probable.

## 865-1080
Third ἐπεισόδιον. Neoptolemus, overcome by remorse, confesses that Troy is their destination. Philoctetes demands the restoration of the bow; and Neoptolemus is on the point of restoring it, when Odysseus enters. As Ph. refuses to accompany them, Odysseus decides to leave him behind, and departs for the ship, ordering N. to follow him. Meanwhile, by N.’s command, the Chorus remain with Ph., in the hope that he may alter his resolve.

## 865
**μηδ᾽ ἀφεστάναι φρενῶν** : Eur. Or. 1021 ἐξέστην φρενῶν. For ἀφεστάναι, cp. Ar. Vesp. 1457 τὸ γὰρ ἀποστῆναι χαλεπὸν | φύσεος. The words convey a hurried reproof and warning,—‘do not lose your wits’ (through fear). All their presence of mind is needed, since Philoctetes is awaking.
