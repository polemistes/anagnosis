## 100
**κλεῖα**, for κλέα or κλέεα, the lays or legends about the heroes of old. Apoll. Rhod. i. 1, ἀρχόμενος σέο, Φοῖβε, παλαιγενέων κλέα φωτῶν μνήσομαι. The lengthened form seems due to the digamma. Compare κλύω, κλυτὸς, with κλείω, κλέω, ῥέω, ῥυτὺς, χέω, χυτὸς, &e. In II. ix. 189, ἄειδε δ᾽ ἄρα κλέα ἀνδρῶν, Van Lennep remarks that Heyne preferred κλέε᾽ ἀνδρῶν. If κλέα he a genuine ancient form, it must be referred to a nominative κλὴς for kAeF-s. Compare κληδών, kAendav. Curtins (150) gives kAv as the root.

## 102
For aly’ Gye there are variants αἶψα δ᾽ 6, αἶψα δ᾽ Gye, aly δ᾽ ὁ, αἶψά Te. This indicates a confusion between two old readings, ἂψ ὅγε and αἶψα δὲ δυσφρ. See New Cratylus, § 297. Two or three MSS. give δυσφροσύνεων, a good reading in itself, and Hesiod is very fond of the termination ---οσύνη.

## 103
**παρέτραπε**, scil. θυμὸν ἀπὸ κηδέων. Euripides, Med. 195, laments that music was not much used for consoling grief; στυγίους δὲ βροτῶν οὐδεὶς λύπας εὕρετο μούσῃ καὶ πολυχόρδοις ᾧδαῖς παύειν.

## 104
**χαίρετε**, “farewell,”—a common conclusion to long invocations, e. g. in

Theoer. Id. ii. 165, xxii. 214, xvii. 136. Perhaps, if we regard this verse as a continuation of the praises of the Muse, the meaning is ‘all hail to you!’, i. e. assist me to sing. Goettling condemns as spurious the passage from 105—115. He says, “hoe versu (104) necessario concluditur prooemium theogoniae.” He objects, that in v. 108 we should have expected χάος kal γαῖα instead of θεοὶ kal γαῖα, especially as the birth of the gods is mentioned in v. 111. This latter verse indeed occurred as v. 46, and it is omitted in the quotation of this passage by Origen (see on v. 125). Further, it seems likely that v. 110 was made up from v. 332. Others have thought that there are traces of two recensions, 6. g. 105—7 in place of 108 —13. We can determine nothing here with certainty, and perhaps Van Lennep is as near the truth as any of the other critics: “Mihi, quod in rudioris aevi carmine non sunt omnia pro nostro sensu concinne dicta aut disposita, quodque tautologia in quibusdam offensionem facit, non adferrre continuo necessitatem videtur, ut vel ordinem in eo versuum immutemus, vel duplieis recensionis commento tautologia liberare illud conemur.” He thinks that in this brief epitome of the subject (105—115) the Muses are invoked to relate who were brought into being, how (108), and in what order (115).
