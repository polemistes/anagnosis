## 1056
This class of unhistorical argument, based on passages taken from holy writ, has been familiar in all ages. The Sophists were very familiar with the Homeric poems, and Protagoras set great store by his ability to analyze them (Plato, *Prot.* 339 A), but even Plato often appeals to them as a final authority (Freeman, *Schools* etc. pp. 228 sqq., P. The argument is an etymological one based on the supposed identity of ἀγορή and ἀγορά, ἀγορᾶσθαι and ἀγοράζειν.
