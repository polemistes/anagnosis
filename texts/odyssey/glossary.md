# title: Glossary of names and words in the Odyssey
# lang: eng
# license: CC BY-SA 4.0

## Ἀλκίνοος
**Ἀλκίνοος, ὁ** Alcinous, king of the Phaeacians in Scheria, son of Nausithous, husband of Arete and father of Nausicaa; he receives Odysseus, hears the tale of his wanderings and sends him home (books 6–13).

## Εὔμαιος
**Εὔμαιος, ὁ** Eumaeus, Odysseus' loyal swineherd, son of Ctesius, king of Syrie, who was kidnapped as a child by Phoenician traders and sold to Laertes (15.403–484); he takes in the disguised Odysseus and fights beside him against the suitors (books 14–17, 20–22).

## Εὐρύμαχος
**Εὐρύμαχος, ὁ** Eurymachus, son of Polybus, with Antinous the leading suitor of Penelope; killed by Odysseus (22.44–88).

## Λαερτιάδης
**Λαερτιάδης, -ου, ὁ** son of Laertes, i.e. Odysseus, often in the address διογενὲς Λαερτιάδη (books 5–24).

## Εὐρύκλεια
**Εὐρύκλεια, ἡ** Eurycleia, daughter of Ops son of Peisenor, bought by Laertes as a girl; the nurse of Odysseus and of Telemachus. She recognizes Odysseus by his scar (book 19) and names the disloyal maids (book 22).

## Αἴγισθος
**Αἴγισθος, ὁ** Aegisthus, son of Thyestes, who seduced Clytaemnestra and murdered Agamemnon on his return from Troy, and was killed by Orestes (1.29–43, 3.193–310, 4.512–537, 11.409, 24.22, 24.97).

## Μέντωρ
**Μέντωρ, -ορος, ὁ** Mentor, son of Alcimus, an old Ithacan friend of Odysseus, who left him in charge of his household (2.225–227). Athena often takes his shape to guide Telemachus and to help Odysseus (books 2–4, 22, 24). Not to be confused with Mentes (Μέντης).

## Αὐτόλυκος
**Αὐτόλυκος, ὁ** Autolycus, father of Anticleia and so Odysseus' grandfather on his mother's side, unmatched in thieving and in oaths; he named Odysseus, who got his scar from a boar while hunting with Autolycus' sons on Parnassus (11.85, 19.394–466, 21.220, 24.334).

## Τειρεσίης
**Τειρεσίης, -αο, ὁ** (Att. Τειρεσίας) Teiresias, the blind Theban seer, who alone among the dead keeps his understanding (10.492–495); Odysseus consults his ghost, who foretells his homecoming and his last journey (11.90–151; also 12.267, 23.251, 23.323).

## Εὐρύλοχος
**Εὐρύλοχος, ὁ** Eurylochus, Odysseus' companion and kinsman by marriage (10.441); he leads the men sent to Circe's house and alone escapes being turned into a swine (10.205–273), and on Thrinacia he persuades the crew to land and to kill the cattle of Helios (12.278–352).

## Ἀρήτη
**Ἀρήτη, ἡ** Arete, queen of the Phaeacians, daughter of Rhexenor and so both niece and wife of Alcinous, mother of Nausicaa; Odysseus is told to supplicate her first (7.53–77, 7.139–154; books 7, 8, 11, 13).

## Πόλυβος
**Πόλυβος, ὁ** Polybus, the name of several men: (1) the father of the suitor Eurymachus (1.399, 2.177, 15.519 etc.); (2) a rich man of Egyptian Thebes, husband of Alcandre, who gave gifts to Menelaus (4.126); (3) a Phaeacian craftsman who made the purple ball (8.373); (4) a suitor, killed by Eumaeus (22.243, 22.284).

## Ἀμφίνομος
**Ἀμφίνομος, ὁ** Amphinomus, son of Nisus, from Dulichium, the most considerate of the suitors and the most pleasing to Penelope (16.394–398); Odysseus warns him in vain to leave (18.119–157), and Telemachus kills him (22.89–96).

## Ναυσικάα
**Ναυσικάα, ἡ** Nausicaa, daughter of Alcinous and Arete; sent by Athena in a dream to wash clothes at the river mouth, she meets the shipwrecked Odysseus and shows him the way to the city (book 6; 7.12, 8.457–468).

## Περσεφόνεια
**Περσεφόνεια, ἡ** Persephone, queen of the dead, wife of Hades (10.491–564, book 11).

## Μελάνθιος
**Μελάνθιος, ὁ** Melanthius, Odysseus' goatherd, son of Dolius, who sides with the suitors; he kicks and abuses the disguised Odysseus (17.212–253), brings the suitors arms from the storeroom (22.135–200) and is put to a cruel death (22.474–477). Also called Μελανθεύς.

## Νεστορίδης
**Νεστορίδης, -ου, ὁ** son of Nestor; in the Odyssey always Peisistratus (books 3, 4, 15).

## Πεισίστρατος
**Πεισίστρατος, ὁ** Peisistratus, the unmarried son of Nestor, who drives Telemachus from Pylos to Sparta and back (3.36–486, book 4, 15.44–216).

## Μέδων
**Μέδων, -οντος, ὁ** Medon, herald in Odysseus' house, who serves the suitors but tells Penelope of their plot against Telemachus (4.677–714, 16.252, 16.412, 17.172); spared at Telemachus' plea (22.357–377), he tells the Ithacans that a god fought for Odysseus (24.439–449). (The common noun μέδων, ruler, lord, belongs to μέδω.)

## Εὐρυνόμη
**Εὐρυνόμη, ἡ** Eurynome, housekeeper (ταμίη) of Penelope (17.495, 18.164–178, 19.96–97, 20.4, 23.154, 23.289–293).

## Δημόδοκος
**Δημόδοκος, ὁ** Demodocus, the blind bard of the Phaeacians, who sings of the quarrel of Odysseus and Achilles, of Ares and Aphrodite, and of the Wooden Horse (8.62–82, 8.266–366, 8.499–520; 13.28).

## Θεσπρωτοί
**Θεσπρωτοί, οἱ** the Thesprotians, a people of Epirus in north-western Greece, whose king Pheidon has a part in Odysseus' false tales (14.315–335, 16.65, 16.427, 17.526, 19.271–292).

## Θεοκλύμενος
**Θεοκλύμενος, ὁ** Theoclymenus, a seer of the line of Melampus, son of Polypheides, who has fled Argos after killing a kinsman; Telemachus takes him on board at Pylos (15.256–286), and he foretells Odysseus' return and the suitors' doom (15.529–534, 17.151–161, 20.350–370).

## Πείραιος
**Πείραιος, ὁ** Peiraeus, son of Clytius, Telemachus' trusted companion, who looks after Theoclymenus (15.539–546) and keeps Menelaus' gifts in his house (17.55–83; 20.372).

## Κίκονες
**Κίκονες, -ων, οἱ** the Cicones, a people of Thrace; Odysseus sacks their city Ismarus and loses six men from each ship when they strike back (9.39–66; 9.165, 23.310).

## Τάφιοι
**Τάφιοι, οἱ** the Taphians, a seafaring people of traders and pirates, ruled by Mentes, whose shape Athena takes (1.105, 1.181, 1.419); Taphian pirates figure in the tales of Eumaeus (14.452, 15.427) and of Antinous' father (16.426).

## Ἀντίλοχος
**Ἀντίλοχος, ὁ** Antilochus, son of Nestor, swift runner and fighter, killed at Troy (3.111–112, 4.186–202); his ghost is with Achilles (11.468, 24.16, 24.78).

## Ἀγέλαος
**Ἀγέλαος, ὁ** Agelaus, son of Damastor, a suitor who leads the fight in the hall; killed by Odysseus (20.321–344, 22.136–293). Also Ἀγέλεως.

## Ἀμφιμέδων
**Ἀμφιμέδων, -οντος, ὁ** Amphimedon, son of Melaneus, a suitor; he grazes Telemachus' wrist and is killed by him (22.277–284); his ghost tells Agamemnon how the suitors died (24.103–190).

## Ἦλις
**Ἦλις, -ιδος, ἡ** Elis, the region of the north-western Peloponnese, ruled by the Epeians (4.635, 13.275, 15.298, 21.347, 24.431).

## Ναυσίθοος
**Ναυσίθοος, ὁ** Nausithous, son of Poseidon and Periboea, father of Alcinous and Rhexenor; he led the Phaeacians from Hypereia to Scheria (6.7–10, 7.56–63) and foretold Poseidon's anger at their convoys (8.564–571).

## Ποντόνοος
**Ποντόνοος, ὁ** Pontonous, herald of Alcinous (7.179–182, 8.65, 13.50–53).

## Ἐπειός
**Ἐπειός, ὁ** (1) Epeius, who built the Wooden Horse with Athena's help (8.493, 11.523). (2) pl. **Ἐπειοί, οἱ** the Epeians, the people of Elis (13.275, 15.298, 24.431).

## Ἀντιφάτης
**Ἀντιφάτης, -αο, ὁ** Antiphates: (1) king of the man-eating Laestrygonians, who seizes one of Odysseus' men for his meal (10.106–124, 10.199); (2) son of the seer Melampus, father of Oecles (15.242–243).

## Μελανθεύς
**Μελανθεύς, -ῆος, ὁ** = Μελάνθιος, Melanthius the goatherd, son of Dolius (17.212, 20.255, 21.176, 22.152, 22.159).

## Φιλοίτιος
**Φιλοίτιος, ὁ** Philoetius, Odysseus' loyal cowherd, who helps him kill the suitors (20.185–240, 21.240, 21.388, book 22).

## Ζάκυνθος
**Ζάκυνθος, ἡ** Zacynthus, a wooded island south of Ithaca, part of Odysseus' realm; some of the suitors come from it (1.246, 9.24, 16.250, 19.131).

## Ἁλιθέρσης
**Ἁλιθέρσης, ὁ** Halitherses, son of Mastor, an old Ithacan seer and friend of Odysseus; he reads the omen of the two eagles (2.157–176) and warns the suitors' kinsmen (24.451–462; 17.68).

## Πύλιος
**Πύλιος, -α, -ον** of Pylos, Pylian; οἱ Πύλιοι, Nestor's people (3.31, 3.59, 15.216, 15.227).

## Μάλεια
**Μάλεια, ἡ** (Ep. gen. pl. Μαλειάων) Malea, the cape at the south-eastern tip of the Peloponnese, where storms drive ships off course (3.287, 4.514, 9.80, 19.187).

## Μεγαπένθης
**Μεγαπένθης, ὁ** Megapenthes, son of Menelaus by a slave woman, married at the time of Telemachus' visit (4.10–14, 15.100–122).

## Δουλίχιον
**Δουλίχιον, τό** Dulichium, an island near Ithaca, rich in wheat, home of Nisus and Amphinomus and of fifty-two of the suitors (16.247); its location is uncertain (9.24, 14.335, 19.292). A man of Dulichium is Δουλιχιεύς (18.424).

## Ἐλπήνωρ
**Ἐλπήνωρ, -ορος, ὁ** Elpenor, the youngest of Odysseus' crew, who, heavy with wine, falls from Circe's roof and is killed (10.552–560); his ghost begs Odysseus for burial (11.51–83), which is given (12.8–15).

## Δαμαστορίδης
**Δαμαστορίδης, -ου, ὁ** son of Damastor, i.e. the suitor Agelaus (20.321, 22.212, 22.241, 22.293).

## Μέντης
**Μέντης, ὁ** Mentes, son of Anchialus, lord of the Taphians and guest-friend of Odysseus' house, whose shape Athena takes on her first visit to Telemachus (1.105, 1.180, 1.418). Not to be confused with Mentor (Μέντωρ).

## Πεισηνορίδης
**Πεισηνορίδης, -ου, ὁ** son of Peisenor, i.e. Ops, the father of Eurycleia (1.429, 2.347, 20.148).

## Φρόνιος
**Φρόνιος, ὁ** Phronius, father of Noemon, who lends Telemachus his ship (2.386, 4.630, 4.648).

## Νηληιάδης
**Νηληιάδης, -ου, ὁ** son of Neleus, i.e. Nestor (3.79, 3.202, 3.247).

## Κλυταιμνήστρη
**Κλυταιμνήστρη, ἡ** Clytaemnestra, wife of Agamemnon; seduced by Aegisthus, she helped to murder her husband and killed Cassandra (3.263–272, 11.409–439).

## Ὀρτίλοχος
**Ὀρτίλοχος, ὁ** Ortilochus, son of the river Alpheus and father of Diocles of Pherae (3.489, 15.187); in his house in Messene Odysseus met Iphitus (21.16).

## Ἐτεωνεύς
**Ἐτεωνεύς, ὁ** Eteoneus, son of Boethous, attendant of Menelaus (4.22–36, 15.95–98).

## Σιδόνιος
**Σιδόνιος, -α, -ον** Sidonian, of Sidon in Phoenicia; οἱ Σιδόνιοι, the Sidonians, whom Menelaus visited (4.84) and whose king gave him a silver mixing-bowl (15.118); Σιδονίη, the land of Sidon (13.285).

## Ἀτρεύς
**Ἀτρεύς, -έος, ὁ** Atreus, father of Agamemnon and Menelaus (4.462, 4.543, 11.436).

## Ἀρκεισιάδης
**Ἀρκεισιάδης, -ου, ὁ** son of Arceisius, i.e. Laertes (4.755, 24.270, 24.517).

## Λαιστρυγών
**Λαιστρυγών, -όνος, ὁ** a Laestrygonian; pl. οἱ Λαιστρυγόνες, a race of man-eating giants at Telepylus, who destroy all Odysseus' ships but his own (10.80–132).

## Πηλείων
**Πηλείων, -ωνος, ὁ** son of Peleus, i.e. Achilles (11.470, 11.551, 24.23).

## Πειρίθοος
**Πειρίθοος, ὁ** Peirithous, king of the Lapiths, companion of Theseus (11.631); at his wedding the drunken Centaur Eurytion began the fight of Centaurs and Lapiths (21.295–304).

## Νῖσος
**Νῖσος, ὁ** Nisus, son of Aretus, a rich lord of Dulichium, father of the suitor Amphinomus (16.395, 18.127, 18.413).

## Ἔχετος
**Ἔχετος, ὁ** Echetus, a cruel king, "the maimer of all men", to whom the suitors threaten to send Irus and the beggar (18.85–87, 18.116, 21.308).

## Πείσανδρος
**Πείσανδρος, ὁ** Peisander, son of Polyctor, a suitor, who gives Penelope a necklace (18.299–300); killed by Philoetius (22.243, 22.268).

## Ἴφιτος
**Ἴφιτος, ὁ** Iphitus, son of Eurytus, who gave Odysseus the great bow when they met in Messene, and was later killed by Heracles (21.11–38).

## Ὤπς
**Ὤψ, Ὠπός, ὁ** Ops, son of Peisenor, father of Eurycleia (1.429, 2.347).

## Ἄντιφος
**Ἄντιφος, ὁ** Antiphus: (1) son of Aegyptius, one of Odysseus' companions, the last man eaten by the Cyclops (2.17–20); (2) an old Ithacan friend of Odysseus' house (17.68).

## Εὐρύνομος
**Εὐρύνομος, ὁ** Eurynomus, son of Aegyptius, one of the suitors (2.21–22, 22.242).

## Τυρώ
**Τυρώ, -οῦς, ἡ** Tyro, daughter of Salmoneus and wife of Cretheus; she bore Pelias and Neleus to Poseidon, who came to her in the shape of the river Enipeus (2.120, 11.235–259).

## Ἀλκμήνη
**Ἀλκμήνη, ἡ** Alcmene, wife of Amphitryon, who bore Heracles to Zeus (2.120, 11.266–268).

## Μαστορίδης
**Μαστορίδης, -ου, ὁ** son of Mastor, i.e. Halitherses (2.158, 24.452).

## Εὐηνορίδης
**Εὐηνορίδης, -ου, ὁ** son of Euenor, i.e. the suitor Leocritus (2.242, 22.294).

## Λειώκριτος
**Λειώκριτος, ὁ** Leocritus, son of Euenor, a suitor who mocks Mentor in the assembly (2.242–256); killed by Telemachus (22.294–296).

## Φιλοκτήτης
**Φιλοκτήτης, ὁ** Philoctetes, son of Poeas, the best archer among the Greeks at Troy (3.190, 8.219).

## Κύδωνες
**Κύδωνες, -ων, οἱ** the Cydonians, a people of western Crete, living by the river Iardanus (3.292, 19.176).

## Φηραί
**Φηραί, -ῶν, αἱ** Pherae in Messenia, home of Diocles, where Telemachus and Peisistratus spend the night on the way to and from Sparta (3.488, 15.186).

## Ἀλφειός
**Ἀλφειός, ὁ** the Alpheus, river of the western Peloponnese, father of Ortilochus (3.489, 15.187).

## Βοηθοΐδης
**Βοηθοΐδης, -ου, ὁ** son of Boethous, i.e. Eteoneus (4.31, 15.140).

## Φυλώ
**Φυλώ, -οῦς, ἡ** Phylo, a maid of Helen (4.125, 4.133).

## Φιλομηλεΐδης
**Φιλομηλεΐδης, -ου, ὁ** Philomeleides, a wrestler in Lesbos whom Odysseus threw (4.343, 17.134).

## Γυραί
**Γυραί, -ῶν, αἱ** the Gyraean rocks, where Poseidon wrecked Ajax son of Oileus (4.499–511); their location is uncertain.

## Ῥαδάμανθυς
**Ῥαδάμανθυς, ὁ** Rhadamanthys, the fair-haired, who lives in the Elysian plain (4.564); the Phaeacians once carried him to Euboea (7.323).

## Ἐχένηος
**Ἐχένηος, ὁ** Echeneus, the oldest of the Phaeacians, who speaks for Odysseus (7.155–166, 11.342–346).

## Πηλείδης
**Πηλείδης, -ου (Ep. -εω), ὁ** son of Peleus, i.e. Achilles (8.75, 24.18).

## Κλυτόνηος
**Κλυτόνηος, ὁ** Clytoneus, son of Alcinous, winner of the foot-race (8.119–125).

## Αἰαίη
**Αἰαίη, ἡ** (adj.) of Aeaea, Aeaean: Κίρκη Αἰαίη, Circe of Aeaea (9.32); νῆσος Αἰαίη, the island of Aeaea (11.70). See Αἰαία.

## Ἴσμαρος
**Ἴσμαρος, ἡ** Ismarus, city of the Cicones in Thrace, sacked by Odysseus (9.40); Maron, priest of Apollo there, gave him the strong wine he later gave the Cyclops (9.196–211).

## Αἰολίη
**Αἰολίη, ἡ** Aeolia, the floating island of Aeolus, walled with bronze (10.1–4, 10.55).

## Ἱπποτάδης
**Ἱπποτάδης, -ου, ὁ** son of Hippotas, i.e. Aeolus, keeper of the winds (10.2, 10.36).

## Αἰαία
**Αἰαία, ἡ** Aeaea, the island of Circe (10.135, 12.3).

## Αἰήτης
**Αἰήτης, -αο, ὁ** Aeetes, son of Helios and Perse, brother of Circe (10.137); the Argo sailed home from his land (12.70).

## Περιμήδης
**Περιμήδης, ὁ** Perimedes, a companion of Odysseus (11.23, 12.195).

## Κρηθεύς
**Κρηθεύς, -ῆος, ὁ** Cretheus, son of Aeolus, husband of Tyro and father of Aeson, Pheres and Amythaon (11.237, 11.258–259).

## Ἐνιπεύς
**Ἐνιπεύς, -ῆος, ὁ** the Enipeus, a river loved by Tyro, whose shape Poseidon took (11.238–252).

## Πελίης
**Πελίης, ὁ** Pelias, son of Tyro by Poseidon, brother of Neleus, king of Iolcus (11.254–257).

## Ἀμφίων
**Ἀμφίων, -ονος, ὁ** Amphion: (1) son of Zeus and Antiope, who with his brother Zethus founded and walled Thebes (11.260–265); (2) son of Iasus, king of Orchomenus, father of Chloris (11.281–284).

## Ζῆθος
**Ζῆθος, ὁ** Zethus, son of Zeus and Antiope, brother of Amphion, a founder of Thebes (11.262); his son Itylus was killed by his own mother, the daughter of Pandareus (19.518–523).

## Ἀμφιτρύων
**Ἀμφιτρύων, -ωνος, ὁ** Amphitryon, husband of Alcmene (11.266); Heracles is called his son (11.270).

## Ἰασίδης
**Ἰασίδης, -ου, ὁ** son of Iasus: (1) Amphion, king of Orchomenus (11.283); (2) Dmetor, king of Cyprus (17.443).

## Ἰφικληεῖος
**Ἰφικληεῖος, -είη, -εῖον** of Iphiclus: βίη Ἰφικληείη, the might of Iphiclus, i.e. mighty Iphiclus, whose cattle had to be driven off from Phylace to win Pero (11.290, 11.296).

## Πλαγκταί
**Πλαγκταί, αἱ** the Planctae or Wandering Rocks, which only the Argo has passed (12.59–72, 23.327).

## Λαμπετίη
**Λαμπετίη, ἡ** Lampetie, a nymph, daughter of Helios and Neaera, who herds his cattle on Thrinacia and tells him of their slaughter (12.132–136, 12.374–375).

## Αἰαῖος
**Αἰαῖος, -αίη, -αῖον** of Aeaea, Aeaean: Κίρκη Αἰαίη, Circe of Aeaea (12.268, 12.273).

## Ἀρκείσιος
**Ἀρκείσιος, ὁ** Arceisius, father of Laertes and grandfather of Odysseus (14.182, 16.118).

## Μάντιος
**Μάντιος, ὁ** Mantius, son of the seer Melampus, brother of Antiphates, father of Cleitus and Polypheides (15.242–249).

## Οϊκλῆς
**Ὀϊκλῆς, ὁ** Oecles, son of Antiphates and father of the seer Amphiaraus (15.243–244).

## Ἀρητιάδης
**Ἀρητιάδης, -ου, ὁ** son of Aretus, i.e. Nisus of Dulichium (16.395, 18.413).

## Δουλιχίης
**Δουλιχιεύς, -ῆος, ὁ** a man of Dulichium: said of Nisus (18.127) and of his son Amphinomus (18.395).

## Εὐρυδάμας
**Εὐρυδάμας, -αντος, ὁ** Eurydamas, a suitor, who gives Penelope a pair of earrings (18.297); killed by Odysseus (22.283).

## Πολυκτορίδης
**Πολυκτορίδης, -ου, ὁ** son of Polyctor, i.e. the suitor Peisander (18.299, 22.243).

## Μελανθώ
**Μελανθώ, -οῦς, ἡ** Melantho, a maid, daughter of Dolius, brought up by Penelope but the mistress of Eurymachus; she insults the disguised Odysseus (18.321–336, 19.65–69).

## Δευκαλίων
**Δευκαλίων, -ωνος, ὁ** Deucalion, son of Minos, father of Idomeneus; in his Cretan tale to Penelope, Odysseus claims to be Deucalion's younger son Aethon (19.180–184).

## Εὐρυτίδης
**Εὐρυτίδης, -ου, ὁ** son of Eurytus, i.e. Iphitus (21.14, 21.37).

## Ἀγέλεως
**Ἀγέλεως, ὁ** = Ἀγέλαος, Agelaus the suitor (22.131, 22.247).

## Δημοπτόλεμος
**Δημοπτόλεμος, ὁ** Demoptolemus, a suitor, killed by Odysseus (22.242, 22.266).

## Νήιον
**Νήιον, τό** Neion, a wooded mountain on Ithaca above the harbour Rheithron (1.186).

## Ἶλος
**Ἶλος, ὁ** Ilus, son of Mermerus, at Ephyra, who would not give Odysseus poison for his arrows, for fear of the gods (1.259–264).

## Μερμερίδης
**Μερμερίδης, -ου, ὁ** son of Mermerus, i.e. Ilus of Ephyra (1.260).

## Πεισήνωρ

## Ἀναβησίσευς
