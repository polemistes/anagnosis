# title: Glossary of names and words in the Symposium
# lang: eng
# license: CC BY-SA 4.0

## Ἀγάθων
**Ἀγάθων, ὁ** Agathon, Athenian tragic poet, at whose house the dinner is held the day after his first victory with a tragedy (173a); he gives the fifth speech (194e–197e).

## Ἐρυξίμαχος
**Ἐρυξίμαχος, ὁ** Eryximachus, Athenian physician, son of Acumenus; he gives the third speech, on love in medicine, music and the seasons (185e–188e).

## Ἀλκιβιάδης
**Ἀλκιβιάδης, ὁ** Alcibiades, Athenian statesman and general; he comes in drunk at the end of the speeches (212d) and praises Socrates (215a–222b).

## Ἀριστοφάνης
**Ἀριστοφάνης, ὁ** Aristophanes, the comic poet; his speech tells how Zeus cut the double human beings in two (189c–193d).

## Ἀριστόδημος
**Ἀριστόδημος, ὁ** Aristodemus of Cydathenaeum, a devoted follower of Socrates, who was at the dinner and told Apollodorus about it (173b).

## Διοτίμα
**Διοτίμα, ἡ** Diotima, a woman of Mantinea, wise in love, whose teaching Socrates reports in his speech (201d–212a).

## Ἄλκηστις
**Ἄλκηστις, ἡ** Alcestis, wife of Admetus, who died in her husband's place; Phaedrus' example of a lover who dies for the beloved (179b–d).

## Μαρσύας
**Μαρσύας, ὁ** Marsyas, the satyr whose piping charmed its hearers; Alcibiades likens Socrates to him (215b–e).

## Γλαύκων
**Γλαύκων, ὁ** Glaucon: at 172c the acquaintance who asks Apollodorus about the dinner; at 222b Glaucon, father of Charmides.

## Ἀκουμενός
**Ἀκουμενός, ὁ** Acumenus, Athenian physician, father of Eryximachus (176b, 198a).

## Παρμενίδης
**Παρμενίδης, ὁ** Parmenides of Elea, philosopher and poet, cited by Phaedrus and by Agathon (178b, 195c).

## Γοργίας
**Γοργίας, ὁ** Gorgias of Leontini, the rhetorician; Agathon's speech reminds Socrates of him (198c).

## Μαντινικός
**Μαντινικός, ή, όν** of Mantinea, a city in Arcadia: Diotima is "the woman of Mantinea" (201d, 211d).

## Περικλῆς
**Περικλῆς, ὁ** Pericles, the Athenian statesman, named by Alcibiades as a great speaker (215e, 221c).

## Λάχης
**Λάχης, ὁ** Laches, Athenian general, beside whom Socrates retreated from Delium (221a–b).

## Κυδαθηναιεύς
**Κυδαθηναιεύς, ὁ** a man of Cydathenaeum, an Athenian deme: so Aristodemus (173b).

## Μυρρινούσιος
**Μυρρινούσιος, ὁ** a man of Myrrhinus, an Athenian deme: so Phaedrus (176d).

## Μελανίππη
**Μελανίππη, ἡ** Melanippe, heroine of Euripides' Melanippe the Wise; Eryximachus quotes the play: "the tale is not mine" (177a).

## Ἀκουσίλεως
**Ἀκουσίλεως, ὁ** Acusilaus of Argos, early writer of genealogies of the gods, cited by Phaedrus (178b).

## Οἴαγρος
**Οἴαγρος, ὁ** Oeagrus, father of Orpheus (179d).

## Αἰσχύλος
**Αἰσχύλος, ὁ** Aeschylus, the tragic poet, cited by Phaedrus on Achilles and Patroclus (180a).

## Ἀριστογείτων
**Ἀριστογείτων, ὁ** Aristogeiton, who with his beloved Harmodius killed Hipparchus, brother of the tyrant Hippias, in 514 BC (182c).

## Ἡράκλειτος
**Ἡράκλειτος, ὁ** Heraclitus of Ephesus, philosopher, whose saying about harmony Eryximachus discusses (187a).

## ἑταιρίστρια
**ἑταιρίστρια, ἡ** a woman who loves women (191e).

## Ἀρκάς
**Ἀρκάς, άδος, ὁ** an Arcadian: Aristophanes alludes to the Arcadians split up by the Spartans (193a).

## Λακεδαιμόνιος
**Λακεδαιμόνιος, α, ον** Lacedaemonian, Spartan (193a).

## Ἰαπετός
**Ἰαπετός, ὁ** Iapetus, a Titan, father of Prometheus, named by Agathon among the old gods (195b).

## πτοίησις
**πτοίησις, ἡ** passionate excitement, fluttering (of desire) (206d).

## Λυκοῦργος
**Λυκοῦργος, ὁ** Lycurgus, the lawgiver of Sparta (209d).

## Βρασίδας
**Βρασίδας, ὁ** Brasidas, Spartan general, Alcibiades' example of a man like Achilles (221c).

## Χαρμίδης
**Χαρμίδης, ὁ** Charmides, son of Glaucon, one of the young men Socrates deceived by seeming their lover (222b).

## Εὐθύδημος
**Εὐθύδημος, ὁ** Euthydemus, son of Diocles, another such young man (222b).
