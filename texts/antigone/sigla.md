L = cod. Laur. 32. 9 (first half of eleventh century). r = one or more of the later MSS. This symbol is used where a more particular statement is unnecessary. ‘MSS.,’ after a reading, means that it is in all the MSS. known to the editor.

[Anagnosis: the lines above are Jebb's note at the head of his critical apparatus. The other manuscripts that the apparatus names he lists in his Introduction ("Manuscripts, editions and commentaries", § 1), in these words:]

The MSS., besides L, to which reference is made, are:—A (13th cent.), E (ascribed to 13th cent., but perhaps of the 14th), T (15th cent.), V (late 13th or early 14th), V2 (probably 14th), with the following 14th century MSS.,—V3, V4, Vat., Vat.b, L2, R. Some account of these has been given in the Introduction to the *Oedipus Tyrannus;* cp. also the Introd. to the *Oed. Col.* p. xlix. A few references are also made to an Augsburg MS. (Aug. b, 14th cent.), to Dresd. a (cod. 183, 14th cent.), and to M4 (Milan, Ambrosian Library, cod. C. 24 sup., 15th cent.).
