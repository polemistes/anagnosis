[Anagnosis: this page is the app's own explanation of the signs in the critical apparatus, put together from Lake's introduction to the Shepherd and his critical apparatus. It is not a page of Lake's edition.]

Lake's critical apparatus stands at the foot of each Greek page, in small type. Its items are numbered afresh on each page, and each answers a small raised numeral after a word of the text; the app drops the numbers and files each item under its verse. An item gives the reading of the text and the witnesses that have it, then the other readings with theirs ("τόπῳ ℵ\* L₁, τρόπῳ ℵᶜ AL₂(E)": ℵ in its first hand and the Vulgate Latin read τόπῳ; ℵ corrected, A, the Palatine Latin and, loosely, the Ethiopic read τρόπῳ). Where only the Latin or Ethiopic preserves a passage that A lacks, Lake translates it back into Greek and prints it in the text; the apparatus then says "retranslated from LE". He writes the readings of the versions in Latin (L) or gives them in English (E), and adds short remarks in English. The list of witnesses below is his (pp. 3-5), one paragraph to a witness, with what his apparatus adds; the abbreviations are explained from their use.

Where a misprint of Lake's in the Greek text is corrected, the apparatus says so: "μετανοίας [Print has: μενανοίας]". A misprint inside one of his apparatus items is corrected in the same way, the printed form following the item in the same bracket. The brackets beginning "Print has:" are the app's, not Lake's.

## The Greek manuscripts

**ℵ** — Codex Sinaiticus, containing Vis. I. i. 1 to Mandate IV. iii. 6, written in the fourth century, probably in Alexandria or the neighbourhood (Lake). The apparatus distinguishes its hands: **ℵ\*** the first hand, **ℵᶜ** a corrector, **ℵᵃ** another corrector (raised small letters as Lake prints them); once (Vis. 3.5.4) he prints **ℵ₂**.

**A** — Codex Athous, a manuscript of the fifteenth century, originally containing ten leaves, of which six are still in the convent of S. Gregory on Mt. Athos, three are at Leipsic, and one, the last, has been lost (Lake). Its text was copied on Mt. Athos by the forger Simonides and later collated, hastily, by Georgandas; both the copy and the collation are very inaccurate (Lake). For most of the book A is the only continuous Greek text; it breaks off after Similitude 9.30.2, where the loss of the last leaf begins (see the appendix).

**H** — a small fragment of a vellum manuscript, containing Sim. IV. 6-7 and V. 1-5, now in Hamburg (Lake).

## The papyri

Lake quotes the papyri as Pᵃᵐ, Pᵒˣ, etc. (p. 4). In the apparatus he prints their sigla with raised letters, not always alike; they are copied as printed.

**Pᵃᵐ**, once **Pᴬᵐʰ** — the Amherst papyrus CXC, containing Vis. I. 1, 2-3, 1; III. 12, 3 and 13, 3-4; Mand. XII. 1, 1 and 1, 3; Sim. IX. 2, 1-2 and 4-5; IX. 12, 2-3 and 5; IX. 17, 1 and 3; IX. 30, 1-2 and 3-4. Published by Grenfell and Hunt in *Amherst Papyri*, ii. The Greek of Sim. 9.30.3-4 in the appendix is from this papyrus.

**Pᵒˣʸ**, once **Pᵒˣ** — the Oxyrhynchus papyri: no. 404, containing Sim. X. 3, 2-5 (the Greek of Sim. 10.3.2-5 in the appendix), and no. 1172, containing Sim. II. 4-10. Published by Grenfell and Hunt in *Oxyrhynchus Papyri*, iii and ix.

**Pᴮᵉʳˡ** — the Berlin papyri 5513, containing Sim. II. 7-10 and Sim. IV. 2-5, and 6789, containing Sim. VIII. 1, 1-12. Published in *Berliner Klassiker Texte*, vi.

## The versions

**L₁** — the Vulgate Latin version, found in many manuscripts and published in the editio princeps of Faber, Paris, 1513. The appendix (Sim. 9.30.3 to the end) gives its text where the Greek is lost.

**L₂** — the Palatine Latin version, found in Cod. Vat. Palatin. 150 and published in the *Patrum Apostolicorum Opera* of von Gebhardt, Harnack and Zahn.

**L** — without qualification, the consensus of L₁ and L₂.

**E** — the Ethiopic translation found by d'Abbadie. Lake gives its readings in English ("E (now)"); twice (Mand. 11.9, Sim. 8.7.1) he prints **E₁**, unexplained.

**C** — fragments of a Sahidic Coptic version in papyri at Paris (Bibliothèque Nationale and the Louvre).

**F** — printed twice in the apparatus ("L₂F", Vis. 2.4.3; "AFL₁", Sim. 9.29.3) but not in Lake's list of witnesses; perhaps a misprint for E, but a clear F on both pages and nowhere explained. Kept as printed.

## The Fathers

**Clem.** — Clement of Alexandria; **Athan.**, **Ath.** — pseudo-Athanasius (Lake numbers two of his citations Ath.¹ and Ath.²); **Ant.** — Antiochus of Palestine. Lake: "Some evidence is also given by the numerous citations in Clement of Alexandria, pseudo-Athanasius, and Antiochus of Palestine." Two passages of the appendix are quotations preserved in Antiochus (Sim. 9.31.5-6; and, abbreviated, the paragraph of Sim. 9.32 whose note stands at 9.32.3, which Lake prints in the Latin).

## Signs and abbreviations

**Parentheses** round a siglum, as A(L) or ℵᶜAL₂(E), mean that the witness supports the reading loosely, not word for word. **om.** — omits; **add.** — adds; **edd.** — the editors; **cf.** — compare; **per homoiot.** — by homoioteleuton (an omission caused by two passages ending alike); **retranslated from LE** — the Greek of the text is Lake's rendering of the Latin and Ethiopic, A lacking it. A dash inside a lemma ("ἁμαρτίας μου—ἁμαρτίας μου om. per homoiot. ℵL₂") joins its first and last words; dots (". . .") do the same. Editors and scholars are named in full or nearly (Hilgenfeld, Funk, Hollenberg). Numerals written with a stroke over them (ε̅) are the manuscripts' numeral letters, an abbreviation with a stroke (ἀν̅ο̅υ̅) the manuscripts' contraction of a sacred word (ἀνθρώπου).
