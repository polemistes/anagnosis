# title: Glossary of names in the Shepherd
# lang: eng
# license: CC BY-SA 4.0

## Ῥόδη
**Ῥόδη, -ης, ἡ** Rhoda (the name means "rose", from ῥόδον), the lady at Rome to whom Hermas was sold as a slave: "He who brought me up sold me to a certain Rhoda at Rome. After many years I made her acquaintance again, and began to love her as a sister" (Lake; Vision 1.1.1); the Athos codex and the Vulgate Latin leave out her name and have "to a certain woman" (Lake's apparatus). Seeing her bathe in the Tiber he wishes he had such a wife (Vision 1.1.2); in his first vision she greets him out of heaven, unnamed, as "that woman whom I had desired", and accuses him of having sinned against her in his heart (Vision 1.1.4–8). Lake takes it that Hermas was born a slave and sold to her; Taylor that he became a freedman, and looked on her "first as a goddess and then as a sister" (Vision 1.1.7).

## Ῥώμη
**Ῥώμη, -ης, ἡ** Rome, where Hermas was sold to Rhoda (Vision 1.1.1); it is "this city" in which he is to read the little book with the elders of the church (Vision 2.4.3).

## Τίβερις
**Τίβερις, -ιος (acc. Τίβεριν), ὁ** the Tiber, the river of Rome, in which Hermas sees Rhoda bathing and helps her out: "the river Tiber" (Lake; Vision 1.1.2).

## Κῶμαι
**Κῶμαι, -ῶν, αἱ** Cumae, the old Greek town on the coast of Campania, home of the Sibyl. Hermas is "going to Cumae" when the spirit carries him off to his first vision (Lake; Vision 1.1.3), and again a year later for the second (Vision 2.1.1). The manuscripts spell it κώμας ("villages"); the editors read Κούμας, Cumae, and Lake thinks Cumae the meaning, though the spelling κώμας may be original, and the phrase could also mean going to the country for the summer (the Italian *villeggiatura*); the Latin has "the city of Ostia" in the first place and "the region of the Cumaeans" in the second (Lake's apparatus). Taylor notes that the neighbourhood of Cumae prepares for the Sibyl (on Vision 2.1.3), for whom Hermas takes the aged woman with the book (Vision 2.4.1).

## Ἑρμᾶς
**Ἑρμᾶς, -ᾶ (voc. Ἑρμᾶ), ὁ** Hermas, the narrator, who writes down the visions, mandates and similitudes. A slave sold to Rhoda at Rome (Vision 1.1.1), later a man of means with a wife, "your sister" (Vision 2.3.1), and children who have "betrayed their parents" (Vision 2.2.2). Rhoda and the aged woman, the Church, greet him by name (Vision 1.1.4, 1.2.2), and the woman calls him by name again (Vision 1.2.3); she calls him "Hermas the temperate, who abstains from every evil desire and is full of all simplicity and great innocence" (Vision 1.2.4); he is named again at Vision 1.4.3 ("Play the man, Hermas"), 2.2.2, 2.3.1, 3.1.6, 3.1.9, 3.8.11, and in the voice that warns "Do not be double-minded, Hermas" (Vision 4.1.4, 4.1.7). Taylor notes the Hermas greeted in Romans 16:14 and that the name may be assumed. Not to be confused with the god Hermes (Ἑρμῆς, -οῦ).

## Χριστός
**Χριστός, -οῦ, ὁ** Christ: the Lord has sworn that "those who have denied their Christ" are cut off from their life (Lake; Vision 2.2.8). The reading is that of the first hand of the Sinaitic codex; the corrector, the Athos codex and the Palatine Latin have "Lord", the Vulgate Latin "Son" (Lake's apparatus). Taylor holds that the name Christ is found nowhere else in the Shepherd and that here the abbreviation for Christ was written by mistake for that of Lord.

## Μάξιμος
**Μάξιμος, -ου, ὁ** Maximus (Latin, "greatest"), a Christian of Rome who has once denied the faith; Hermas is to warn him: "Behold, persecution is coming, if it seems good to you deny the faith again" (Lake; Vision 2.3.4). Taylor: "we have no further information".

## Ἐλδάδ
**Ἐλδάδ, ὁ** (indeclinable) Eldad, with Modat one of the two elders on whom the Spirit rested in the camp, "and they prophesied" (Numbers 11:26); Hermas quotes "The Lord is near those that turn to him ... as it is written in the Book of Eldad and Modat, who prophesied to the people in the wilderness" (Lake; Vision 2.3.4). The book is lost; Lake notes that it is listed among the apocrypha of the New Testament in the Athanasian Synopsis and the Stichometry of Nicephorus, and Taylor notes it is the only book Hermas quotes by name. The manuscripts vary: Ἐλὰδ καὶ Μωδάδ, Latin Heldam, Heldat (Lake's apparatus).

## Μωδάτ
**Μωδάτ, ὁ** (indeclinable) Modat (Medad in the English Bible, Μωδάδ in the Greek Old Testament), Eldad's companion in prophesying (Numbers 11:26); "the Book of Eldad and Modat" (Lake; Vision 2.3.4). See Ἐλδάδ.

## Σίβυλλα
**Σίβυλλα, -ης, ἡ** the Sibyl, the aged prophetess of the Greeks and Romans, whose seat was at Erythrae or Cumae and whose books of oracles were kept at Rome. Asked who the aged woman was from whom he received the little book, Hermas answers "The Sibyl", and is told "You are wrong, ... she is not"; she is the Church (Lake; Vision 2.4.1). Taylor: her age, her book and the neighbourhood of Cumae suggest the Sibyl; Hermas and Justin Martyr are the earliest Christian writers to name her.

## Ἐκκλησία
**Ἐκκλησία, -ας, ἡ** the Church, personified as the woman who appears to Hermas in the Visions, at first old and seated on a white chair with a book (Vision 1.2.2), then ever younger (Vision 3.10–13). A young man tells him who she is: "‘Who is she, then?’ I said. ‘The Church,’ he said. ... ‘she was created the first of all things ... and for her sake was the world established’" (Lake; Vision 2.4.1); she herself says "The tower which you see being built is myself, the Church" (Vision 3.3.3); Hermas recognises her again (Vision 4.2.2) and speaks of the visions shown him "by his holy Church" (Vision 4.1.3). In the Similitudes the Shepherd explains that the Holy Spirit spoke with Hermas "in the form of the Church" (Similitude 9.1.1–2). With a small letter (ἐκκλησία) the word is the ordinary congregation, as "the elders who are in charge of the church" (Vision 2.4.3).

## Κλήμης
**Κλήμης, -εντος, ὁ** Clement (Latin Clemens, "merciful"), to whom Hermas is to send one of the two copies of the little book: "Clement then shall send it to the cities abroad, for that is his duty" (Lake; Vision 2.4.3). Taylor takes him to be Clement, bishop of Rome, who wrote for the Roman church to the Corinthians; on this mention some have dated the book to 90–100 A.D. Origen allegorised him as the one who writes to souls outside carnal things.

## Γραπτή
**Γραπτή, -ῆς, ἡ** Grapte (the name means "written", from γράφω), who receives the other copy of the little book: "Grapte shall exhort the widows and orphans" (Lake; Vision 2.4.3). Taylor: the name is uncommon, and she is supposed to have been a deaconess; Origen allegorised her as "the bare letter", the elementary teaching.

## Πίστις
**Πίστις, -εως, ἡ** Faith, personified: the first of the seven women who hold up the tower, "who is clasping her hands" (Lake; Vision 3.8.3): "Through her the chosen of God are saved"; the mother of the rest, "From Faith is born Continence" (Vision 3.8.4, 3.8.7). Again the first of the four stronger maidens at the corners of the gate (Similitude 9.15.2). Taylor: Faith and Love stand first and last, as in 1 Corinthians 13:13.

## Ἐγκράτεια
**Ἐγκράτεια, -ας, ἡ** Continence, self-control (ἐγκρατής, "having power over oneself"), personified: the second woman, "who is girded and looks like a man ... she is the daughter of Faith" (Lake; Vision 3.8.4, 3.8.7); the second of the four stronger maidens, "Temperance" (Lake; Similitude 9.15.2).

## Ἁπλότης
**Ἁπλότης, -ητος, ἡ** Simplicity, single-mindedness (ἁπλοῦς, "single"), personified: one of the daughters, born of Continence (Lake; Vision 3.8.5, 3.8.7); one of the eight maidens between the corners (Similitude 9.15.2).

## Ἐπιστήμη
**Ἐπιστήμη, -ης, ἡ** Knowledge, personified: one of the daughters in Vision 3.8.5; born of Reverence, and the mother of Love (Lake; Vision 3.8.7).

## Ἀκακία
**Ἀκακία, -ας, ἡ** Innocence, guilelessness (ἄκακος, "without evil"), personified: one of the daughters, born of Simplicity (Lake "Innocence"; Vision 3.8.5, 3.8.7); one of the eight maidens, "Guilelessness" (Lake; Similitude 9.15.2).

## Σεμνότης
**Σεμνότης, -ητος, ἡ** Reverence, gravity (σεμνός, "revered, grave"), personified: one of the daughters, born of Innocence (Lake "Reverence"; Vision 3.8.5, 3.8.7). Taylor: it might also be rendered gravity or reverence (Latin *modestia*).

## Ἀγάπη
**Ἀγάπη, -ης, ἡ** Love, personified: the last of the seven women, born of Knowledge (Lake; Vision 3.8.5, 3.8.7), and the last of the twelve maidens (Similitude 9.15.2).

## καμπανός
**καμπανός, -ή, -όν** Campanian: ἡ ὁδὸς ἡ καμπανή, the Via Campana, the road by which Hermas goes out to his field before the vision of the beast: "I was going into the country by the Via Campana. The place is about ten furlongs from the public road" (Lake; Vision 4.1.2). Taylor: some take it for the country road on the right bank of the Tiber (Zahn, Funk).

## Θεγρί
**Θεγρί, ὁ** (indeclinable) Thegri, the angel set over the wild beasts, whom the Lord sent to shut the mouth of the great beast that met Hermas on the road: "the Lord sent his angel, whose name is Thegri, who is over the beast, and shut his mouth that he should not hurt you" (Lake; Vision 4.2.4, after Daniel 6:22). Lake: no other mention of this angel is found in Jewish or Christian literature, and no suitable meaning has been suggested for the name; Rendel Harris proposed reading Segri, "shutter", from the Hebrew *sagar*, "to shut", the word in Daniel 6:22, and Taylor adopts it.

## Μιχαήλ
**Μιχαήλ, ὁ** (indeclinable) Michael (Hebrew, "who is like God?"), the archangel: in the parable of the willow the "great and glorious angel is Michael, who has power over this people and governs them; for this is he who put the law into the hearts of those who believe" (Lake; Similitude 8.3.3); he looks to see whether those to whom he gave it have kept it. Lake notes that in Similitude 9 the glorious angel is the Son of God; Taylor compares Michael as giver of the law in Daniel 10 and 12 and Acts 7:53.

## Ἀρκαδία
**Ἀρκαδία, -ας, ἡ** Arcadia, the mountain country of the central Peloponnese: the Shepherd "took me away to Arcadia, to a breast-shaped mountain", from which Hermas sees the great plain ringed by twelve mountains (Lake; Similitude 9.1.4). Lake: all the authorities have Arcadia, though it plays no further part in the story; Zahn emends to Aricia, near Rome. Taylor: it has been inferred from this that Arcadia was Hermas' native place.

## Δύναμις
**Δύναμις, -εως, ἡ** Power, personified: the third of the four stronger maidens at the corners of the gate (Lake; Similitude 9.15.2).

## Μακροθυμία
**Μακροθυμία, -ας, ἡ** Long-suffering, patience (μακρόθυμος, "slow to anger"), personified: the fourth of the stronger maidens (Lake; Similitude 9.15.2).

## Ἁγνεία
**Ἁγνεία, -ας, ἡ** Purity (ἁγνός, "pure"), personified: one of the eight maidens between the corners, "Holiness" (Lake; Similitude 9.15.2).

## Ἱλαρότης
**Ἱλαρότης, -ητος, ἡ** Cheerfulness (ἱλαρός, "cheerful"), personified: one of the eight maidens, "Joyfulness" (Lake; Similitude 9.15.2).

## Ἀλήθεια
**Ἀλήθεια, -ας, ἡ** Truth, personified: one of the eight maidens (Lake; Similitude 9.15.2).

## Σύνεσις
**Σύνεσις, -εως, ἡ** Understanding, personified: one of the eight maidens (Lake; Similitude 9.15.2).

## Ὁμόνοια
**Ὁμόνοια, -ας, ἡ** Concord, being of one mind (ὁμόνοος), personified: one of the eight maidens (Lake; Similitude 9.15.2).

## Ἀπιστία
**Ἀπιστία, -ας, ἡ** Unbelief, personified: the first of the four stronger women in black raiment (Lake; Similitude 9.15.3), the opposite of Faith.

## Ἀκρασία
**Ἀκρασία, -ας, ἡ** Incontinence, lack of self-control (ἀκρατής), personified: the second of the women in black, "Impurity" (Lake; Similitude 9.15.3), the opposite of Continence.

## Ἀπείθεια
**Ἀπείθεια, -ας, ἡ** Disobedience, personified: the third of the women in black (Lake; Similitude 9.15.3).

## Ἀπάτη
**Ἀπάτη, -ης, ἡ** Deceit, personified: the fourth of the women in black (Lake; Similitude 9.15.3).

## Λύπη
**Λύπη, -ης, ἡ** Grief, personified: the first of the eight women in black who follow the four (Lake; Similitude 9.15.3); compare the teaching on grief in Mandate 10.

## Πονηρία
**Πονηρία, -ας, ἡ** Wickedness, personified: one of the followers in black (Lake; Similitude 9.15.3).

## Ἀσέλγεια
**Ἀσέλγεια, -ας, ἡ** Licentiousness, wantonness, personified: one of the followers in black (Lake; Similitude 9.15.3).

## Ὀξυχολία
**Ὀξυχολία, -ας, ἡ** Ill temper, quickness to anger (ὀξύς, "sharp", and χολή, "bile"), personified: one of the followers in black, "Bitterness" (Lake; Similitude 9.15.3); as a common noun the subject of Mandate 5.

## Ψεῦδος
**Ψεῦδος, -ους, τό** Lying, falsehood, personified: one of the followers in black (Lake; Similitude 9.15.3).

## Ἀφροσύνη
**Ἀφροσύνη, -ης, ἡ** Foolishness, personified: one of the followers in black (Lake; Similitude 9.15.3).

## Καταλαλιά
**Καταλαλιά, -ᾶς, ἡ** Evil-speaking, slander (καταλαλέω, "speak against"), personified: one of the followers in black (Lake; Similitude 9.15.3).

## Μῖσος
**Μῖσος, -ους, τό** Hate, personified: the last of the followers in black: "The servant of God who bears these names shall see the Kingdom of God, but shall not enter into it" (Lake; Similitude 9.15.3).
