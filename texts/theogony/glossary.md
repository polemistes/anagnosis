# title: Glossary of names and rare words in the Theogony
# lang: eng

## Περμησσός
**Περμησσός, -οῖο (-οῦ), ὁ** the Permessus, a stream of Helicon in which the Muses wash before their dances (Th. 5).

## Ὀλμειός
**Ὀλμειός, -οῦ, ὁ** the Olmeius, a holy stream of Helicon in which the Muses wash before their dances (Th. 6).

## Ἐλευθήρ
**Ἐλευθήρ, -ῆρος** Eleuther, a place over whose hills Mnemosyne reigns (Th. 54); its site is uncertain, and some take it to be Eleutherae in Boeotia (Paley).

## Ἰαπετός
**Ἰαπετός, -οῦ, ὁ** Iapetus, a Titan, son of Earth and Heaven (Th. 134), named among the gods the Muses sing (Th. 18); he took Clymene, daughter of Ocean, to wife, and she bore him Atlas, Menoetius, Prometheus and Epimetheus (Th. 507–511); Atlas is "the son of Iapetus" at Th. 746.

## Κρῖος
**Κρῖος, -ου, ὁ** Crius, a Titan, son of Earth and Heaven (Th. 134); with Eurybia he begot Astraeus, Pallas and Perses (Th. 375–377).

## Κητώ
**Κητώ, -οῦς, ἡ** Ceto, "fair-cheeked", daughter of Sea (Pontus) and Earth (Th. 238); to Phorcys she bore the Graiae and the Gorgons (Th. 270–276) and, youngest, the snake that guards the golden apples (Th. 333–336).

## Εὐρυβίη
**Εὐρυβίη, -ης, ἡ** Eurybia, daughter of Sea (Pontus) and Earth, "who has a heart of flint within her" (Th. 239); to Crius she bore Astraeus, Pallas and Perses (Th. 375–377).

## Πλωτώ
**Πλωτώ, -οῦς, ἡ** Ploto, a Nereid, daughter of Nereus and Doris (Th. 243).

## Εὐκράντη
**Εὐκράντη, -ης, ἡ** Eucrante, a Nereid (Th. 243).

## Σαώ
**Σαώ, -οῦς, ἡ** Sao, a Nereid (Th. 243).

## Εὐδώρη
**Εὐδώρη, -ης, ἡ** Eudora, a Nereid (Th. 244), and an Oceanid, daughter of Ocean and Tethys (Th. 360).

## Γλαύκη
**Γλαύκη, -ης, ἡ** Glauce, a Nereid (Th. 244).

## Σπειώ
**Σπειώ, -οῦς, ἡ** Speo, a Nereid (Th. 245).

## Θόη
**Θόη, -ης, ἡ** Thoe, a Nereid (Th. 245), and an Oceanid, daughter of Ocean and Tethys (Th. 354).

## Πασιθέη
**Πασιθέη, -ης, ἡ** Pasithea, a Nereid (Th. 246).

## Εὐλιμένη
**Εὐλιμένη, -ης, ἡ** Eulimene, a Nereid (Th. 247).

## Ἀγαυή
**Ἀγαυή, -ῆς, ἡ** Agaue, a Nereid (Th. 247); also Agave, daughter of Cadmus and Harmonia (Th. 976).

## Πρωτώ
**Πρωτώ, -οῦς, ἡ** Proto, a Nereid (Th. 248).

## Φέρουσα
**Φέρουσα, -ης, ἡ** Pherusa, a Nereid (Th. 248).

## Δυναμένη
**Δυναμένη, -ης, ἡ** Dynamene, a Nereid (Th. 248).

## Νησαίη
**Νησαίη, -ης, ἡ** Nisaea, a Nereid (Th. 249).

## Ἀκταίη
**Ἀκταίη, -ης, ἡ** Actaea, a Nereid (Th. 249).

## Πρωτομέδεια
**Πρωτομέδεια, -ης, ἡ** Protomedea, a Nereid (Th. 249).

## Πανόπεια
**Πανόπεια, -ης, ἡ** Panopea, a Nereid (Th. 250).

## Γαλάτεια
**Γαλάτεια, -ης, ἡ** Galatea, "comely", a Nereid (Th. 250).

## Ἱπποθόη
**Ἱπποθόη, -ης, ἡ** Hippothoe, a Nereid (Th. 251).

## Ἱππονόη
**Ἱππονόη, -ης, ἡ** Hipponoe, "rosy-armed", a Nereid (Th. 251).

## Ἠιόνη
**Ἠιόνη, -ης, ἡ** Eione, a Nereid (Th. 255).

## Ἁλιμήδη
**Ἁλιμήδη, -ης, ἡ** Alimede, "rich-crowned", a Nereid (Th. 255).

## Γλαυκονόμη
**Γλαυκονόμη, -ης, ἡ** Glauconome, "fond of laughter", a Nereid (Th. 256).

## Ληαγόρη
**Ληαγόρη, -ης, ἡ** Leagore, a Nereid (Th. 257).

## Εὐαγόρη
**Εὐαγόρη, -ης, ἡ** Euagore, a Nereid (Th. 257).

## Λαομέδεια
**Λαομέδεια, -ης, ἡ** Laomedea, a Nereid (Th. 257).

## Πουλυνόη
**Πουλυνόη, -ης, ἡ** Polynoe, a Nereid (Th. 258).

## Αὐτονόη
**Αὐτονόη, -ης, ἡ** Autonoe: (1) a Nereid (Th. 258); (2) a daughter of Cadmus and Harmonia, whom Aristaeus wedded (Th. 977).

## Λυσιάνασσα
**Λυσιάνασσα, -ης, ἡ** Lysianassa, a Nereid (Th. 258).

## Εὐάρνη
**Εὐάρνη, -ης, ἡ** Euarne, a Nereid, "lovely of shape and without blemish of form" (Th. 259).

## Ψαμάθη
**Ψαμάθη, -ης, ἡ** Psamathe, a Nereid (Th. 260); loved by Aeacus, she bore Phocus (Th. 1003–1005).

## Μενίππη
**Μενίππη, -ης, ἡ** Menippe, a Nereid (Th. 260).

## Νησώ
**Νησώ, -οῦς, ἡ** Neso, a Nereid (Th. 261).

## Εὐπόμπη
**Εὐπόμπη, -ης, ἡ** Eupompe, a Nereid (Th. 261).

## Θεμιστώ
**Θεμιστώ, -οῦς, ἡ** Themisto, a Nereid (Th. 261).

## Προνόη
**Προνόη, -ης, ἡ** Pronoe, a Nereid (Th. 261).

## Ἠλέκτρη
**Ἠλέκτρη, -ης, ἡ** Electra, daughter of deep-flowing Ocean, wife of Thaumas and mother of Iris and the Harpies (Th. 265–267); named among the Oceanids (Th. 349).

## Πεμφρηδώ
**Πεμφρηδώ, -οῦς, ἡ** Pemphredo, "well-clad", one of the Graiae, daughters of Phorcys and Ceto (Th. 273).

## Σθεννώ
**Σθεννώ, -οῦς, ἡ** Sthenno, one of the three Gorgons, daughters of Phorcys and Ceto; with Euryale she is undying and ageless (Th. 276–277).

## Εὐρυάλη
**Εὐρυάλη, -ης, ἡ** Euryale, one of the three Gorgons, undying and ageless like Sthenno (Th. 276–277).

## Μέδουσα
**Μέδουσα, -ης, ἡ** Medusa, the one mortal Gorgon (Th. 276–277); the Dark-haired One (Poseidon) lay with her, and when Perseus cut off her head Chrysaor and the horse Pegasus sprang forth (Th. 278–281).

## Καλλιρόη
**Καλλιρόη, -ης, ἡ** Callirrhoe, daughter of Ocean, who bore three-headed Geryones to Chrysaor (Th. 288, 979–981); named among the Oceanids (Th. 351).

## Ἐρυθεία
**Ἐρυθεία, -ης, ἡ** Erythea, the sea-girt island beyond Ocean where Heracles killed Geryones for his oxen (Th. 290, 983).

## Τίρυνς
**Τίρυνς, -υνθος, ἡ** Tiryns, "holy", to which Heracles drove the oxen of Geryones (Th. 292).

## Ὄρθος
**Ὄρθος, -ου, ὁ** Orthus, the hound of Geryones, whom Heracles killed (Th. 293); a son of Echidna and Typhaon (Th. 309); with Echidna he begot the Sphinx and the Nemean lion (Th. 326–327).

## Εὐρυτίων
**Εὐρυτίων, -ωνος, ὁ** Eurytion, the herdsman of Geryones, whom Heracles killed in the dim stead beyond Ocean (Th. 293–294).

## Ἄριμοι
**Ἄριμοι, -ων, οἱ** the Arimi, or Arima (εἰν Ἀρίμοισιν), where grim Echidna keeps guard beneath the earth (Th. 304).

## Λερναῖος
**Λερναῖος, -η, -ον** of Lerna; the Hydra of Lerna, offspring of Echidna, nursed by Hera and slain by Heracles (Th. 313–318).

## Ἀμφιτρυωνιάδης
**Ἀμφιτρυωνιάδης, -ου, ὁ** son of Amphitryon, i.e. of his house: Heracles (Th. 317).

## Ἰόλαος
**Ἰόλαος, -ου, ὁ** Iolaus, "warlike", who with Heracles destroyed the Hydra of Lerna (Th. 317).

## Βελλεροφόντης
**Βελλεροφόντης, -ου, ὁ** Bellerophon, who with Pegasus slew the Chimaera (Th. 325).

## Ἀπέσας
**Ἀπέσας, -αντος, ὁ** Apesas, a mountain by Nemea over which the Nemean lion held power, with Tretus (Th. 331).

## Ἀλφειός
**Ἀλφειός, -οῦ, ὁ** the Alpheus, a river of the Peloponnese, one of the rivers Tethys bore to Ocean (Th. 338).

## Ῥῆσος
**Ῥῆσος, -ου, ὁ** the Rhesus, a river of the Troad, one of the rivers Tethys bore to Ocean (Th. 340).

## Νέσσος
**Νέσσος, -ου, ὁ** the Nessus, one of the rivers Tethys bore to Ocean (Th. 341).

## Ῥόδιος
**Ῥόδιος, -ου, ὁ** the Rhodius, a river of the Troad, one of the rivers Tethys bore to Ocean (Th. 341).

## Ἁλιάκμων
**Ἁλιάκμων, -ονος, ὁ** the Haliacmon, a river of Macedonia, one of the rivers Tethys bore to Ocean (Th. 341).

## Γρήνικος
**Γρήνικος, -ου, ὁ** the Granicus, a river of the Troad, one of the rivers Tethys bore to Ocean (Th. 342).

## Αἴσηπος
**Αἴσηπος, -ου, ὁ** the Aesepus, a river of the Troad, one of the rivers Tethys bore to Ocean (Th. 342).

## Πηνειός
**Πηνειός, -οῦ, ὁ** the Peneus, the river of Thessaly, one of the rivers Tethys bore to Ocean (Th. 343).

## Ἕρμος
**Ἕρμος, -ου, ὁ** the Hermus, a river of Lydia, one of the rivers Tethys bore to Ocean (Th. 343).

## Κάικος
**Κάικος, -ου, ὁ** the Caicus, "fair-flowing", a river of Mysia, one of the rivers Tethys bore to Ocean (Th. 343).

## Λάδων
**Λάδων, -ωνος, ὁ** the Ladon, a river of Arcadia, one of the rivers Tethys bore to Ocean (Th. 344).

## Εὔηνος
**Εὔηνος, -ου, ὁ** the Euenus, a river of Aetolia, one of the rivers Tethys bore to Ocean (Th. 345).

## Ἄρδησκος
**Ἄρδησκος, -ου, ὁ** the Ardescus, one of the rivers Tethys bore to Ocean (Th. 345).

## Ἀδμήτη
**Ἀδμήτη, -ης, ἡ** Admete, an Oceanid, one of the daughters of Ocean and Tethys who with Apollo and the Rivers have youths in their keeping (Th. 346–349).

## Ἰάνθη
**Ἰάνθη, -ης, ἡ** Ianthe, an Oceanid, daughter of Ocean and Tethys (Th. 349).

## Πρυμνώ
**Πρυμνώ, -οῦς, ἡ** Prymno, an Oceanid, daughter of Ocean and Tethys (Th. 350).

## Ἱππώ
**Ἱππώ, -οῦς, ἡ** Hippo, an Oceanid, daughter of Ocean and Tethys (Th. 351).

## Κλυμένη
**Κλυμένη, -ης, ἡ** Clymene, an Oceanid, daughter of Ocean and Tethys (Th. 351); wife of Iapetus, to whom she bore Atlas, Menoetius, Prometheus and Epimetheus (Th. 507–511).

## Ῥόδεια
**Ῥόδεια, -ης, ἡ** Rhodea, an Oceanid, daughter of Ocean and Tethys (Th. 351).

## Ζευξώ
**Ζευξώ, -οῦς, ἡ** Zeuxo, an Oceanid, daughter of Ocean and Tethys (Th. 352).

## Κλυτίη
**Κλυτίη, -ης, ἡ** Clytie, an Oceanid, daughter of Ocean and Tethys (Th. 352).

## Πασιθόη
**Πασιθόη, -ης, ἡ** Pasithoe, an Oceanid, daughter of Ocean and Tethys (Th. 352).

## Πληξαύρη
**Πληξαύρη, -ης, ἡ** Plexaura, an Oceanid, daughter of Ocean and Tethys (Th. 353).

## Γαλαξαύρη
**Γαλαξαύρη, -ης, ἡ** Galaxaura, an Oceanid, daughter of Ocean and Tethys (Th. 353).

## Πολυδώρη
**Πολυδώρη, -ης, ἡ** Polydora, "handsome", an Oceanid, daughter of Ocean and Tethys (Th. 354).

## Κερκηίς
**Κερκηίς, -ίδος, ἡ** Cerceis, "lovely of form", an Oceanid, daughter of Ocean and Tethys (Th. 355).

## Πλουτώ
**Πλουτώ, -οῦς, ἡ** Pluto, "soft-eyed", an Oceanid, daughter of Ocean and Tethys (Th. 355).

## Ἰάνειρα
**Ἰάνειρα, -ης, ἡ** Ianeira, an Oceanid, daughter of Ocean and Tethys (Th. 356).

## Ἀκάστη
**Ἀκάστη, -ης, ἡ** Acaste, an Oceanid, daughter of Ocean and Tethys (Th. 356).

## Πετραία
**Πετραίη, -ης, ἡ** Petraea, "the fair", an Oceanid, daughter of Ocean and Tethys (Th. 357).

## Μενεσθώ
**Μενεσθώ, -οῦς, ἡ** Menestho, an Oceanid, daughter of Ocean and Tethys (Th. 357).

## Εὐρυνόμη
**Εὐρυνόμη, -ης, ἡ** Eurynome, an Oceanid, daughter of Ocean and Tethys (Th. 358); "beautiful in form", she bore Zeus the three Graces, Aglaea, Euphrosyne and Thaleia (Th. 907–909).

## Τελεστώ
**Τελεστώ, -οῦς, ἡ** Telesto, "saffron-clad", an Oceanid, daughter of Ocean and Tethys (Th. 358).

## Ἀμφιρώ
**Ἀμφιρώ, -οῦς, ἡ** Amphirho, an Oceanid, daughter of Ocean and Tethys (Th. 360).

## Ὠκυρόη
**Ὠκυρόη, -ης, ἡ** Ocyrrhoe, an Oceanid, daughter of Ocean and Tethys (Th. 360).

## Ἀστραῖος
**Ἀστραῖος, -ου, ὁ** Astraeus, son of Crius and Eurybia (Th. 376); to him Eos bore the winds Zephyrus, Boreas and Notus (Th. 378–380).

## Ἀστερίη
**Ἀστερίη, -ης, ἡ** Asteria, "of happy name", daughter of Phoebe and Coeus, sister of Leto; Perses led her to his house as his wife, and she bore Hecate (Th. 404–411).

## Λύκτος
**Λύκτος, -ου, ἡ** Lyctus, a town in Crete, to which Rhea was sent to bear Zeus, and where Earth first carried him and hid him in a cave (Th. 477, 481–483).

## Μενοίτιος
**Μενοίτιος, -ου, ὁ** Menoetius, son of Iapetus and Clymene (Th. 510); for his outrage and pride Zeus struck him with a thunderbolt and sent him down to Erebus (Th. 514–516).

## Ἀλκμήνη
**Ἀλκμήνη, -ης, ἡ** Alcmena, "shapely-ankled", mother of Heracles by Zeus (Th. 526, 943–944, 950).

## Ἰαπετιονίδης
**Ἰαπετιονίδης, -ου, ὁ** son of Iapetus: Prometheus (Th. 528, 543, 559, 614).

## Μηκώνη
**Μηκώνη, -ης, ἡ** Mecone, where gods and mortal men had their dispute, and Prometheus divided the ox to deceive Zeus (Th. 535–536).

## περιοίχομαι
**περιοίχομαι** (verb; περοίχεται, with the -ι- elided) go round, run round; τεῖχος δὲ περοίχεται ἀμφοτέρωθεν, a wall runs round it on every side, of the prison of the Titans (Th. 733).

## Αἴτνη
**Αἴτνη, -ης, ἡ** Aetna, the mountain in Sicily: Rzach reads Ἀίτνης at Th. 860, where the flame of the thunderstruck Typhoeus shot forth in its glens; Evelyn-White translates another reading (ἀιδνῆς), "the dim rugged glens of the mount".

## Μήδεια
**Μήδεια, -ης, ἡ** Medea, daughter of Aeetes and Idyia (Th. 961); the son of Aeson (Jason) led her away and took her to Iolcus as his wife, and she bore him Medeus (Th. 992–1001).

## Ἀριάδνη
**Ἀριάδνη, -ης, ἡ** Ariadne, "brown-haired", daughter of Minos, whom Dionysus made his wife and Zeus made deathless and unageing (Th. 947–949).

## Αἰήτης
**Αἰήτης, -αο and -εω, ὁ** Aeetes the king, son of Helios and Perseis, brother of Circe (Th. 956–957); husband of Idyia and father of Medea (Th. 958–962), whom Jason led away from him (Th. 992–994).

## Ἰασίων
**Ἰασίων, -ωνος, ὁ** Iasion, the hero with whom Demeter lay in a thrice-ploughed fallow in Crete, and bore Plutus (Th. 969–971).

## Ἠμαθίων
**Ἠμαθίων, -ωνος, ὁ** Emathion, "the lord", son of Eos and Tithonus, brother of Memnon (Th. 984–985).

## Αἰσονίδης
**Αἰσονίδης, -ου, ὁ** son of Aeson: Jason (Th. 993, 999).

## Πελίης
**Πελίης, -αο, ὁ** Pelias, the great king, overbearing and outrageous, who set Jason his many grievous labours (Th. 995–996).

## Ἰαωλκός
**Ἰαωλκός, -οῦ, ἡ** Iolcus (Ἰωλκός), to which Jason came home with Medea on his swift ship (Th. 997).

## Ἰήσων
**Ἰήσων, -ονος, ὁ** Iason (Jason), "shepherd of the people", husband of Medea and father of Medeus (Th. 1000–1001).

## Μήδειος
**Μήδειος, -ου, ὁ** Medeus, son of Jason and Medea, brought up in the mountains by Cheiron (Th. 1001–1002).

## Φιλυρίδης
**Φιλυρίδης, -ου, ὁ** son of Philyra: Cheiron (Th. 1002).

## Φῶκος
**Φῶκος, -ου, ὁ** Phocus, son of Aeacus and the Nereid Psamathe (Th. 1004–1005).

## Αἰακός
**Αἰακός, -οῦ, ὁ** Aeacus, who loved the Nereid Psamathe and begot Phocus (Th. 1003–1005).

## Ἀγχίσης
**Ἀγχίσης, -ου, ὁ** Anchises, the hero with whom Cytherea (Aphrodite) lay on the peaks of Ida, and bore Aeneas (Th. 1008–1010).

## Λατῖνος
**Λατῖνος, -ου, ὁ** Latinus, "faultless and strong", son of Circe and Odysseus, brother of Agrius; they ruled over the Tyrsenians (Th. 1011–1016).

## Ναυσίθοος
**Ναυσίθοος, -ου, ὁ** Nausithous, son of Odysseus and Calypso (Th. 1017–1018).

## Ναυσίνοος
**Ναυσίνοος, -ου, ὁ** Nausinous, son of Odysseus and Calypso (Th. 1017–1018).
