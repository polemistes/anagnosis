# source: A. Rzach, Hesiodi carmina, Leipzig, Teubner, 1908, "Notae librorum" (p. 2) and Praefatio (pp. III-V); transcribed from the Internet Archive scan hesiodicarminare00hesi

Rzach's critical apparatus stands at the foot of each page of the text, in Latin. Each item begins with the number of the line it concerns; where a reading of the text is not that of all the manuscripts, the apparatus gives the others and their sigla, and it names the scholars whose corrections or deletions are recorded ("215 ἧς scripsi, αἷς libri": Rzach's own correction; the manuscripts read αἷς). Lines he holds to be interpolated are printed in square brackets in the text; a dagger † marks a passage he thinks corrupt. In the preface he says that the manuscripts and the other evidence are described in full in the preface of his larger edition of 1902, and repeats here only what matters most about the best manuscripts and papyri.

## The papyri

**A** — papyrus, Paris, Bibliothèque nationale, suppl. gr. 1099, 4th-5th century.

**B** — fragment of a papyrus, London, British Museum, CLIX, about the 4th century.

**R** — fragments of a papyrus of the Archduke Rainer, now in the Imperial Library, Vienna, L. P. 21-29, 4th century.

Where Rzach gives a papyrus's reading letter for letter, the apparatus prints it in capitals as the papyrus has it, with the lunate sigma (C), and puts in angle brackets ⟨ ⟩ the letters the papyrus has lost.

## The manuscripts

**C** — fragments in a manuscript in Paris, Bibliothèque nationale, suppl. gr. 663, 12th century (once on Mount Athos).

**D** — Florence, Laurentian Library, XXXII 16, 13th century (1281). In Rzach's judgement the best of the manuscripts that hold the whole poem, the leader of the better class (Ω).

**E** — Florence, Laurentian Library, Conventi soppressi 158, 14th century.

**F** — Paris, Bibliothèque nationale, 2833, 15th century.

**G** — Rome, Vatican Library, 915, 14th century.

**H** — Paris, Bibliothèque nationale, 2772, 14th century.

**I** — Florence, Laurentian Library, XXXI 32, 15th century.

**K** — Venice, Marcian Library, IX 6, 14th century.

**L** — Paris, Bibliothèque nationale, 2708, 15th century.

## Classes and families

**Ω** — the better class, of three families: **Ωa** codices C and D, **Ωb** codices E and F, **Ωc** codices G, H and I. **Ωbc** means families b and c together.

**Ψ** — the second class, codices K and L; its text is somewhat worse, but not to be neglected.

**Φ** — the third class, of the Works and Days only (a Byzantine recension); named here only where the apparatus compares that poem.

**x** — contaminated manuscripts of a recension "x": Rome, Casanatense 356 (14th century), Vatican 1392 (15th century), Florence, Laurentian LXXXXI sup. 10 (15th century).

**t** — the recension of Demetrius Triclinius: Venice, Marcian 464 (1316-1320), and the manuscripts derived from it.

## Words and signs in the apparatus

**codd.**, **libri** — the manuscripts (all, or all but those named). **schol.** — the scholia, the ancient commentary. **m¹**, **m²** — the first and a second hand of a manuscript; **m. rec.** a later hand; **m. al.** another hand. **γρ.** (γράφεται) — a variant written in a manuscript as another reading. **superscr.** — written above the line; **in marg.** — in the margin; **in ras.** — over an erasure; **e corr.** — made out of another reading by correction; **eras.** — erased; **///** — erased letters. **om.** — leaves out. **scripsi** — Rzach's own correction; **coni.** — a scholar's conjecture; **corr.** — corrected (by the scholar named). **eiecit**, **seclusit**, **proscripsit**, **del.** — a scholar has declared the line(s) spurious. **post 26** — after line 26. **sq.**, **sqq.** — and the following line(s). **cf.** — compare. **recensio x**, **recensio t** — see above.

Other witnesses named in the apparatus are ancient and Byzantine authors who quote the Theogony (Stobaeus, Plutarch, Aristides, Lucian, Ioannes Galenus, Tzetzes and others), and the editors and critics whose readings are recorded (Goettling, Hermann, Guyet, Peppmüller, Paley, Fick, Flach, Schoemann and others).
