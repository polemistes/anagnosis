Sidgwick's critical apparatus, at the foot of each page of the text, gives the reading of the manuscripts wherever they differ among themselves or are all wrong, except where the correction is obvious and generally accepted. A reading in the text that is a conjecture and stands in no manuscript is marked there with an asterisk, which this text leaves out; the apparatus then gives the manuscripts' reading and the scholar the correction is due to: "69. ὑποκλαίων MSS. corr. Cas." means that the manuscripts read ὑποκλαίων and that the reading of the text is Casaubon's. Where neither the manuscripts nor any proposed reading satisfies him, he marks the passage with a dagger (†ἐμήν, line 14).

## The manuscripts

**M.** — the Mediceus, Florence (Laurentian Library), parchment, 10th or 11th century; Agamemnon 1-310 and 1067-1159 only, the middle and end being lost.

**G.** — the Guelferbytanus, Wolfenbüttel, paper, 15th century; the same parts as M., of which it is clearly a copy.

**Ma.** — the Marcianus, Florence, silk, 15th century; also a copy of M., with the same parts.

**B.** — the Bessarionis, Venice (St. Mark's), paper, about the 13th century; Agamemnon 1-348, probably copied from M. before the loss of its central leaves.

**V.** — the Venetian (Venetus B), Venice (St. Mark's), parchment, 13th century (15th, according to Wecklein); Agamemnon 1-45 and 1095 to the end.

**Fl.** — the Florentinus, Florence (Laurentian Library), paper, 14th century; the Agamemnon entire.

**Fa.** — the Farnesianus, Naples, 14th century; the Agamemnon entire, in the revision of Demetrius Triclinius.

**MSS.** — all the manuscripts that have the passage. Sidgwick's table of what each part of the play rests on: 1-45 M. G. Ma. B. V. Fl. Fa.; 46-311 M. G. Ma. B. Fl. Fa.; 312-348 B. Fl. Fa.; 349-1066 Fl. Fa.; 1067-1094 M. G. Ma. Fl. Fa.; 1095-1159 M. G. Ma. V. Fl. Fa.; 1160 to the end V. Fl. Fa.
