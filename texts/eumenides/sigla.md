Sidgwick's critical apparatus, at the foot of each page of the text, gives the manuscripts' reading of all the doubtful places, wherever one or all the manuscripts are wrong, except where the correction is obvious and certain, and even then wherever anybody could attach any importance to the variation. Where the reading of the text stands in a manuscript, the others' variants are given in the apparatus without a mark in the text ("22. δʼ ἔχει V. Fl. Fa. text M.": the Venetian, Florentine and Farnese manuscripts read δʼ ἔχει, the Medicean ἔχει only). A reading in the text that is a conjecture is marked there with an asterisk, which this text leaves out, and the apparatus gives the manuscripts' reading and the corrector ("27. πλείστους MSS. corr. Turn.": the manuscripts have πλείστους, which Turnèbe corrected to Πλειστοῦ). Where neither the manuscripts nor the proposed corrections satisfy him, the passage is marked with a dagger (352, 364, 387, 635 and others).

## The manuscripts

### The Medicean group

**M.** — the Mediceus, Florence (Laurentian Library), parchment, about the beginning of the 11th century. Far the oldest and most important; in Sidgwick's view the only real manuscript authority for the Eumenides.

**G.** — the Guelferbytanus, Wolfenbüttel, paper, 15th century; merely a copy of M., badly and ignorantly made.

**Ma.** — the Marcianus, Florence (Laurentian Library), silk, 15th century; also merely a copy of M.

**P.** — the Parisiensis, Paris, paper, 15th century; very like M. and probably derived from it.

**A.** — the Augustanus, Munich, paper, 16th century; contains the Eumenides only from line 576 to the end; derived from M.

### The Venetian group

**V.** — the Venetus, Venice (Library of St Mark), parchment, 13th century (15th according to Wecklein); omits 582-642 and 794-823.

**Fl.** — the Florentinus, Florence (Laurentian Library), paper, 14th century; the same omissions as V.

**Fa.** — the Farnesianus, Naples (once in the Farnese library), silk, 14th century; the same omissions, and the recension of Demetrius Triclinius.

**MSS.** — the manuscripts. **Schol.** — the scholia of M., which sometimes explain an older and truer text than M.'s own.
