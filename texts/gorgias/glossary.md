# title: Glossary of names and words in the Gorgias
# lang: eng
# license: CC BY-SA 4.0

## Γοργίας
**Γοργίας, ὁ** Gorgias of Leontini in Sicily, the famous teacher of rhetoric, a guest at Callicles' house; he answers Socrates first (447a–461b).

## Πῶλος
**Πῶλος, ὁ** Polus of Acragas, a young follower of Gorgias and writer on rhetoric; he takes over the argument from 461b to 481b.

## Καλλικλῆς
**Καλλικλῆς, ὁ** Callicles of Acharnae (495d), the host, a young Athenian with political ambitions; he argues that the stronger by nature should have more (482c–527e).

## Χαιρεφῶν
**Χαιρεφῶν, ὁ** Chaerephon, Socrates' devoted friend, who comes with him and puts the first questions to Gorgias (447a–448d).

## Ἡρόδικος
**Ἡρόδικος, ὁ** Herodicus, a physician, brother of Gorgias (448b; 456b).

## Ἀριστοφῶν
**Ἀριστοφῶν, ὁ** Aristophon son of Aglaophon, a painter, brother of Polygnotus (448b).

## Ζεῦξις
**Ζεῦξις, ὁ** Zeuxis of Heraclea, the painter (453c–d).

## Θεμιστοκλῆς
**Θεμιστοκλῆς, ὁ** Themistocles, the Athenian statesman who built the fleet and the walls (455e; 503c; 515d–519a).

## Περικλῆς
**Περικλῆς, ὁ** Pericles, the Athenian statesman (455e; 503c; 515c–516d; 519a); Socrates argues that he made the Athenians worse.

## Κίμων
**Κίμων, ὁ** Cimon son of Miltiades, Athenian general and statesman, ostracised (503c; 515d; 516d; 519a).

## Μιλτιάδης
**Μιλτιάδης, ὁ** Miltiades, the victor of Marathon, whom the Athenians nearly put to death (503c; 515d; 516d).

## Ἀρχέλαος
**Ἀρχέλαος, ὁ** Archelaus, king of Macedonia, son of Perdiccas and a slave woman, who seized the throne by murder; Polus' example of the happy wrongdoer (470d–479e; 525d).

## Περδίκκας
**Περδίκκας, ὁ** Perdiccas II, king of Macedonia, father of Archelaus (470d–471c).

## Ἀλκέτης
**Ἀλκέτης, ὁ** Alcetas, brother of Perdiccas, whom Archelaus murdered with his son Alexander (471a–b).

## Νικίας
**Νικίας, ὁ** Nicias son of Niceratus, Athenian general, who with his brothers dedicated tripods in the precinct of Dionysus (472a).

## Ἀριστοκράτης
**Ἀριστοκράτης, ὁ** Aristocrates son of Scellias, Athenian general, who dedicated an offering at Delphi (472a–b).

## Πυριλάμπης
**Πυριλάμπης, ὁ** Pyrilampes, Athenian, Plato's stepfather; his son Demus was Callicles' beloved (481d; 513b).

## Δῆμος
**Δῆμος, ὁ** Demus son of Pyrilampes; Socrates puns on his name and the Athenian δῆμος, Callicles' two loves (481d; 513b).

## Ἀλκιβιάδης
**Ἀλκιβιάδης, ὁ** Alcibiades son of Clinias, Socrates' beloved (481d; 519a).

## Ζῆθος
**Ζῆθος, ὁ** Zethus, the practical brother in Euripides' *Antiope*, who urges his musical brother Amphion to give up his pursuits; Callicles takes his part towards Socrates (485e; 489e; 506b).

## Ἀμφίων
**Ἀμφίων, ὁ** Amphion, the musician brother of Zethus in Euripides' *Antiope* (485e; 506b).

## Εὐριπίδης
**Εὐριπίδης, ὁ** Euripides, the tragic poet, quoted from the *Antiope* (484e–486c) and from a lost play (492e).

## Πίνδαρος
**Πίνδαρος, ὁ** Pindar, the lyric poet, quoted on law, "king of all" (484b; 488b).

## Ἡρακλῆς
**Ἡρακλῆς, ὁ** Heracles, who drove off the cattle of Geryon without paying for them (484b).

## Γηρυόνης
**Γηρυόνης, ὁ** Geryon, the three-bodied giant whose cattle Heracles took (484b).

## Τείσανδρος
**Τείσανδρος, ὁ** Tisander of Aphidnae, one of Callicles' companions in study (487c).

## Ἄνδρων
**Ἄνδρων, ὁ** Andron son of Androtion, one of Callicles' companions (487c).

## Ναυσικύδης
**Ναυσικύδης, ὁ** Nausicydes of Cholargus, one of Callicles' companions (487c).

## Κινησίας
**Κινησίας, ὁ** Cinesias son of Meles, a dithyrambic poet (501e).

## Μέλης
**Μέλης, ὁ** Meles, a lyre-singer, father of Cinesias (501e; 502a).

## Ἐπίχαρμος
**Ἐπίχαρμος, ὁ** Epicharmus, the Sicilian comic poet, quoted (505e).

## Ἀναξαγόρας
**Ἀναξαγόρας, ὁ** Anaxagoras of Clazomenae, the philosopher, whose saying "all things together" Socrates recalls (465d).

## Θεαρίων
**Θεαρίων, ὁ** Thearion, a famous baker (518b).

## Μίθαικος
**Μίθαικος, ὁ** Mithaecus, a Sicilian who wrote a cookery book (518b).

## Σάραμβος
**Σάραμβος, ὁ** Sarambus, a wine-dealer (518b).

## Ἀριστείδης
**Ἀριστείδης, ὁ** Aristides son of Lysimachus, "the Just", Athenian statesman (526b).

## Μίνως
**Μίνως, ὁ** Minos, son of Zeus, king of Crete, a judge of the dead in the myth (523e–526c).

## Ῥαδάμανθυς
**Ῥαδάμανθυς, ὁ** Rhadamanthys, son of Zeus, judge of the dead from Asia (523e–526b).

## Αἰακός
**Αἰακός, ὁ** Aeacus, son of Zeus, judge of the dead from Europe (524a; 526c).

## Τάρταρος
**Τάρταρος, ὁ** Tartarus, the prison of punishment below the earth (523b; 524a; 526b).

## Ἅιδης
**Ἅιδης, ὁ** Hades, the god of the dead; ἐν Ἅιδου, "in the house of Hades", the underworld (493b; 522e; 525b–e).

## Θερσίτης
**Θερσίτης, ὁ** Thersites, the ugly railer of the *Iliad* (525e).

## Τάνταλος
**Τάνταλος, ὁ** Tantalus, punished for ever in Hades (525e).

## Σίσυφος
**Σίσυφος, ὁ** Sisyphus, punished for ever in Hades (525e).

## Τιτυός
**Τιτυός, ὁ** Tityus, punished for ever in Hades (525e).

## Προμηθεύς
**Προμηθεύς, ὁ** Prometheus, told to take from men the knowledge of their death (523d).

## Ὅμηρος
**Ὅμηρος, ὁ** Homer, quoted at 449a, 516c, 523a, 525d and 526d.

## Ἀγλαοφῶν
**Ἀγλαοφῶν, -ῶντος, ὁ** Aglaophon, father of the painter Aristophon and of his brother (448b); the brother was the painter Polygnotus of Thasos (Lodge on the passage).

## Κλεοπάτρα
**Κλεοπάτρα, ἡ** Cleopatra, mother of the boy whom Archelaus threw into a well and drowned, telling her that he had fallen in chasing a goose (471c).

## Νικήρατος
**Νικήρατος, ὁ** Niceratus, father of the Nicias whom Polus could call as a witness (472a).

## Σκελλίας
**Σκελλίας, -ου, ὁ** Scellias (or Scellius), father of Aristocrates, another witness Polus could call (472b; Lodge on the passage).

## Κλεινίας
**Κλεινίας, -ου, ὁ** Cleinias, father of Alcibiades, with whom Socrates says he is in love (481d).

## Κλεινίειος
**Κλεινίειος, -α, -ον** of Cleinias: ὁ Κλεινίειος οὗτος, this son of Cleinias, Alcibiades (482a).

## Ξέρξης
**Ξέρξης, -ου, ὁ** Xerxes, who marched against Greece: Callicles' example of the right of the stronger (483d).

## Ἀφιδναῖος
**Ἀφιδναῖος, -α, -ον** of Aphidnae, an Attic deme (of the tribe Aiantis, Lodge on 487c): Tisander of Aphidnae, one of Callicles' companions in the pursuit of wisdom (487c).

## Ἀνδροτίων
**Ἀνδροτίων, -ωνος, ὁ** Androtion, father of Andron, another of Callicles' companions (487c).

## Ἀλωπεκῆθεν
**Ἀλωπεκῆθεν** (adverb) from Alopece, an Attic deme: Σωκράτης ὁ Ἀλωπεκῆθεν, as Callicles is of Acharnae (495d).
