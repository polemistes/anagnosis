# title: Glossary of names and invented words in A True Story
# lang: eng
# license: CC BY-SA 4.0

## Κτησίας
**Κτησίας, -ου, ὁ** Ctesias of Cnidus, physician at the Persian court, who wrote about India "a great deal ... that he had never seen himself nor heard from anyone else with a reputation for truthfulness" (Harmon; VH 1.3); in the place of the wicked he suffers the severest punishment, with Herodotus, among those who wrote what was not true (VH 2.31).

## Κτησίοχος
**Κτησίοχος, -ου, ὁ** Ctesiochus, father of Ctesias (VH 1.3).

## Ἰαμβοῦλος
**Ἰαμβοῦλος, -ου, ὁ** Iambulus, author of a fabulous voyage to islands "in the great sea", who "made up a falsehood that is patent to everybody, but wrote a story that is not uninteresting for all that" (Harmon; VH 1.3).

## Ἀλκίνοος
**Ἀλκίνοος (contr. Ἀλκίνους), -όου (-ου), ὁ** Alcinous, king of the Phaeacians, to whom Odysseus told his wanderings in the Odyssey: "Alcinous and his court" (Harmon; VH 1.3).

## Ἐνδυμίων
**Ἐνδυμίων, -ωνος, ὁ** Endymion, in the myth the youth whom the Moon loved as he slept; here "a human being ... who had once been ravished from our country in his sleep, and on coming there had been made king of the land", the Moon (Harmon; VH 1.11); he wars with Phaethon over the colony on the Morning Star (VH 1.12–20), offers the narrator his son in marriage (VH 1.21) and sends him off with gifts (VH 1.27).

## Φαέθων
**Φαέθων, -οντος, ὁ** Phaethon (the name means "shining", from φαέθω), "the king of the inhabitants of the sun" (Harmon; VH 1.12), Endymion's enemy, who leads the Ant Dragoons and walls off the Moon from the Sun's light (VH 1.16–19, 1.28); in the myth the son of Helios who drove his father's chariot.

## Κεγχροβόλος
**Κεγχροβόλος, -ου, ὁ** one of the "Millet-shooters" (Harmon), a corps in Endymion's army (VH 1.13); from κέγχρος, millet, and βάλλω, throw.

## Ἀνεμοδρόμοι
**Ἀνεμοδρόμοι, -ων, οἱ** the "Volplaneurs" (Harmon), "wind-runners" (ἄνεμος, wind, and δραμεῖν, run), allies of Endymion from the Great Bear: infantry that fly without wings, letting their long tunics fill with wind like sails (VH 1.13).

## Στρουθοβάλανοι
**Στρουθοβάλανοι, -ων, οἱ** the "Sparrowcorns" (Harmon), from στρουθός, sparrow, and βάλανος, acorn: allies promised from the stars over Cappadocia, who never came, so that the narrator did not venture to describe them (VH 1.13).

## Νυκτερίων
**Νυκτερίων, -ωνος, ὁ** "Owlett son of Fairweather" (Harmon: Νυκτερίων ὁ Εὐδιάνακτος), leader of Endymion's infantry (VH 1.15); from νύκτερος, of the night, and νυκτερίς, bat.

## Κυνοβάλανοι
**Κυνοβάλανοι, -ων, οἱ** the "Puppycorns" (Harmon), from κύων, dog, and βάλανος, acorn: "dogfaced men who fight on the back of winged acorns", sent to Phaethon by the inhabitants of the Dog-star (VH 1.16).

## Πυρωνίδης
**Πυρωνίδης, -ου, ὁ** "Firebrace" (Harmon), one of the three Sunites who swear to the peace treaty (VH 1.20); from πῦρ, fire, with a patronymic ending.

## Θερείτης
**Θερείτης, -ου, ὁ** "Parcher" (Harmon), one of the three Sunites who swear to the peace treaty (VH 1.20); from θέρος, summer heat.

## Φλόγιος
**Φλόγιος, -ου, ὁ** "Burns" (Harmon), one of the three Sunites who swear to the peace treaty (VH 1.20); from φλόξ, flame.

## Νύκτωρ
**Νύκτωρ, -ορος, ὁ** "Darkling" (Harmon), one of the three Moonites who swear to the peace treaty (VH 1.20); the adverb νύκτωρ, by night, made a name.

## Μήνιος
**Μήνιος, -ου, ὁ** "Moony" (Harmon), one of the three Moonites who swear to the peace treaty (VH 1.20); from μήνη, the moon.

## Πολυλάμπης
**Πολυλάμπης, -ου, ὁ** "Allbright" (Harmon), one of the three Moonites who swear to the peace treaty (VH 1.20); from πολύς, much, and λάμπω, shine.

## Δενδρῖτης
**Δενδρῖτης, -ου, ὁ** one of the "Arboreals" (Harmon; Greek Δενδρῖται, Tree-men, from δένδρον, tree), a kind of men on the Moon who grow on a tree of flesh sprung from a man's planted right testicle and are shelled out of its acorns (VH 1.22).

## Κόρωνος
**Κόρωνος, -ου, ὁ** king of Cloudcuckooland, "Crow" (from κορώνη, crow), son of Κοττυφίων, "Blackbird": Harmon makes the pair "Crow Dawson" (VH 1.29); Κόρωνος is Dindorf's correction of the manuscripts' κορωνός.

## Κοττυφίων
**Κοττυφίων, -ωνος, ὁ** "Blackbird" (from κόττυφος, blackbird), father of Κόρωνος, the king of Cloudcuckooland (VH 1.29); Harmon's "Crow Dawson" makes him a daw.

## Ἀριστοφάνης
**Ἀριστοφάνης, -ους, ὁ** Aristophanes, the Athenian comic poet, whose Birds invented Cloudcuckooland; the narrator calls him "a wise and truthful man whose writings are distrusted without reason" (Harmon; VH 1.29).

## Σκίνθαρος
**Σκίνθαρος, -ου, ὁ** Scintharus, the old Cypriote merchant whom the travellers find living in the whale with his son Cinyras after his ship was swallowed; the tribes of the whale make war on him over his tribute (VH 1.33–37); he is the sailing-master on the escape (VH 2.1–2, 2.6), and his bald head grows hair again (VH 2.41).

## Ταριχᾶνες
**Ταριχᾶνες, -ων, οἱ** the "Broilers" (Harmon), from τάριχος, salted fish: "an eel-eyed, lobster-faced people", warlike and carnivorous, living in the tail of the whale's forest (VH 1.35), led by Πήλαμος (VH 1.38).

## Τριτωνομένδητες
**Τριτωνομένδητες, -ων, οἱ** the "Mergoats" (Harmon), from Τρίτων, the merman of the sea, and Μένδης, the goat-god of Mendes in Egypt: "like men above and catfish below", the least wicked of the whale's tribes, who stay neutral in the war (VH 1.35, 1.38–39).

## Παγουρίδαι
**Παγουρίδαι, -ῶν, οἱ** "Clan Crawfish" (Harmon), the "sons of the crab" (πάγουρος, crab, with the patronymic ending -ίδαι), a tribe of the whale's interior, "good fighters and swift runners", who with the Solefeet attack Scintharus first (VH 1.35–36).

## Πήλαμος
**Πήλαμος, -ου, ὁ** "Tom Cod" (Harmon), leader of the Broilers (VH 1.38); from πηλαμύς, a young tunny.

## Αἰολοκένταυρος
**Αἰολοκένταυρος, -ου, ὁ** "Aeolocentaur" (Harmon), from Αἴολος, keeper of the winds (or αἰόλος, nimble), and κένταυρος, centaur: king of one of the two fleets of island-riding giants, whose dolphins Brinedrinker had driven off; his side wins the isle-fight (VH 1.42).

## Θαλασσοπότης
**Θαλασσοπότης, -ου, ὁ** "Brinedrinker" (Harmon), from θάλασσα, sea, and πότης, drinker: king of the other fleet in the isle-fight (VH 1.42).

## Γαλάτεια
**Γαλάτεια, -ας, ἡ** Galatea the Nereid, whose temple stands in the middle of the cheese island in the sea of milk (VH 2.3); her name recalls γάλα, milk.

## Τυρώ
**Τυρώ, -οῦς, ἡ** Tyro, daughter of Salmoneus, loved by Poseidon in the myth; queen of the cheese island, an honour Poseidon gave her (VH 2.3); her name recalls τυρός, cheese.

## Σαλμωνεύς
**Σαλμωνεύς, -έως, ὁ** Salmoneus, father of Tyro (VH 2.3).

## Ῥαδάμανθυς
**Ῥαδάμανθυς, -υος, ὁ** Rhadamanthus, "the Cretan", ruler and judge of the Isle of the Blest (VH 2.6), who tries Ajax, Theseus and Menelaus, and the narrator's company (VH 2.7–10), threatens Socrates with banishment (VH 2.17), musters the heroes against the wicked (VH 2.23), pursues Cinyras and Helen (VH 2.26) and foretells the narrator's further wanderings (VH 2.27).

## Ἱπποκράτης
**Ἱπποκράτης, -ους, ὁ** Hippocrates, "the Coan physician" (Harmon), to whom Ajax is handed over for the hellebore treatment (VH 2.7).

## Ἀννίβας
**Ἀννίβας, -ου (Dor. -α), ὁ** Hannibal of Carthage, who loses the case of precedence to Alexander (VH 2.9).

## Ἀριστείδης
**Ἀριστείδης, -ου, ὁ** Aristides "the Just" of Athens, one of Rhadamanthus' assessors (VH 2.10).

## Ἀρίων
**Ἀρίων, -ονος, ὁ** Arion of Lesbos, singer to the lyre, who leads the choruses of the Blest (VH 2.15).

## Ἀνακρέων
**Ἀνακρέων, -οντος, ὁ** Anacreon of Teos, the lyric poet, among the leaders of the choruses of the Blest (VH 2.15).

## Ἀνάχαρσις
**Ἀνάχαρσις, -ιος, ὁ** Anacharsis, "the Scythian" sage, among the barbarians in the Isle of the Blest (VH 2.17).

## Ζάμολξις
**Ζάμολξις, -ιος, ὁ** Zamolxis (Zalmoxis), "the Thracian", revered by the Getae as a god and said to have been a slave of Pythagoras (Herodotus 4.94–96); among the barbarians in the Isle of the Blest (VH 2.17).

## Νομᾶς
**Νομᾶς, -ᾶ, ὁ** "Numa the Italian" (Harmon), Numa Pompilius, the lawgiving second king of Rome (VH 2.17).

## Λυκοῦργος
**Λυκοῦργος, -ου, ὁ** "Lycurgus of Sparta" (Harmon), the lawgiver (VH 2.17).

## Τέλλος
**Τέλλος, -ου, ὁ** "Tellus of Athens" (Harmon), whom Solon named the happiest of men (Herodotus 1.30) (VH 2.17).

## Περίανδρος
**Περίανδρος, -ου, ὁ** Periander, tyrant of Corinth, counted among the Seven Wise Men; the one sage missing from the Isle of the Blest (VH 2.17).

## Σωφρονίσκος
**Σωφρονίσκος, -ου, ὁ** Sophroniscus, father of Socrates (VH 2.17).

## Θεσπιεύς
**Θεσπιεύς, -έως, ὁ** a man of Thespiae in Boeotia: "Narcissus of Thespiae" (Harmon; VH 2.17).

## Ὕλας
**Ὕλας, -α, ὁ** Hylas, the youth loved by Heracles, drawn down into a spring by the nymphs; among the handsome lads about Socrates (VH 2.17).

## Ἀρίστιππος
**Ἀρίστιππος, -ου, ὁ** Aristippus of Cyrene, the philosopher of pleasure, whose followers, with Epicurus', are "in the highest favour among the heroes" (Harmon; VH 2.18).

## Αἴσωπος
**Αἴσωπος, -ου, ὁ** "Aesop the Phrygian", the fable-teller, whom the heroes "have ... for a jester" (Harmon; VH 2.18).

## Διογένης
**Διογένης, -ους, ὁ** Diogenes of Sinope, the Cynic, who in the Isle of the Blest has so changed his ways that he married Lais the courtesan and dances when drunk (VH 2.18).

## Λαίς
**Λαΐς, -ΐδος, ἡ** Lais, the famous courtesan, whom Diogenes marries in the Isle of the Blest (VH 2.18).

## Χρύσιππος
**Χρύσιππος, -ου, ὁ** Chrysippus, the Stoic philosopher, who may not set foot on the island until he has taken hellebore, the cure for madness, a fourth time (VH 2.18).

## Τιγράνης
**Τιγράνης, -ου, ὁ** Tigranes, Homer's real name among his fellow-countrymen, for he tells the narrator he was a Babylonian; he became "Homer" when he was a hostage (ὅμηρος) among the Greeks (VH 2.20).

## Ζηνόδοτος
**Ζηνόδοτος, -ου, ὁ** Zenodotus of Ephesus, the first editor of Homer at Alexandria, who, with Aristarchus, marked lines as spurious; Homer claims them all, so the narrator holds the grammarians "guilty of pedantry in the highest degree" (Harmon; VH 2.20).

## Ἐμπεδοκλῆς
**Ἐμπεδοκλῆς, -έους, ὁ** Empedocles of Acragas, the philosopher said to have leapt into Etna: he arrives "all burned and his body completely cooked" and is refused (Harmon; VH 2.21).

## Ἐπειός
**Ἐπειός, -οῦ, ὁ** Epeius, builder of the Wooden Horse and a boxer in the Iliad; his bout with Areius the Egyptian at the Games of the Dead is a draw (VH 2.22).

## Φάλαρις
**Φάλαρις, -ιδος, ὁ** "Phalaris of Acragas" (Harmon), the tyrant who roasted men in a bronze bull; a leader of the wicked who break out against the Isle of the Blest (VH 2.23).

## Ἀκραγαντῖνος
**Ἀκραγαντῖνος, -η, -ον** of Acragas (Agrigentum) in Sicily: Phalaris (VH 2.23).

## Βούσιρις
**Βούσιρις, -ιδος, ὁ** "Busiris the Egyptian" (Harmon), the mythical king who sacrificed strangers until Heracles killed him; a leader of the wicked (VH 2.23).

## Τελαμώνιος
**Τελαμώνιος, -α, -ον** son of Telamon: "Ajax, the son of Telamon" (Harmon), who leads the heroes, by now in his right mind again (VH 2.23).

## Δήλιον
**Δήλιον, -ου, τό** Delium in Boeotia, where the Athenians were defeated in 424 BC and Socrates fought in the retreat; on the island he is braver "than when he fought at Delium in his lifetime" (Harmon; VH 2.23).

## Νεκρακαδημία
**Νεκρακαδημία, -ας, ἡ** "the Academy of the Dead" (Harmon), from νεκρός, dead, and Ἀκαδημία, the Academy: Socrates' name for the park given him as a prize, where he gathers his comrades (VH 2.23).

## Κινύρας
**Κινύρας, -ου, ὁ** Cinyras, son of Scintharus, "a tall and handsome lad" (Harmon), who elopes with Helen (VH 2.25), is caught and sent to the place of the wicked (VH 2.26), where the travellers see him hung in the smoke of a slow fire (VH 2.31); he bears the name of the legendary king of Cyprus.

## Τυρόεσσα
**Τυρόεσσα, -ης, ἡ** "Cheesie" (Harmon), from τυρός, cheese: the cheese island in the sea of milk (VH 2.3), to which Cinyras means to flee with Helen and near which the pursuers overtake them (VH 2.25–26).

## Λουκιανός
**Λουκιανός, -οῦ, ὁ** Lucian, the author, who names himself in the couplet Homer composes for him and he sets up by the harbour: "One Lucian, whom the blessed gods befriend, / Beheld what's here, and home again did wend" (Harmon; VH 2.28).

## Τίμων
**Τίμων, -ωνος, ὁ** "Timon of Athens" (Harmon), the misanthrope, warder of the single narrow way into the place of the wicked (VH 2.31).

## Ἡρόδοτος
**Ἡρόδοτος, -ου, ὁ** Herodotus, the historian, who says a fragrance breathes from "Araby the blest" (Harmon; VH 2.5); he suffers among the writers of untruths (VH 2.31).

## Νυκτίπορος
**Νυκτίπορος, -ου, ὁ** "Sleepwalker" (Harmon), the river beside the Isle of Dreams (VH 2.33); the adjective νυκτιπόρος, night-faring, made a name.

## Παννυχία
**Παννυχία, -ας, ἡ** "Eight-hours" (Harmon), one of the two springs by the gates of the city of Dreams, "All-night" (from πάννυχος, all night long); the other is Νήγρετος, "Soundly" (VH 2.33).

## Ταραξίων
**Ταραξίων, -ωνος, ὁ** "Nightmare" (Harmon), from ταράσσω, trouble, son of Ματαιογένης, one of the two satraps of Sleep (VH 2.33).

## Ματαιογένης
**Ματαιογένης, -ους, ὁ** "Causeless" (Harmon), from μάταιος, idle, vain, and -γενής, born: father of Ταραξίων (VH 2.33).

## Πλουτοκλῆς
**Πλουτοκλῆς, -έους, ὁ** "Rich" (Harmon), from πλοῦτος, wealth, and κλέος, fame: son of Φαντασίων, the other satrap of Sleep (VH 2.33).

## Φαντασίων
**Φαντασίων, -ωνος, ὁ** "Fancy" (Harmon), from φαντασία, appearance, imagination: father of Πλουτοκλῆς (VH 2.33).

## Καρεῶτις
**Καρεῶτις, -ιδος, ἡ** "Drowsimere" (Harmon), from κάρος, heavy sleep: the spring in the centre of the square of the city of Dreams (VH 2.33).

## Ἀντιφῶν
**Ἀντιφῶν, -ῶντος, ὁ** Antiphon, "the interpreter of dreams" (Harmon), who presides as prophet over the oracle in the city of Dreams (VH 2.33).

## Κολοκυνθοπειράτης
**Κολοκυνθοπειράτης, -ου, ὁ** one of the "Pumpkin-pirates" (Harmon), from κολόκυνθα, pumpkin, and πειρατής, pirate: savages who sail in hollowed pumpkins and shoot pumpkin-seeds, enemies of the Nut-sailors (VH 2.37).

## Μινώταυρος
**Μινώταυρος, -ου, ὁ** the Minotaur, the bull-headed son of Pasiphae: the Bullheads have horns "in the style that the Minotaur is represented at home" (Harmon; VH 2.44).

## Ἐκβαλοῦσα
**Ἐκβαλοῦσα, -ης, ἡ** the island of the Asslegs (VH 2.46), in Nilén's text "she who casts out" (participle of ἐκβάλλω), the reading of the manuscript Γ; the other manuscripts have καβαλοῦσα, which Harmon prints as Cabalusa and renders "Witchery". Nilén notes that the names here are not yet certainly mended.

## Ὑδαμαρδία
**Ὑδαμαρδία, -ας, ἡ** "Watertown" (Harmon), the city of the island of the Asslegs (VH 2.46); its name begins with ὕδωρ, water, and the woman the narrator binds turns to water (VH 2.46).

## Ὀνοσκελέα
**Ὀνοσκελέα, -ας, ἡ** one of the "Asslegs" (Harmon), from ὄνος, ass, and σκέλος, leg: sea-women with asses' hooves who make strangers drunk and devour them (VH 2.46–47).
