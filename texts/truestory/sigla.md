Nilén's critical apparatus stands at the foot of each page of the text, in Latin. In the print each item begins with the number of the line of the page it concerns; the app drops these numbers and files each item under its section. An item gives the reading of the text and the manuscripts that have it, then the other readings with their sigla, and the corrections and conjectures of editors and scholars, named in short forms (Schw, Ibtz, Bekk ...). Where the reading of the text is followed by a square bracket ("τοὺς λόγους] ΓΩIS λόγους ZNFv"), the sigla after the bracket are the witnesses for the text and the rest give the variant. Each book opens with a note naming its manuscripts: "Libri: ΓΩSZPNF", adding for Book 1 that the beginning of the book is lost in P up to the middle of chapter 14 (p. 129). Nilén explains the date classes, the hands of the correctors and his tables in the praefatio; he gives no key to the Latin abbreviations or to the signs inside the readings, and those are explained below from their use.

## The manuscripts used for the Verae Narrationes

Nilén's table of the witnesses used for each of libelli 1–14 (p. X) gives for libelli 13 and 14, the two books of the True Story: Γ, Ω, F, N, P, S, Z. In the list of all the witnesses (p. VII) each has a "date class", a raised letter whose meaning he gives in a footnote (pp. VII–VIII): the vowels aᵃ to aᵘ stand for the 9th/10th to the 12th century, the consonants rᶠ to rᶻ for the 13th/14th to the 16th/17th century, and a bare r for a more recent age. He does not turn the single steps into centuries; the letters seem to rise in alphabetical order through each range. The repertory on pp. XIV–XVI shows where the two books stand in each manuscript. For these seven witnesses, Nilén's table (p. X) says that the silence of the apparatus can be trusted everywhere, except in the damaged parts that the apparatus points out: where a manuscript is not named, it agrees with the printed text.

**Γ** — Vaticanus 90 (Rome, Vatican Library), date class aᵃ, the earliest step of the 9th/10th–12th-century range (p. VII). Nilén calls it the most trustworthy manuscript; in 59 works it is the oldest of all Lucian manuscripts (p. III), and although no early manuscript has come down complete, Γ can be treated as complete in substance (p. IV, after M. Rothstein). He keeps its order of the works (p. III) and has the whole manuscript in photographs (p. V). The two books are its works 13 and 14 (p. XIV).

**Ω** — Marcianus 434 (Venice, Marcian Library), date class aⁱ (p. VII). One of the manuscripts that stand out by age and trustworthiness (p. IV); Nilén has it whole in photographs (p. V). Works 13 and 14 (p. XIV).

**S** — Mutinensis 193 (Modena, Biblioteca Estense), date class aᵒ (p. VII). Also among the outstanding old manuscripts (p. IV); photographed in great part (p. V). Works 12 and 13 (p. XV).

**Z** — Vaticanus 1323 (Rome, Vatican Library), date class rˡ (p. VII). Here the two books stand first and second (p. XV).

**P** — Vaticanus 76 (Rome, Vatican Library), date class rˢ (p. VII). It has lost the beginning of Book 1 (table, p. X, and repertory, p. XV), up to the middle of chapter 14 (apparatus, p. 129).

**N** — Parisinus 2957 (Paris, Bibliothèque nationale), date class rʳ (p. VII). Nilén used the collations of N that Eduard Schwartz gave him (p. X). Works 41 and 42 (p. XVI).

**F** — Guelferbytanus fol. (Wolfenbüttel, Herzog August Bibliothek; Nilén gives no number), date class rˡ (p. VII). Nilén knows it from Jacobitz's apparatus (p. X). Works 22 and 23 (p. XV).

**Fv** — F and v set side by side mean that Jacobitz says nothing about F there, and F seems to have been collated against v; F is therefore taken to read what v reads (apparatus, p. 129, repeated for Book 2 on p. 167). On p. IX Nilén says that Jacobitz's silence about A, B and K, and likewise about F, O and the early editions, can nearly always be trusted if one remembers that their collations were written out against v.

Less often the apparatus names other witnesses. For these, Nilén's table (p. XI) says that the silence of the apparatus cannot be trusted enough: an unnamed manuscript need not agree with the text.

**I** — Urbinas 118 (Rome, Vatican Library), date class rᶠ (p. VII). It is cited in both books ("ΩIS"), more often in Book 1. In Book 1 a middle part is lost (table, p. XI; repertory, p. XIV, "ιη*").

**i** — the supplements of I, written by a later hand to fill its gaps, date class rˣ (p. VII; "s." = supplied, p. XIV). In Book 1 I is missing from chapter 13 (after ἐθεασάμην) to the beginning of chapter 21, and i stands in for it ("defic. I suppl. i", in the apparatus).

**A** — Gorlicensis 12 (Görlitz), date class rⁿ (p. VII); cited through Jacobitz (p. X; table, p. XI).

**O** — Monacensis 490 (Munich), date class rʳ (p. VII); cited through Jacobitz for Book 2 (p. X; table, p. XI).

**ε** — Laurentianus C. S. 88 (Florence), date class rʳ (p. VII); named once, for the scholion at the end of Book 2.

**v** — the vulgate, the old common text, cited from the Amsterdam edition of 1743 by Hemsterhuis and Reitz (pp. III, VII and IX). Nilén says that, apart from a few places, the vulgate goes back to the first printed edition (Florence, 1496) and its sources (p. III). The books are numbered 26 and 27 in the vulgate order (p. XVI).

**libri** — the manuscripts; "libri" without other sigla means all of them. **rell.** — the rest of the manuscripts.

## Hands and corrections

Nilén explains the raised signs in a key on p. XI, written for the correctors of Γ, Ω, B, S and U, and adds that he marks the correctors of the other manuscripts in the same way (footnote, p. XI). A siglum with no sign (Γ) gives the reading of the manuscript.

**¹** (Γ¹, Ω¹, S¹, P¹ ...) — the original writing of the first hand, where the manuscript was later corrected.

**\*** (Γ*, Ω*) — a correction made with the scraper, by erasing; "eras. Γ*" means erased.

**\* with another sign** (Γ*ᵃ, Ω*ˣ, S*², P*ˣ ...) — a correction made both by scraping and with ink, by the hand that the second sign shows: Γ*ᵃ is a correction by erasure and fresh ink by hand a.

**²** (Γ², Ω², S²) — the first hand, in its own ink.

**³** (Γ³, S³) — the first hand, in the red ink of the rubricator.

**ˢ, ᵃ** (Γᵃ, Ωᵃ) — hands of the scribe's own time or nearly so.

**ᵛ, ˣ** (Γᵛ, Γˣ, Ωᵛ, Ωˣ, Sᵛ, Sˣ, Pˣ, Zˣ, Fˣ, Nˣ, Iˣ) — correctors later than the first hand, whose age can hardly be fixed more closely.

**Γʸ, Ωᵇ** — correctors of the 13th/14th century (neither is listed for the True Story).

**Γᶜ, Ωᶜ** — one and the same corrector, of the 15th century, who worked in both manuscripts.

**ʳ** (Γʳ, Ωʳ, Sʳ, Pʳ) — the most recent corrector.

For the True Story, Nilén's tables (pp. XI–XII) list these hands: in Γ, Γ*, Γ², Γ³, Γᵃ, Γˣ and Γᶜ (Book 1), and Γ*, Γ², Γ³, Γᵃ, Γᵛ, Γˣ and Γʳ (Book 2); in Ω, Ω*, Ω², Ωᵃ, Ωᵛ, Ωˣ, Ωᶜ and Ωʳ (Book 1), and Ω*, Ω², Ωᵛ, Ωˣ, Ωᶜ and Ωʳ (Book 2); in S, S², Sᵛ, Sˣ and Sʳ (Book 1), and S*, S², S³, Sᵛ and Sˣ (Book 2). He gives no tables for Z, P, N, F and I, but marks them the same way (Z¹, Z², Z³, Zˣ, Z*ˣ; P¹, P*, Pˣ, P*ˣ, Pʳ; N¹, N², Nˣ; F¹, Fˣ; I¹, I², Iˣ, I*ˣ). Pˣ is the hand named most often in the apparatus. Once the apparatus has Γ¹* (on τέγος, Book 2), a combination that the key does not explain.

## Editors and scholars named

Nilén writes these names in short forms and does not list them. Who they are is given here in the usual way, with a page reference where he says something about them himself.

**Ibtz** — Karl Jacobitz, editor of Lucian (Leipzig, 1836–1841, and the Teubner text). Nilén uses his apparatus as the witness for A, F and O and for the other manuscripts Jacobitz used (p. X); the table of Jacobitz's witnesses is on p. VIII.

**Dind** — Wilhelm Dindorf, editor of Lucian (Paris, 1840; Leipzig, 1858).

**Bekk** — Immanuel Bekker, editor of Lucian (Leipzig, 1853).

**Sbdt** — Julius Sommerbrodt, editor of Lucian (Berlin, 1886–1899). He let Nilén use his apparatus (p. X); the table of his witnesses is on pp. IX–X. "Rohde ap. Sbdt" is Rohde as reported in Sommerbrodt's edition.

**Schw** — Eduard Schwartz, the name met most often. He gave Nilén his collations (selections from V, many readings of C, H and N, and of M in three works), and he helped to shape both the text and the apparatus (pp. VI and X).

**Frze** — Franz Volkmar Fritzsche, editor of Lucian (Rostock, 1860–1882), whom Nilén names on pp. VIII–X.

**Rothst** — Max Rothstein, author of the Quaestiones Lucianeae (1888), cited by Nilén on pp. IV–V.

**du Soul** — Moïse du Soul (Solanus), whose notes are printed in the Amsterdam edition of 1743.

**Guyet** — François Guyet (1575–1655), French critic.

**Gesn**, **Gesner** — Johann Matthias Gesner (1691–1761), whose notes are also in the Amsterdam edition.

**Gronov** — Gronovius (the apparatus does not say which member of the family).

**Wesseling** — Petrus Wesseling (1692–1764), Dutch scholar.

**Reitz** — Johann Friedrich Reitz, who with Tiberius Hemsterhuis produced the Amsterdam edition of 1743 (v).

**Iensius** — Johannes Jensius (1671–1718), author of Lectiones Lucianeae.

**Markland** — Jeremiah Markland (1693–1776), English critic.

**Budé** — Guillaume Budé (1467–1540).

**Belin** — Belin de Ballu, French translator of Lucian.

**Schmieder** — Schmieder, an editor of Lucian.

**Lehm** — Johann Theophilus Lehmann, editor of Lucian (Leipzig, 1822–1831).

**Cob** — Carel Gabriel Cobet (1813–1889), Dutch critic.

**Rohde** — Erwin Rohde (1845–1898).

**Herw** — Henricus van Herwerden (1831–1910), Dutch critic.

**Schneidewin** — named once, as perhaps proposing the same reading as Rohde; the apparatus does not say which Schneidewin.

**Photius** — the Byzantine patriarch's Bibliotheca, cited by its numbered "codices": cod. 166 (on Antonius Diogenes, whom Photius calls the source of the True Story) and cod. 72 (on Ctesias).

**Thom. Mag.** — Thomas Magister, Byzantine grammarian of the 14th century, who quotes the text.

**Suida** — the Suda, the Byzantine lexicon.

**Voss.** — named once, at the end of Book 2, among the witnesses for the closing scholion (ΩSFε Voss.). It is probably a manuscript of the Vossius collection at Leiden, but Nilén does not list it among his witnesses or explain it.

**schol.**, **scholiastes** — the ancient and Byzantine notes written in the manuscripts; "schol. ΩS" is the scholion in Ω and S.

Other works of Lucian are cited by short titles to compare: **Iupp. Trag.** (Zeus Rants), **Bis Acc.** (The Double Indictment), **Iudic. vocal.** (The Consonants at Law), **Syria Dea** (The Syrian Goddess). Homer and Herodotus are cited too (**Il.**, **Od.**, **Odyss.**, **Herod.**).

## Signs and abbreviations

Nilén does not explain any of these in the praefatio. The meanings given here are the usual ones in a Teubner apparatus, checked against how he uses them.

**corr.** — corrected: "corr. Fˣ" means F's later hand corrected it. **ex corr.** — produced by correction. **ex ... corr.** — corrected from the reading given ("ex φ***τίδος corr. Z*ˣ"). **in ... corr.** — changed into the reading given. **correx.**, **emendavit** — corrected (by a scholar).

**spscr.** — written above, over the line or over the word. **supra vs.**, **supra** — above the line. **in vsu** — in the line itself (versu, as opposed to above it or in the margin). **subscr.** — written below; also the subscription at the end of a book ("subscr. Γᵃ in mg.", the note of Alexander, bishop of Nicaea, at the end of Book 1).

**mg.**, **in mg.** — in the margin; **mg. super.**, **mg. infer.** the upper and lower margin; **extr. mg.** the outer edge of the margin; **absc.** cut off (the edge of the page trimmed away).

**γρ** — γράφεται, "it is also written": a variant written in the manuscript as another reading, usually in the margin.

**ras.**, **rasura** — an erasure; **eras.** erased; **litura** a blot or smear where something was wiped out. **spat. vac. rel.** — an empty space left (spatio vacuo relicto); **in lac. rel.** in the gap left.

**om.** — leaves out. **add.** — adds. **del.** — deletes, by a hand of the manuscript or by a scholar; **del. indic.** marks for deletion. **puncto not.** — marked with a dot (as a sign of deletion). **transp.** — transposed; **transp. not. β et α spscr.** the change of order marked by β and α written above. **inser.** — inserted. **suppl.** — supplied, added to fill a gap. **defic.** — is missing.

**coni.**, **coniec.** — conjectured (by the scholar named). **conieci**, **scripsi**, **recepi**, **delevi**, **temptavi** — Nilén's own work: I have conjectured, written, adopted, deleted, tried. **eiecit** — threw out (as spurious). **defendit** — defended. **postulat** — calls for. **prob.**, **probante** — approves, with the approval of. **recte** — rightly. **fort.** — fortasse, perhaps. **dubitanter** — hesitantly. **ut vid.** — ut videtur, as it seems. **ni f.** — nisi fallor, unless I am mistaken. **et si recte legi** — if I read it rightly. **legit** — read. **vol.** — voluit, meant to write; **pot. voluisse** rather to have meant. **orsus** — began to write.

**sic** — so in the manuscript (the odd form is not a misprint). **ambig.**, **ambiguus** — the reading cannot be made out for certain. **evan.** — faded. **adumbr.** — only faintly sketched, as with a correction lightly marked. **compend.**, **comp.**, **compendiose** — written as an abbreviation. **tachygr.** — written with a shorthand sign. **litt.**, **lit.** — letter(s).

**edd.** — the editors, or the editions. **recc.** — the more recent ones (as in "recc. edd. omnes", all the recent editors). **cfr.** — compare. **coll.** — comparing. **ap.** — apud, in the work of. **l. l.** — loco laudato, in the passage already cited. **c.** — chapter; **in.**, **med.**, **fin.** the beginning, middle and end. **cod.** — codex (in Photius, a numbered chapter of the Bibliotheca). **i. e.** — that is. **vel** — or; **aut** — or; **post**, **ante**, **inter** — after, before, between; **sq.** — and the following line.

**■** — a black block, printed for a letter lost or unreadable in the manuscript, for example where the margin of Γ is damaged; there is roughly one block for each letter.

**\*** inside a Greek word (ἰχ***, **θέντες) — erased letters, one sign for each letter as far as can be told. Nilén sometimes says in brackets what they seem to have been ("ἰχ*** (θ**)") or how many letters are gone ("2 vel 3 litt. funditus erasae"). For the asterisk after a siglum, see Hands and corrections.

**|** — the end of a line in the manuscript.

**⟨ ⟩** — letters or words added by an editor that are not in the manuscripts ("⟨αὐτοὺς⟩ Schw"). In the text, Nilén prints Schwartz's additions this way (⟨μεγάλοι καὶ⟩, ⟨πέντε⟩).

**[ ]** — words that an editor thinks should be removed. In the text Nilén brackets words he thinks spurious ([καὶ οὗτοι], "καὶ οὗτοι delevi").

**\*\*\*** — in the text, a gap: words lost from the text (after ἔμελλον at the end of the preface, where Nilén says that a gap must be assumed). In the apparatus, three erased letters (see above).

**—** — between two words of a lemma (Κτησίας — ἤκουσεν): the whole passage from the first word to the last. Inside a quotation it marks words left out. In the print, a range of line numbers is also written with a dash (3—6).

**( )** — Nilén's remarks inside an item, such as what a doubtful letter may be ("(αι ni f.)") or the extent of an omission.

**?** — doubt about a reading.

**‖** and **†** are not used in the apparatus of the True Story.
