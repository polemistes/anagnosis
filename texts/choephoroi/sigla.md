Sidgwick's critical apparatus, at the foot of each page of the text, gives the manuscripts' reading of all the doubtful places, wherever one or all the manuscripts are wrong, except where the correction is obvious and generally adopted. Where the reading of the text stands in a manuscript and another manuscript differs, the variant is given in the apparatus without a mark in the text ("36. βαρύ G.": the manuscripts read βαρύς, except G., which has βαρύ by a slip). A reading in the text that is a conjecture is marked there with an asterisk, which this text leaves out, and the apparatus gives the manuscripts' reading and usually the corrector ("38. ἔλαχον MSS. corr. Turn.": the manuscripts read ἔλαχον, and the reading of the text was first given by Turnèbe). Where no reading satisfies him he marks the passage with a dagger.

## The manuscripts

**M.** — the Mediceus, Florence (Laurentian Library), parchment, 10th or 11th century. The beginning of the play is lost: M. begins at line 10, fragments of nine lines being known from other sources. The only original manuscript authority for the Choephoroi.

**G.** — the Guelferbytanus, Wolfenbüttel, paper, 15th century; a copy of M., badly and ignorantly made.

**Ma.** — the Marcianus, Florence (Laurentian Library), silk, 15th century; also merely a copy of M.

**MSS.** — the manuscripts. **Rob.** — Robortelli's edition (Venice 1552), which claims a very old manuscript from Padua, either M. itself or one very like it. **Schol.** — the scholia of M., which sometimes explain a better text than M.'s own.
