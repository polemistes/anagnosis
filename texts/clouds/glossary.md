# title: Glossary of names and rare words in the Clouds
# lang: eng
# license: CC BY-SA 4.0

## Στρεψιάδης
**Στρεψιάδης, -ου, ὁ** Strepsiades ("Twister", from στρέφω), the old countryman of the deme Cicynna, son of Pheidon, sunk in debt by his son's horses, who goes to Socrates to learn how to cheat his creditors (Clouds 134, 633, 1145a, 1206, 1221).

## Φειδιππίδης
**Φειδιππίδης, -ου, ὁ** Pheidippides, Strepsiades' son, mad on horses and chariot racing; his name is the compromise between his father's wish to name him after his grandfather Pheidon (Pheidonides) and his mother's aristocratic -ippos (Clouds 67, 80, 827, 1143, 1229).

## Πασίας
**Πασίας, -ου, ὁ** Pasias, the creditor to whom Strepsiades owes twelve minae for the horse branded with a koppa (Clouds 21, 22, 30).

## Μεγακλῆς
**Μεγακλῆς, -έους, ὁ** Megacles, the great name of the Alcmaeonid family: Strepsiades' wife is the niece of Megacles son of Megacles (the uncle and his father); the mother dreams of her son driving a chariot to the city in a fine robe like Megacles, and Pheidippides trusts his rich uncle Megacles not to leave him horseless (Clouds 46, 70, 124, 815, "eat Megacles' columns", the costly family house); also printed uncontracted Μεγακλέης.

## Ξάνθιππος
**Ξάνθιππος, -ου, ὁ** Xanthippus ("Bay horse"), a name proposed by Strepsiades' wife for her son, one of the horsy names of the old families; Pericles' father and son bore it (Clouds 64).

## Χάριππος
**Χάριππος, -ου, ὁ** Charippus ("Delighting in horses"), a second horsy name the mother wanted for her son (Clouds 64).

## Καλλιππίδης
**Καλλιππίδης, -ου, ὁ** Callippides ("Son of a fine horse"), the third horsy name the mother proposed (Clouds 64).

## Φειδιππίδιον
**Φειδιππίδιον, -ου, τό** little Pheidippides, a pet diminutive of Pheidippides, a comic coinage: Strepsiades coaxing his son awake (Clouds 80).

## Χαιρεφῶν
**Χαιρεφῶν, -ῶντος, ὁ** Chaerephon of the deme Sphettus, Socrates' devoted follower, thin and pale (nicknamed "the bat"); in the play Socrates' partner in the Thinkery, who measured a flea's jump; Socrates promises Strepsiades he will become just like Chaerephon, and Strepsiades cries that he will be half dead (Clouds 104, 144, 146, 156, 503, 831, 1465).

## Λεωγόρας
**Λεωγόρας, -ου, ὁ** Leogoras, a rich and luxurious Athenian, father of the orator Andocides; Pheidippides would not give up riding even for the pheasants (or, as some explain, Phasian horses) that Leogoras keeps (Clouds 109).

## Κικυννεύς
**Κικυννεύς, -έως, ὁ** a man of Cicynna (Κίκυννα), a small country deme of Attica; Strepsiades looks for his fellow demesmen on the map a pupil of the Thinkery shows him (Clouds 210; compare Κικυννόθεν, "from Cicynna", 134).

## Περικλῆς
**Περικλῆς, -έους, ὁ** Pericles (died 429), the leading statesman of Athens: Euboea was "stretched out" by him when he crushed its revolt in 446; and Strepsiades' "I lost them for necessary purposes, like Pericles" recalls the famous item εἰς τὸ δέον in his accounts, said to be the bribe to the Spartan king in 446 (Clouds 213, 859).

## Βυζάντιον
**Βυζάντιον, -ου, τό** Byzantium on the Bosporus, where iron coins were current: Strepsiades asks whether the Thinkers swear by iron money, as in Byzantium, since gods are no coin with them (Clouds 249).

## Ἀθάμας
**Ἀθάμας, -αντος, ὁ** Athamas, king of Orchomenus, brought wreathed to the altar to be sacrificed (in Sophocles' Athamas); Strepsiades, given a wreath, fears he is to be sacrificed like him (Clouds 257).

## Μαιῶτις
**Μαιῶτις, -ιδος, ἡ** (with λίμνη) Lake Maeotis, the Sea of Azov, one of the far places where Socrates calls on the Clouds to be (Clouds 273).

## ἀνταποπέρδω
**ἀνταποπέρδω** (aor. ἀνταπέπαρδον) to break wind back in answer, a comic coinage: Strepsiades wants to answer the Clouds' thunder (Clouds 293).

## Ξενόφαντος
**Ξενόφαντος, -ου, ὁ** Xenophantus, father of the long-haired poet Hieronymus; on seeing his son, a hairy wild fellow, the Clouds take the shape of Centaurs (Clouds 349).

## Κλεώνυμος
**Κλεώνυμος, -ου, ὁ** Cleonymus, a fat politician of Cleon's party, always mocked by Aristophanes as a glutton and as the man who threw away his shield (ῥίψασπις); the Clouds turn into deer at the sight of him, and he is a perjurer whom the thunderbolt spares (Clouds 353, 400); the kneading-trough joke makes him effeminate (673, 674, 675).

## Κλεισθένης
**Κλεισθένης, -ους, ὁ** Cleisthenes, a beardless and effeminate Athenian, a constant butt of comedy; seeing him, the Clouds turned into women (Clouds 355).

## παπαπαππάξ
**παπαπαππάξ** (interj.) a sound made up to imitate the full crash of thunder and the rumbling of the bowels, after the softer παππάξ (Clouds 390, 391).

## Θέωρος
**Θέωρος, -ου, ὁ** Theorus, a flatterer and associate of Cleon, named with Cleonymus as a notorious perjurer whom Zeus' thunderbolt does not strike (Clouds 400).

## Κλέων
**Κλέων, -ωνος, ὁ** Cleon, the tanner (the Paphlagonian of 581) and leading demagogue after Pericles: Aristophanes struck him in the belly at the height of his power (in the Knights, 424); when he was elected general the moon left her course and the sun drew in his wick and said he would not shine; the chorus urges the Athenians to convict "Cleon the gull" of bribery and theft and put his neck in the stocks (Clouds 549, 586, 591).

## Ὑπέρβολος
**Ὑπέρβολος, -ου, ὁ** Hyperbolus, the lamp-maker and demagogue who followed Cleon (ostracized about 417): other poets keep trampling on him and his mother; he was chosen hieromnemon (envoy to the Delphic council) and lost his wreath; he paid a talent to learn rhetoric; he made many talents out of the lamp market by villainy (Clouds 551, 557, 558, 623, 876, 1065).

## Μαρικᾶς
**Μαρικᾶς, -ᾶ, ὁ** Maricas, Eupolis' comedy (421), whose title figure stood for Hyperbolus; Aristophanes says it was his own Knights turned inside out (Clouds 553, in the revised parabasis).

## Φρύνιχος
**Φρύνιχος, -ου, ὁ** Phrynichus, the comic poet, a rival of Aristophanes, who long ago had put on stage the drunken old woman eaten by a sea monster, which Eupolis copied in his Maricas (Clouds 556).

## Ἕρμιππος
**Ἕρμιππος, -ου, ὁ** Hermippus, the comic poet, who also wrote a play against Hyperbolus (Clouds 557).

## Κύνθιος
**Κύνθιος, -α, -ον** of Cynthus, the mountain on Delos where Apollo was born: "Delian, who holds the high-horned Cynthian rock" (Clouds 596).

## καρδόπη
**καρδόπη, -ης, ἡ** Socrates' coinage, a feminine form of κάρδοπος, kneading-trough, which is feminine though it ends like a masculine; Strepsiades later uses it against a creditor who says κάρδοπος (Clouds 678, 679, 680, 1251).

## Σωστράτη
**Σωστράτη, -ης, ἡ** Sostrate, a woman's name, given as the model for the feminine καρδόπη (Clouds 678).

## Κλεωνύμη
**Κλεωνύμη, -ης, ἡ** "Cleonyme", Socrates' comic feminine of Cleonymus, mocking his unmanliness (Clouds 680).

## Λύσιλλα
**Λύσιλλα, -ης, ἡ** Lysilla, a woman's name, the first of Strepsiades' examples of feminine names (Clouds 684).

## Κλειταγόρα
**Κλειταγόρα, -ας, ἡ** Clitagora, a woman's name, among Strepsiades' examples of feminine names; also the name of a drinking song (Clouds 684).

## Δημητρία
**Δημητρία, -ας, ἡ** Demetria, a woman's name, among Strepsiades' examples of feminine names (Clouds 684).

## Μελησίας
**Μελησίας, -ου, ὁ** Melesias, a man's name, among Strepsiades' examples of masculine names; a name of the family of Thucydides son of Melesias (Clouds 686).

## Κοισύρα
**Κοισύρα, -ας, ἡ** Coesyra, a proud lady of the Alcmaeonid house, a byword for luxury and airs; Pheidippides comes "from the high-flying women of Coesyra's line" through his mother (Clouds 800; compare ἐγκεκοισυρωμένην, "all Coesyra-fied", 48).

## Τήλεφος
**Τήλεφος, -ου, ὁ** Telephus, the Mysian king whom Euripides brought on stage in beggar's rags; the Just Argument says the Unjust once begged, claiming to be Telephus the Mysian and chewing maxims out of his wallet (Clouds 922).

## Φρῦνις
**Φρῦνις, -ιδος, ὁ** Phrynis of Mytilene, the lyre singer (victor at the Panathenaea about 446), who brought the twisting new modulations into music; the Just Argument condemns his tortuous turns (Clouds 971).

## Κηδείδης
**Κηδείδης, -ου, ὁ** Cedeides (Starkie's spelling, after Nauck; the manuscripts have Κηκείδης, Cecides), an old dithyrambic poet, named with the Dipolieia and the Buphonia as a byword for old-fashioned things (Clouds 985).

## Ἰαπετός
**Ἰαπετός, -οῦ, ὁ** Iapetus, a Titan, father of Prometheus, a byword for an old fogey: the young must not call their father Iapetus (Clouds 998).

## Ἱπποκράτης
**Ἱπποκράτης, -ους, ὁ** Hippocrates, Athenian general (killed at Delium in 424), nephew of Pericles; his sons were mocked as boorish dunces, and the Unjust Argument warns that the youth who obeys the Just one will be like them and be called a mother's simpleton (Clouds 1001).

## Τληπόλεμος
**Τληπόλεμος, -ου, ὁ** Tlepolemus, son of Heracles; the creditor's tragic lament parodies Xenocles' Licymnius, in which Tlepolemus killed Licymnius, so Strepsiades asks what Tlepolemus has done to him (Clouds 1266).

## Αἰσχύλος
**Αἰσχύλος, -ου, ὁ** Aeschylus (died 456), the tragic poet: Strepsiades asked his son to recite some Aeschylus at dinner, and Pheidippides called him first among poets only for noise and bombast (Clouds 1365, 1366).
