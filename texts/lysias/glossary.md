# title: Glossary of names in Lysias
# lang: eng

## Ἐρατοσθένης
**Ἐρατοσθένης, -ους, ὁ** Eratosthenes: (1) of Oe, the adulterer killed by Euphiletus (speech 1); (2) one of the Thirty, the defendant of speech 12. The second arrested Lysias' brother Polemarchus, who was put to death (12.16–34); he stayed in the city with Pheidon when the rest of the Thirty were driven out (12.54).

## Εὐφίλητος
**Εὐφίλητος, -ου, ὁ** Euphiletus, the speaker of speech 1, who killed Eratosthenes (1.16).

## Ὀῆθεν
**Ὀῆθεν, adv.** of the deme Oe (1.16).

## Σώστρατος
**Σώστρατος, -ου, ὁ** Sostratus: (1) the friend of Euphiletus (1.22, 1.39); (2) a friend of Polyaenus (9.13). The first supped with Euphiletus on the evening Eratosthenes was killed.

## Θερμώδων
**Θερμώδων, -οντος, ὁ** the Thermodon, the river by which the Amazons lived (2.4).

## Πολυνείκης
**Πολυνείκης, -ους, ὁ** Polynices, who marched with Adrastus against Thebes (2.7).

## Εὐρυσθεύς
**Εὐρυσθεύς, -έως, ὁ** Eurystheus, who persecuted the children of Heracles; the Athenians would not give them up and defeated him (2.11–16).

## Ξέρξης
**Ξέρξης, -ου, ὁ** Xerxes, king of Persia ("the king of Asia", 2.27).

## Λακεδαιμόνιος
**Λακεδαιμόνιος, -α, -ον** Lacedaemonian, Spartan; οἱ Λακεδαιμόνιοι, the Lacedaemonians.

## Θεμιστοκλῆς
**Θεμιστοκλῆς, -έους, ὁ** Themistocles, the Athenian general and statesman: general at Salamis (2.42), builder of the walls (12.63), named with Solon and Pericles as a lawgiver (30.28).

## Πλαταιαί
**Πλαταιαί, -ῶν, αἱ** Plataea, the town in Boeotia; the battle of 479 BC (2.46).

## Πλαταιεύς
**Πλαταιεύς, -έως, ὁ** a Plataean, a man of Plataea (2.46). The Plataeans settled at Athens had Athenian citizenship: Pancleon claims to be one (speech 23).

## Γερανεία
**Γερανεία, -ας, ἡ** Geraneia, the mountain range of the Megarid, seized by the Corinthians and their allies (2.49).

## Μυρωνίδης
**Μυρωνίδης, -ου, ὁ** Myronides, the Athenian general who led the oldest and the youngest men to victory in the Megarid (2.52).

## Πλαταϊκός
**Πλαταϊκός, -ή, -όν** Plataean (3.5: Theodotus, a Plataean boy).

## Ἀριστόκριτος
**Ἀριστόκριτος, -ου, ὁ** Aristocritus, hit by the stone Simon threw at the speaker (3.8).

## Αὐτοκλῆς
**Αὐτοκλῆς, -έους, ὁ** Autocles, a companion of Simon (3.12).

## Μόλων
**Μόλων, -ωνος, ὁ** Molon, the fuller (3.16).

## Λάμπων
**Λάμπων, -ωνος, ὁ** Lampon, by whose house the speaker came on the fight (3.17).

## Κορώνεια
**Κορώνεια, -ας, ἡ** Coronea, the town in Boeotia; the campaign of 394 BC (3.45).

## Λάχης
**Λάχης, -ητος, ὁ** Laches, the taxiarch whom Simon struck (3.45).

## Φιλῖνος
**Φιλῖνος, -ου, ὁ** Philinus, a witness named in 4.4.

## Διοκλῆς
**Διοκλῆς, -έους, ὁ** Diocles: (1) a witness named in 4.4; (2) the son of Zacorus the hierophant (6.54); (3) the archon of 409/8 BC (21.2).

## Ἀνδοκίδης
**Ἀνδοκίδης, -ου, ὁ** Andocides, the defendant of speech 6: the orator, who had informed against his friends and kinsmen in the affair of the Mysteries and the Hermae (6.2) and is accused of impiety for taking part in the rites after his return.

## Ἰωνία
**Ἰωνία, -ας, ἡ** Ionia (6.6).

## Περικλῆς
**Περικλῆς, -έους, ὁ** Pericles, the Athenian statesman (6.10; 30.28); he persuaded Cephalus, the father of Lysias, to settle at Athens (12.4).

## Εὐμολπίδαι
**Εὐμολπίδαι, -ῶν, οἱ** the Eumolpidae, the priestly clan of Eleusis, who expound the unwritten laws (6.10).

## Ἄρχιππος
**Ἄρχιππος, -ου, ὁ** Archippus, whom Andocides accused of impiety against his family's Hermes (6.11–12).

## Διαγόρας
**Διαγόρας, -ου, ὁ** Diagoras of Melos, the type of the impious man (6.17).

## Κιτιεύς
**Κιτιεύς, -έως, ὁ** a man of Citium in Cyprus (6.26: the king of the Citians).

## Εὐαγόρας
**Εὐαγόρας, -ου, ὁ** Evagoras, king of Salamis in Cyprus (6.28; 19.20, 19.23, 19.27b), the ally of Conon and of Athens.

## Κηφίσιος
**Κηφίσιος, -ου, ὁ** Cephisius, an accuser of Andocides (6.42).

## Βάτραχος
**Βάτραχος, -ου, ὁ** Batrachus, an informer under the Thirty (6.45; 12.48).

## Ζάκορος
**Ζάκορος, -ου, ὁ** Zacorus, the hierophant, father of Diocles (6.54).

## Πείσανδρος
**Πείσανδρος, -ου, ὁ** Pisander, a leader of the Four Hundred (12.66; 25.9); the plot of speech 7 had been his before his property was confiscated (7.4).

## Ἀντικλῆς
**Ἀντικλῆς, -έους, ὁ** Anticles: (1) who bought the plot of speech 7 and let it (7.4); (2) a master of Eumares (13.64).

## Καλλίστρατος
**Καλλίστρατος, -ου, ὁ** Callistratus, who rented the plot of speech 7 in the archonship of Pythodorus and farmed it two years (7.9).

## Πυθόδωρος
**Πυθόδωρος, -ου, ὁ** Pythodorus, the archon of 404/3 BC (7.9).

## Ἀλκίας
**Ἀλκίας, -ου, ὁ** Alcias, a freedman of Antisthenes, who rented the plot of speech 7 (7.10).

## Ἀντισθένης
**Ἀντισθένης, -ους, ὁ** Antisthenes, the former master of Alcias (7.10).

## Πρωτέας
**Πρωτέας, -ου, ὁ** Proteas, who rented the plot of speech 7 for three years (7.10).

## Σουνιάδης
**Σουνιάδης, -ου, ὁ** Suniades, the archon of 397/6 BC (7.11).

## Νικόμαχος
**Νικόμαχος, -ου, ὁ** Nicomachus: (1) the accuser in speech 7 (7.20, 7.36, 7.39); (2) the defendant of speech 30. The second was a commissioner for writing up the laws, charged with keeping the office for years and altering the laws for pay.

## Ἡγέμαχος
**Ἡγέμαχος, -ου, ὁ** Hegemachus, named in the affair of the pledged horse in speech 8 (8.10).

## Διόδωρος
**Διόδωρος, -ου, ὁ** Diodorus, a member of the society of speech 8 (8.10, 8.14).

## Πολυκλῆς
**Πολυκλῆς, -έους, ὁ** Polycles, the speaker's opponent in the dispute of speech 8 about the horse and the twelve minae (8.10, 8.12, 8.16).

## Κλειτόδικος
**Κλειτόδικος, -ου, ὁ** Cleitodicus, a member of the society of speech 8 (8.13).

## Αὐτοκράτης
**Αὐτοκράτης, -ους, ὁ** Autocrates, a member of the society of speech 8 (8.15).

## Εὐρυπτόλεμος
**Εὐρυπτόλεμος, -ου, ὁ** Euryptolemus, a member of the society of speech 8 (8.15).

## Μηνόφιλος
**Μηνόφιλος, -ου, ὁ** Menophilus, a member of the society of speech 8 (8.15).

## Καλλικράτης
**Καλλικράτης, -ους, ὁ** Callicrates, a man named beside Polyaenus in 9.5.

## Πολύαινος
**Πολύαινος, -ου, ὁ** Polyaenus, the speaker of speech 9 (9.5).

## Φίλιος
**Φίλιος, -ου, ὁ** Philius, a banker (9.5).

## Κτησικλῆς
**Κτησικλῆς, -έους, ὁ** Ctesicles, the magistrate (ἄρχων) who with his colleagues fined Polyaenus (9.6).

## Λυσίθεος
**Λυσίθεος, -ου, ὁ** Lysitheus, who impeached Theomnestus for speaking in the Assembly after throwing away his arms (10.1, 10.12).

## Θεόμνηστος
**Θεόμνηστος, -ου, ὁ** Theomnestus, the defendant of speeches 10 and 11, sued for slander for saying that the speaker had killed his own father.

## Πανταλέων
**Πανταλέων, -οντος, ὁ** Pantaleon, the elder brother of the speaker of speech 10, who took over the whole estate as guardian (10.5).

## πεφασμένως
**πεφασμένως, adv.** openly: a word of an old law of Solon, which the speaker explains by φανερῶς (10.19); from the perfect passive participle of φαίνω.

## Θέογνις
**Θέογνις, -ιδος, ὁ** Theognis, one of the Thirty, who with Piso proposed that the metics be seized (12.6); he guarded the prisoners at Damnippus' house (12.13–15).

## Πείσων
**Πείσων, -ωνος, ὁ** Piso, one of the Thirty, who took Lysias' money on the promise of saving him and then handed him over (12.6–12).

## Κυζικηνός
**Κυζικηνός, -ή, -όν** of Cyzicus; ὁ Κυζικηνός (στατήρ), the Cyzicene stater, a coin of electrum (12.11; 32.6).

## Μηλόβιος
**Μηλόβιος, -ου, ὁ** Melobius, one of the Thirty (12.12); he took the gold earrings from the ears of Polemarchus' wife (12.19).

## Μνησιθείδης
**Μνησιθείδης, -ου, ὁ** Mnesitheides, one of the Thirty (12.12).

## Δάμνιππος
**Δάμνιππος, -ου, ὁ** Damnippus, a friend of Lysias, at whose house he was held by the Thirty and from which he escaped (12.12–15).

## Ἀρχένεως
**Ἀρχένεως, -εω, ὁ** Archeneos, the shipowner to whose house Lysias fled, and whom he sent to town for news of his brother (12.16).

## Πολέμαρχος
**Πολέμαρχος, -ου, ὁ** Polemarchus, the brother of Lysias, arrested by Eratosthenes and put to death by the Thirty (12.16–34).

## Ἰατροκλῆς
**Ἰατροκλῆς, -έους, ὁ** Iatrocles, with whom Eratosthenes left his ship and fled from the Hellespont (12.42).

## Κριτίας
**Κριτίας, -ου, ὁ** Critias, the leader of the Thirty (12.43, 12.55; 13.55).

## Αἰσχυλίδης
**Αἰσχυλίδης, -ου, ὁ** Aeschylides, an informer under the Thirty (12.48).

## Θηραμένης
**Θηραμένης, -ους, ὁ** Theramenes, one of the Thirty, attacked in 12.62–79 as the man who made the surrender to Sparta and set up the oligarchy (also 13.9–17); Eratosthenes claimed to be of his party.

## Φυλή
**Φυλή, -ῆς, ἡ** Phyle, the fort on Mount Parnes seized by Thrasybulus and the democrats in the winter of 404/3 BC (12.52; 13.63, 13.77–82).

## Φείδων
**Φείδων, -ωνος, ὁ** Pheidon, one of the Thirty, afterwards one of the board that replaced them (12.54–55, 12.58).

## Ἱπποκλῆς
**Ἱπποκλῆς, -έους, ὁ** Hippocles, one of the board that replaced the Thirty (12.55).

## Ἐπιχάρης
**Ἐπιχάρης, -ους, ὁ** Epichares of Lamptrae, one of the board that replaced the Thirty (12.55).

## Λαμπτρεύς
**Λαμπτρεύς, -έως, ὁ** of the deme Lamptrae (12.55).

## Χαρικλῆς
**Χαρικλῆς, -έους, ὁ** Charicles, one of the Thirty, with Critias the head of the extreme party (12.55).

## Λύσανδρος
**Λύσανδρος, -ου, ὁ** Lysander, the Spartan admiral who took Athens in 404 BC and supported the oligarchs (12.59, 12.71–74; 13.34; 14.38).

## Κάλλαισχρος
**Κάλλαισχρος, -ου, ὁ** Callaeschrus, a leader of the Four Hundred (12.66).

## Ἀντιφῶν
**Ἀντιφῶν, -ῶντος, ὁ** Antiphon, the orator, one of the Four Hundred, accused by Theramenes and put to death (12.67).

## Ἀρχεπτόλεμος
**Ἀρχεπτόλεμος, -ου, ὁ** Archeptolemus, one of the Four Hundred, accused by Theramenes and put to death with Antiphon (12.67).

## Φιλοχάρης
**Φιλοχάρης, -ους, ὁ** Philochares, present with Lysander at the assembly that set up the Thirty (12.72).

## Μιλτιάδης
**Μιλτιάδης, -ου, ὁ** Miltiades, present with Lysander at the assembly that set up the Thirty (12.72).

## Δρακοντίδης
**Δρακοντίδης, -ου, ὁ** Dracontides, who moved the decree that set up the Thirty (12.73).

## Διονυσόδωρος
**Διονυσόδωρος, -ου, ὁ** Dionysodorus, brother-in-law and cousin of the speaker of speech 13, a taxiarch put to death on the information of Agoratus (13.1–2, 13.13, 13.40–41).

## Ἀγόρατος
**Ἀγόρατος, -ου, ὁ** Agoratus, the defendant of speech 13: the informer whose denunciations under the Thirty (404 BC) brought Dionysodorus, Strombichides and many others to their death. The speaker calls him a slave and a slave's son (13.64) and denies that he killed Phrynichus or was made an Athenian for it (13.70–76).

## Κλεοφῶν
**Κλεοφῶν, -ῶντος, ὁ** Cleophon, the popular leader who spoke against the peace with Sparta in 404 BC and was condemned to death by a court arranged for the purpose (13.7–12; 30.10–13); he left no money (19.48).

## Στρομβιχίδης
**Στρομβιχίδης, -ου, ὁ** Strombichides, an Athenian general put to death under the Thirty on the information of Agoratus (13.13; 30.14).

## Θεόκριτος
**Θεόκριτος, -ου, ὁ** Theocritus, "the son of Elaphostictus", a companion of Agoratus, sent before the Council to inform (13.19–22).

## Ἐλαφόστικτος
**Ἐλαφόστικτος, -ου, ὁ** Elaphostictus ("tattooed with a deer"), the name given to the father of Theocritus (13.19).

## Νικίας
**Νικίας, -ου, ὁ** Nicias: (1) the general, uncle of the speaker of speech 18 (18.1–6; 19.47); (2) a surety for Agoratus (13.23).

## Νικομένης
**Νικομένης, -ους, ὁ** Nicomenes, a surety for Agoratus (13.23).

## Μουνιχίασι
**Μουνιχίασι, adv.** at Munichia, the hill and harbour of Piraeus, with its altar (13.24) and its theatre (13.32).

## Μουνιχίαζε
**Μουνιχίαζε, adv.** to Munichia (13.29).

## Κουριεύς
**Κουριεύς, -έως, ὁ** of Curium (13.54: Xenophon; some manuscripts read Καριεύς).

## Μενέστρατος
**Μενέστρατος, -ου, ὁ** Menestratus of Amphitrope, denounced by Agoratus, who then informed against others himself; he was later condemned and executed (13.55–57).

## Ἁγνόδωρος
**Ἁγνόδωρος, -ου, ὁ** Hagnodorus of Amphitrope, a kinsman by marriage of Critias, who brought Menestratus before the Assembly and got him immunity for informing (13.55).

## Ἀμφιτροπαιεύς
**Ἀμφιτροπαιεύς, -έως, ὁ** of the deme Amphitrope (13.55).

## Ἀριστοφάνης
**Ἀριστοφάνης, -ους, ὁ** Aristophanes: (1) of Cholleidae, a surety for Agoratus (13.58–60); (2) the son of Nicophemus, whose property is the matter of speech 19. The first would not turn informer and was put to death. The second was executed without trial with his father (19.7); the speaker's father, his father-in-law, is suspected of holding part of his confiscated property.

## Χολλῄδης
**Χολλῄδης, -ου, ὁ** of the deme Cholleidae (13.58).

## Εὐμάρης
**Εὐμάρης, -ους, ὁ** Eumares, the father of Agoratus, a slave of Nicocles and Anticles (13.64).

## Νικοκλῆς
**Νικοκλῆς, -έους, ὁ** Nicocles, a master of Eumares (13.64).

## Φαινιππίδης
**Φαινιππίδης, -ου, ὁ** Phaenippides, who arrested a brother of Agoratus as a clothes-stealer (13.68).

## Φρύνιχος
**Φρύνιχος, -ου, ὁ** Phrynichus, a leader of the Four Hundred (411 BC), who was assassinated (13.70–76, where Agoratus' claim to have killed him is refuted; 20.11; 25.9).

## Καλυδώνιος
**Καλυδώνιος, -α, -ον** of Calydon (13.71: Thrasybulus of Calydon, who plotted against Phrynichus).

## Ἀναγυράσιος
**Ἀναγυράσιος, -ου, ὁ** of the deme Anagyrus (13.73).

## Ἄνυτος
**Ἄνυτος, -ου, ὁ** Anytus, a leader of the democrats: as general at Phyle he would not have Agoratus killed there (13.78, 13.82); in speech 22 he is one of the magistrates who advised the corn-dealers (22.8–9).

## Αἴσιμος
**Αἴσιμος, -ου, ὁ** Aesimus, who led the procession of the men from Piraeus; he threw down Agoratus' shield and drove him out of it as a murderer (13.80–82).

## Ἀλκιβιάδης
**Ἀλκιβιάδης, -ου, ὁ** Alcibiades: (1) the general; (2) his son, the defendant of speeches 14 and 15. The son is charged with serving in the cavalry when he was enrolled as a hoplite; most of speech 14 is an attack on the father (14.16–40; also 19.52; 21.6). A third Alcibiades, the defendant's great-grandfather, was twice ostracised (14.39).

## Ἀρχεστρατίδης
**Ἀρχεστρατίδης, -ου, ὁ** Archestratides, the chief accuser of the younger Alcibiades (14.3; 15.12).

## Ἀρχέδημος
**Ἀρχέδημος, -ου, ὁ** Archedemus "the blear-eyed", at whose house the younger Alcibiades drank as a boy (14.25).

## Θεότιμος
**Θεότιμος, -ου, ὁ** Theotimus, with whom the younger Alcibiades plotted against his father and betrayed Orni (14.26).

## Ὄρνοι
**Ὄρνοι, -ων, οἱ** Orni, a stronghold of Alcibiades, which his son betrayed (14.26; the name is Lipsius' correction of the manuscripts).

## Ἀρχεβιάδης
**Ἀρχεβιάδης, -ου, ὁ** Archebiades, who ransomed the younger Alcibiades (14.27).

## Λευκή
**Λευκή, -ῆς, ἡ** Λευκὴ ἀκτή, "White Shore", a place on the Propontis (14.27).

## Μεγακλῆς
**Μεγακλῆς, -έους, ὁ** Megacles, the maternal grandfather of the elder Alcibiades, twice ostracised (14.39).

## Ἁλίαρτος
**Ἁλίαρτος, -ου, ὁ** Haliartus, the town in Boeotia; the expedition of 395 BC (16.13).

## Ὀρθόβουλος
**Ὀρθόβουλος, -ου, ὁ** Orthobulus, who enrolled Mantitheus in the cavalry (16.13).

## Στειριεύς
**Στειριεύς, -έως, ὁ** of the deme Steiria; "the pompous man of Steiria" (16.15) is Thrasybulus.

## Ἐράτων
**Ἐράτων, -ωνος, ὁ** Eraton: (1) the father of Erasiphon (17.2–4); (2) his son (17.3). The father borrowed two talents from the speaker's grandfather; the sons did not pay, and their property was afterwards confiscated.

## Ἐρασιφῶν
**Ἐρασιφῶν, -ῶντος, ὁ** Erasiphon, a son of Eraton (17.2–6).

## Ἐρασίστρατος
**Ἐρασίστρατος, -ου, ὁ** Erasistratus, a son of Eraton, the only one of the brothers in town, against whom the speaker's father won his suit for the whole debt (17.3–6).

## Ξεναίνετος
**Ξεναίνετος, -ου, ὁ** Xenaenetus, the archon of 401/0 BC (17.3).

## Κικυννοῖ
**Κικυννοῖ, adv.** at Cicynna, an Attic deme (17.5, 17.8).

## Εὐκράτης
**Εὐκράτης, -ους, ὁ** Eucrates, the brother of Nicias and father of the speaker of speech 18, chosen general after the last sea-fight, who would not join the oligarchs (18.4).

## Νικήρατος
**Νικήρατος, -ου, ὁ** Niceratus, the son of Nicias, put to death by the Thirty (18.6, 18.10; 19.47).

## Πολίοχος
**Πολίοχος, -ου, ὁ** Poliochus, the speaker's opponent in speech 18 (18.13).

## Διόμνηστος
**Διόμνηστος, -ου, ὁ** Diomnestus, a kinsman of the speaker of speech 18, who serves as trierarch with him and his brother (18.21).

## Νικόφημος
**Νικόφημος, -ου, ὁ** Nicophemus, father of Aristophanes, the friend of Conon, who lived in Cyprus; put to death without trial with his son (19.7).

## Κόνων
**Κόνων, -ωνος, ὁ** Conon, the Athenian general, who won the sea-fight of 394 BC (19.28); the friend and patron of Nicophemus and Aristophanes (19.12–44) and father of Timotheus (19.34).

## Παιανιεύς
**Παιανιεύς, -έως, ὁ** of the deme Paeania (19.15).

## Φαῖδρος
**Φαῖδρος, -ου, ὁ** Phaedrus of Myrrhinus, his nephew, to whom the speaker's father gave a daughter in marriage though he had become poor (19.15): the Phaedrus of Plato's dialogues. The house of a Phaedrus is named in 32.14.

## Μυρρινούσιος
**Μυρρινούσιος, -ου, ὁ** of the deme Myrrhinus (19.15).

## Κριτόδημος
**Κριτόδημος, -ου, ὁ** Critodemus of Alopece, the father of the speaker's wife, killed by the Lacedaemonians after the sea-fight in the Hellespont (19.16).

## Ἀλωπεκῆθεν
**Ἀλωπεκῆθεν, adv.** of the deme Alopece (19.16).

## Εὔνομος
**Εὔνομος, -ου, ὁ** Eunomus, a friend and guest-friend of Dionysius, who sailed to Sicily with Aristophanes (19.19, 19.23).

## Δῆμος
**Δῆμος, -ου, ὁ** Demus, the son of Pyrilampes, a trierarch bound for Cyprus, who offered a gold cup, a token from the Great King, as a pledge for a loan (19.25–26).

## Πυριλάμπης
**Πυριλάμπης, -ους, ὁ** Pyrilampes, the father of Demus (19.25).

## Εὐβουλίδης
**Εὐβουλίδης, -ου, ὁ** Eubulides, the archon of 394/3 BC (19.28).

## Ἰσχόμαχος
**Ἰσχόμαχος, -ου, ὁ** Ischomachus, a man thought very rich, whose estate proved far smaller at his death (19.46).

## Στέφανος
**Στέφανος, -ου, ὁ** Stephanus, the son of Thallus, said to own more than fifty talents; at his death the estate proved to be about eleven (19.46).

## Θαλλός
